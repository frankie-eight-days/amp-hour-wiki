An **oscilloscope** is an electronic test instrument that displays a varying signal voltage as a graph against time, providing the only direct view of what a circuit is actually doing.[289][613] Over two decades the instrument's role has expanded from waveform visualisation and timing measurement into an analysis platform expected to perform compliance measurement against standards, jitter decomposition, and frequency- and modulation-domain analysis in a single box.[117][104] Real-time bandwidths in production instruments now span from tens of megahertz at the entry level to a true 110 GHz at 256 gigasamples per second at the top of the market.[72][404] Because the instrument itself shapes what it shows, effective use requires understanding how the measurement distorts the circuit under test.[289][533]

## Principles of operation

### Sweep, time base and display

The cathode ray tube of an analogue oscilloscope deflects its beam electrostatically, using plates rather than the deflection coils of television and electron-optical instruments, because electrostatic deflection is much faster and more precise.[669] Analogue oscilloscopes divide into recurrent-sweep instruments, which free-run, and triggered-sweep instruments, which start the sweep on a qualifying signal event; the recurrent-sweep class is far less useful, and consumer and kit oscilloscopes of the valve era commonly shipped with free-running sweeps that made signals visible for the first time but could not present a stable, repeatable waveform.[117][424]

An oscilloscope has a single trigger point shared by all channels, so on a two- or four-channel instrument only signals synchronous with the trigger source stand still; two inputs differing by one hertz produce one stable trace and one that travels across the screen at a one-hertz rate, rendering the second useless for catching events.[145] The analogue answer to zooming into a region far downstream of the trigger was delayed sweep, in which a second time base runs at a faster sweep speed and begins only after a dialled-in delay from the trigger.[690] A digital oscilloscope instead relies on one trigger event and capture memory deep enough to hold the region of interest, which is then expanded in software, and only a small number of digital instruments retain two independent time bases with independent triggering to reproduce the delayed-sweep behaviour.[690]

### Triggering and acquisition memory

Triggering exists because acquisition memory is finite: continuous capture at the rate needed to see a microsecond-scale event over days produces unusable volumes of data, so the instrument discards everything not associated with the qualifying event, and oscilloscopes and logic analyzers default to 50 percent pre- and post-trigger capture so the operator sees what happened before the event as well as after.[510] Acquisition is inherently discontinuous—the instrument triggers, captures for a window, and is blind until it re-arms—which is why instruments intended for long-duration energy measurement stream without gaps instead.[607] Memory depth and sample rate together fix the capture window: 100 megasamples at one terasample per second is 100 microseconds of record, enough to hold only a handful of packets when catching specific frames on a fast serial link.[600] Available acquisition memory has moved from 2K samples being respectable and 8K commanding an extreme price to a megapoint in a 300-dollar instrument.[119]

### Vertical resolution

General-purpose digital oscilloscopes almost universally use 8-bit converters; high-resolution modes recover effective resolution by oversampling and averaging intermediate samples, yielding an effective 10 to 12 bits from an 8-bit converter at reduced bandwidth.[106] Higher native converter resolutions buy dynamic range in the spectrum: under otherwise identical conditions a signal below fifteen microvolts was recovered from the noise floor on a 14-bit instrument where it was invisible on a 12-bit instrument.[677] A leading-edge 14-bit converter at 1 GHz of bandwidth is unlikely to be a single monolithic die, and such acquisition front ends are delivered as all-in-one hybrid modules combining several semiconductor processes in one package.[677] Around 500 microvolts per division is close to the practical floor for vertical sensitivity on a general-purpose bench oscilloscope, because the inherent noise of the front end at high bandwidth into a 1 megohm input impedance sets the limit.[87]

### Bandwidth and rise time

The bandwidth needed to reproduce a given edge is approximately 0.35 divided by the 10–90 percent rise time, because the harmonics of a fast edge fall off but remain significant up to that bandwidth.[252] The rule assumes a brick-wall response with zero amplitude above the bandwidth; a real front end rolls off gradually, so the rule is an approximation whose error depends on the instrument's actual response shape.[252] An oscilloscope's bandwidth can be verified on the bench by applying a known fast edge and observing the reproduced rise time, a technique documented in Jim Williams's application notes.[465]

## Probes and probing

Probes, not the mainframe, set the practical bandwidth of a high-bandwidth oscilloscope: a 1 GHz instrument cannot reach its specification through the supplied 10:1 passive probe, and the active probe that can reach it costs on the order of the oscilloscope itself, so a good FET probe can exceed the price of the instrument it plugs into.[76] A 10:1 passive probe rated to 1 GHz has been made by essentially one manufacturer, which makes buying a general-purpose oscilloscope far beyond two to three hundred megahertz questionable when the supplied probes will not reach that bandwidth.[567] Probes must be compensated against a clean fast edge or every measurement made through them is degraded; Tektronix's John Addis built a very fast, very clean square wave source into the front panel of oscilloscopes such as the 485 so that compensation did not require a separate pulse generator.[459]

Oscilloscope probes are microphonic: the piezoelectric effect is present in essentially every probe, so mechanical stimulation of the probe body produces a signal at the input, with an orientation dependence.[38] Coaxial cable also generates charge when flexed, so moving a probe puts a visible blip on the screen; the same triboelectric noise corrupts measurements from charge-output sensors such as accelerometers, where the cable's own charge adds to the sensor's.[215]

Connecting an oscilloscope loads the circuit, and a circuit that works only while probed is usually one whose oscillation the probe's impedance is damping; radio-frequency test equipment avoids this class of problem by defining a common 50 ohm system impedance so the instrument does not perturb the behaviour it measures.[533] A one-kilohm series resistor into a 50-ohm-terminated oscilloscope input makes a 20:1 divider whose input impedance is high and broadband, isolating the cable's capacitance and inductance from the circuit under test.[474]

### Grounding and earth reference

The BNC shells on a mains-powered bench oscilloscope are bonded to mains earth, so clipping the ground lead to any node that is not at earth potential shorts that node to earth through the instrument; this is the standard mechanism by which oscilloscopes and circuits are destroyed during work on mains-referenced equipment, and it is why battery-powered floating oscilloscopes are used for service work.[20] Because bench oscilloscopes are chassis grounded, their inputs cannot probe high-voltage or floating nodes, and even 5-volt or 3.3-volt rails can be damaged if the ground lead lands on them, since the earth path can carry considerable current.[527] Working on deliberately floating electronics is straightforward until it must be measured, because almost every bench oscilloscope and bench multimeter is earth-referenced, and handheld instruments are the usual way out.[274] The safe default when a measurement must be taken off an earth reference is to isolate the power supply rather than to lift the earth on the oscilloscope or the laptop.[307] Measuring current with an inline shunt resistor runs into the same reference problem—neither end of the shunt is at the oscilloscope's earth reference—and doing it correctly requires a true differential probe or differential current probe costing more than a dedicated current-measurement instrument.[607]

## Architectures and variants

### Analogue and hybrid instruments

Hybrid analogue and digital storage oscilloscopes were built with a genuine analogue front end and a button that switched the same signal path into a basic megasample digital storage mode, giving both display technologies in one instrument.[229] The micro-channel plate CRT, marketed by Tektronix as bright-eye and fitted to a small number of models including the 2467, placed an electron multiplier at the face of the tube that amplified the beam electrons roughly ten thousand times before they struck the phosphor.[117] Analogue storage oscilloscopes existed but were priced for well-funded laboratories, so routine single-shot capture arrived with digital storage; before digital storage, recording a trace meant photographing the screen with a Polaroid camera.[690][600]

### Sampling oscilloscopes

In an equivalent-time sampling oscilloscope the bandwidth is set entirely by the front-end sample-and-hold, which captures the signal in a window of order ten picoseconds and holds it; the conversion afterwards can be arbitrarily slow, so a ten-cent microcontroller ADC is sufficient behind a 10 GHz front end.[178] A cheap sampling front end typically has no attenuator and only a 50 ohm SMA input, so probing a high-impedance source requires an active probe costing many times the instrument, and without one the fast edge the instrument exists to capture is rounded off.[178] There is no known-perfect oscilloscope at the top of the bandwidth range: a published study applying one input to nine of the fastest sampling oscilloscopes in the world produced nine visibly different results, so at those speeds the response of the instrument itself must be understood and removed from the measurement.[465]

### PC-hosted and USB instruments

PC and USB oscilloscopes exist as a separate class because omitting knobs, screen and enclosure removes substantial cost, and because a host-connected instrument can stream continuously to disk for data logging and offer channel counts a bench instrument does not, at the price of much lower sample rates.[87] A traditional bench oscilloscope contains a complete embedded computer because it must both capture and display the data; headless USB-connected instruments delete that computer and push display and processing onto the host.[527] The two architectures available to a USB instrument are continuous streaming at whatever rate the bus sustains, or a much higher sample rate with local triggering and capture memory that is read out slowly over the bus, and the choice determines whether the device is a data logger or an oscilloscope.[198] Early tablet-tethered mixed-signal instruments paired a single analogue channel of roughly 12 megasamples per second with four digital inputs, with the phone or tablet supplying the display and user interface.[41] The OpenScope, built on a Microchip PIC32MZ and targeted at under a hundred dollars, combined two roughly six-megasample channels at up to 3 MHz analogue bandwidth and ±25 volts with a five-megasample waveform generator and a 50-megasample logic analyzer served to a browser over Wi-Fi.[302] Thunderscope, which grew out of a 2020 hardware contest project, is a four-channel oscilloscope front end presented to a host over Thunderbolt as a PCI Express device rather than over USB.[627] The useful distinction between bench and PC instruments is form factor rather than capability: a bench instrument is a self-contained box with its own screen, whether the controls are knobs or a touch interface, while a PC instrument has no display or controls of its own.[516] Remote or tablet control adds little to an oscilloscope in bench use because the operator is holding probes on the circuit and is therefore always within arm's reach of the front panel.[122]

A significant class of instruments marketed as multimeters or oscilloscopes are data loggers built around a 16-bit converter, specified by accuracy rather than by digits or bandwidth, and are properly assessed on the logging specification rather than the label.[199] A logic analyzer's timing-analysis mode is an oscilloscope with one-bit vertical resolution, sampling asynchronously at a fixed rate such as 50 or 100 megasamples per second and rendering the result as square transitions; the analogue input on a logic analyzer such as the Saleae Logic 4 reaches only around six megahertz on a single channel and so supplements rather than replaces an oscilloscope.[436][355]

### Mixed-domain instruments and absorbed functions

A mixed-domain oscilloscope is defined by synchronous capture across the time and frequency domains; a cost-reduced model in one such family kept the name but removed that capability, so its spectrum and waveform captures cannot be tied together in time.[186] Modern oscilloscopes have absorbed several formerly separate instruments: the built-in frequency counter and a competent FFT implementation cover measurements, including clock jitter analysis, that once required dedicated boxes.[613] The neighbouring time-and-frequency instruments differ in what they plot: an oscilloscope shows voltage against time, a spectrum analyzer amplitude against frequency, and a modulation domain analyzer frequency against time, the last being able to show the settling behaviour of a frequency source as it loses and regains lock.[613] A dynamic signal analyzer is an FFT instrument working in the frequency domain rather than the time domain, and differs from a spectrum analyzer in targeting very low, typically audio, frequencies.[570] A vector network analyzer characterises gain and complex impedance by returning magnitude and phase directly; the same sine waves can be seen on an oscilloscope, but extracting magnitude and phase from them is difficult, which is the practical reason for the separate instrument.[533]

## Measurement practice

A procedure for an unfamiliar signal on an unfamiliar oscilloscope is to set the fastest time base, apply the signal, and slow the time base until something appears, which guarantees the display is never undersampled; modern instruments with a built-in frequency counter warn of undersampling directly.[169] Instrument documentation specifies capability and is silent on limits, so the measurement error mode that matters is the one outside the tool's stated envelope, an asymmetry that applies to circuit simulators as it does to oscilloscopes.[117] Linear Technology application note 47 contains a full tutorial on oscilloscopes, probes, connections and grounding, and is the standard reference to hand a student who cannot get a clean trace, since most such failures are instrumentation and grounding problems rather than circuit problems.[119]

A circuit oscillating above the oscilloscope's bandwidth produces no trace but wrong voltage readings and nonsensical waveforms; damping the suspect nodes by touch, then soldering in a few picofarads of capacitance between them, both confirms and cures it.[117] Before filtering the noise from a switching regulator module, its switching frequency is scoped under several loads to establish whether it is fixed or variable, because only a fixed frequency can be targeted with a tuned LC filter and the module must tolerate the added output LC.[360] A control loop can be compensated empirically by injecting a square wave at the input, watching the output on an oscilloscope, and adjusting until the response is rounded with no overshoot, a valid alternative to analysing the loop mathematically.[377]

The debugging order for a non-working I²C bus is physical layer first: scope the lines to confirm the pull-ups and clean edges, then check chip addressing and enables, and only then question the protocol implementation or the provenance of the library.[274] The edge asymmetry that distinguishes bus types is visible directly on an oscilloscope: SPI and most digital outputs are totem-pole, driving both directions, while I²C is open-collector and relies on a pull-up resistor for the rising edge, giving a near-instant fall and an RC-shaped rise.[274] The quantisation steps at a DAC output are directly visible by probing an arbitrary waveform generator's output, zooming in and raising vertical gain, which is why a reconstruction filter is fitted.[278] Clean edges and correct clock and data assignment on an oscilloscope do not confirm correct protocol behaviour: the oscilloscope answers the analogue question and a logic analyzer with protocol decode answers the framing and content question, and using the first when the fault is in the second wastes the debugging effort.[396] When an oscilloscope will not decode a serial stream at the configured rate, the assumption to check is the rate itself—measure the bit period on screen and calculate the actual clock rate rather than trusting the configured value.[551] Probing a vehicle diagnostic port shows only the raw digital waveform; CAN bus has been mandated for vehicle communications since about 2009 and its contents cannot be interpreted from the analogue trace without protocol decode, with earlier and coexisting physical layers including K-Line, L-Line, LIN and J1850.[388] An unencrypted bus can be debugged by hanging an oscilloscope on it and logging the exchange between failing devices, but electric-vehicle CCS charging encrypts its link, so an interoperability failure between one car and one charger cannot be diagnosed that way.[524]

Intermittent faults that appear between spot measurements cannot be found by spot measurement: one fault traced to a lifting bond wire inside a diode, which half-rectified and dropped a rail, was caught only by monitoring every rail continuously on about eight channels across two oscilloscopes.[551] A dual-processor bus fault was found as a faint spike on one address line present only when the second processor drove the bus, the inactive processor's connection forming an unterminated stub on the PCB trace; reading such a trace requires knowing which on-screen artefacts are normal.[222] Where bus signals travel in several directions rather than end to end, terminators can be placed mid-bus to absorb excess energy and are tuned empirically by watching the waveform and minimising overshoot.[684] Bench-verifying a high-current switching converter means measuring the dead time between switch pulses on an oscilloscope, because overlap destroys the bridge; the measurement is taken directly on the IGBTs using high-voltage probes with either isolated probes or an isolation transformer on the instrument.[522] A power supply intended for harsh service is qualified by deliberately short-circuiting its output with a screwdriver repeatedly while watching the recovery on an oscilloscope, then re-running the full acceptance suite to confirm nothing has degraded.[701] Putting an oscilloscope across the grounds of two powered boards demonstrates that no connection is ideal and that the two grounds move relative to each other; differential signalling absorbs a volt or two of common-mode difference, adequate between adjacent boards over a good chassis ground but not over a hundred-foot run.[704] A modern processor core rail is not a static voltage: it is commanded dynamically between roughly two volts and 0.3 volts and into sleep states as workloads change, and a 15-to-20-watt part can demand 150-amp peaks within ten microseconds of waking, so the oscilloscope shows a continuously moving rail rather than a DC level.[566] In a trailing-edge dimmer switching about four amps, the inductance of the supply transformer's secondary produced an 800-volt spike at turn-off that destroyed an internal PCB-mount mains power supply; the narrow, large-amplitude spike was found by scoping the incoming mains, and the fix is to avoid the transformer or add a few microfarads across its output.[524]

Low-level AC measurement is another oscilloscope task: a digital multimeter's AC accuracy specification typically applies only above about a tenth of full scale, below which its rectifier-based RMS converter becomes nonlinear, so low-level AC measurement needs either a dedicated AC millivoltmeter or an oscilloscope with a preamplifier front end.[464] A JTAG port left enabled gives a usable improvised oscilloscope: running boundary scan in bypass mode shifts bits through at up to a few hundred megahertz, sampling pin states without any external instrument.[693]

## Failure modes and limitations

Beyond the grounding hazards described above, oscilloscope front ends are fragile and expensive to repair, so protection circuitry that disconnects the delicate stages on excess voltage or power dissipation, with a panel light indicating it has acted, is a deliberate design feature rather than an accident of later models.[459] Electron microscope work requires probing rails at thousands of volts, and an arc-over inside a high-voltage tank destroys oscilloscope input channels, so in that field oscilloscopes are treated as consumables; higher-kilovolt tanks are gas filled while scanning-electron-microscope tanks are usually oil insulated or potted.[431] The LMH6518 variable-gain amplifier, containing the differential and variable-gain stages, sits in the front end of almost every modern oscilloscope and was originally specified to handle 400 millivolts of input offset, which is what supports the front-panel vertical position control; a silent redesign of the amplifier reduced its input-offset capability below that figure without a part number change, the difference being visible only deep in the datasheet, and oscilloscopes built with the later part have been disabled by excessive front-end DC offset.[727]

Where measurements are computed in software rather than in the acquisition hardware, enabling one of them can collapse throughput: on one instrument a single horizontal frequency measurement took the waveform update rate from a million per second to under a thousand, while vertical measurements cost nothing.[619] Implementing serial decode, FFT and measurement functions in a custom acquisition ASIC rather than in software means those functions can be enabled without reducing the waveform update rate, which is the practical reason a vendor develops its own silicon.[619]

## Market structure and economics

### Price structure

The entry-level oscilloscope bandwidth stayed at 25 MHz, rising to 50 MHz, for many years, because a manufacturer that unilaterally raises the low-end specification must build enough volume to supply the whole segment rather than just its own customers.[72] Rigol collapsed the price of its 100 MHz model to that of its 50 MHz model, which had been 399 dollars, making the lower-bandwidth unit obsolete overnight and giving owners of the 50 MHz version a straight resell-and-rebuy path in place of the firmware bandwidth hack.[72] The Rigol DS1052E entered at eight hundred dollars, dropped to four hundred within a year or two and stayed there for years, later selling around 349 dollars; the floor on a bench oscilloscope's price is set by the enclosure, display, probes and silicon rather than by the design.[455] Digital oscilloscopes went from nothing under a thousand dollars to capable instruments at 250 to 300 dollars within about five years, following the same deflation as the rest of the industry, with new features and half the previous price appearing annually.[127] The modern price ladder runs from a four-channel Rigol or Siglent around four hundred dollars, through an eight-hundred-dollar tier buying a larger touchscreen, higher sample rate and more memory, to four-figure instruments that exceed hobby requirements; the four-hundred-dollar four-channel instrument serves general daily use indefinitely.[606] Unlocking a 999-dollar four-channel bench oscilloscope yields 800 MHz to 1 GHz of bandwidth with around 500 megapoints of memory plus a dual-channel 100 MHz signal generator, capability that had previously belonged to instruments an order of magnitude more expensive; Rigol priced the result of a multi-year in-house ASIC programme at 999 dollars although the nearest competing instrument was around double that and a 1500-dollar price would still have led the segment on capability per dollar.[710]

At the top of the market, a high-end oscilloscope is a compliance instrument rather than a waveform viewer: it is bought to run automated analysis of high-speed serial signals against a standard such as SATA, including jitter decomposition over billions of measurements to produce an error-rate figure, with the analysis software typically sold as a costly extra.[104] The Keysight UXR reaches a true 110 GHz of real-time bandwidth on all channels at 256 gigasamples per second using an indium phosphide front end, and real-time bandwidth at that level lets a 50 GHz transmitter be sampled live and its modulation and demodulation analysed inside the instrument, with customers largely in high-speed serial links inside FPGAs and similar silicon.[404] The top of the market reaches a million dollars per instrument, at which point demonstration units are not shipped to prospective customers.[236] Tektronix's 6 series was built on two new in-house ASICs giving 6.25 gigasamples per second per channel with 12-bit resolution at around twenty-five thousand dollars; the LeCroy HDO8000A preceded it by two months with eight analogue channels at 12 bits and 10 gigasamples per second, plus a separate external trigger and 16 digital channels.[347]

Extending real-time oscilloscope bandwidth past 100 GHz depends on III–V semiconductor processes rather than silicon—indium phosphide heterojunction bipolar and high-electron-mobility transistor technology, whose wafers and chips are expensive—which is why that capability sits with vertically integrated owners of the process.[98] The systems needed to test, calibrate and qualify a gigahertz-class oscilloscope are themselves major instruments, and the requirement scales sharply with the bandwidth being verified.[236] High-value instrument manufacture does not follow the usual offshoring logic: at fifty to a hundred thousand dollars per unit the labour content is negligible against calibration, qualification and volume, so production stays where the engineering is.[236] Test equipment must lead the technology it measures: each new serial data rate requires an oscilloscope fast enough to see it before the first silicon exists, and the instrument maker must also be able to prove that what the instrument displays is correct.[714]

### Licensing, upgrades and procurement

Oscilloscope vendors sell sample memory, serial decode and bandwidth as separately licensed options on hardware that already contains the capability, bandwidth in particular being limited in software below what the front end can do.[117] The stated economic case for software-locked options is that leading-edge development is funded by margin on high-end features, and selling a fully unlocked 1 GHz decoding oscilloscope at four hundred dollars would not sustain the engineering that produced it.[117] Some vendors state that different bandwidth models are physically different hardware when the boards are in fact identical and the difference is a licence setting, which is the factual basis for the argument that unlocking a purchased instrument costs the vendor no unit sale.[136] Where a vendor sells bandwidth as a licence key, the hardware can also be modified directly: a top-of-line 100 MHz model was raised in bandwidth by a hardware change rather than by the licence, since the firmware setting ultimately only configures hardware that is already present, and some oscilloscopes ship with unpopulated pads for additional memory so that fitting the RAM chips and changing the model configuration converts the unit into the higher model, including features such as a colour screen.[339][434] The four-channel entry-level oscilloscopes sold around four hundred dollars carry hardware capable of 200 MHz and can be unlocked to it, so the licensed bandwidth understates what the instrument on the bench can do.[606]

Field-upgradeable bandwidth and features suit corporate procurement, where spending above a capital-expenditure threshold requires senior approval: a lower-specification instrument clears the threshold now and incremental licence upgrades can be bought a year at a time.[145] Instrument acquisition inside large organisations is governed by which budget the money comes from rather than total cost: rental at two hundred thousand dollars a year can be approved where a fifty-thousand-dollar capital purchase cannot.[136] Test-equipment vendors win educational sales by supplying courseware: shipping course books that explain how the instrument works removes the lecturer's preparation effort, and instructors buy on the strength of the pre-written material.[189] For teaching laboratories the selection criteria are warranty and replacement support, the supplied teaching material, and whether the same model will still be purchasable in ten years, since lab notes written for one model must remain valid for its replacements.[170] Acquisition silicon has a very long service life: one chip carried through a vendor's 1000 and 2000 series and into the TBS basics line, that vendor's cheapest oscilloscope at around three hundred dollars sold largely into school laboratories, and was still shipping years after introduction.[646]

The incumbent high-end instrument makers have retreated from the low end rather than abandoning basic research: they cannot compete on price with newer entrants, and the margin that funds work such as 80 GHz front ends is at the top of the range.[164] High-bandwidth oscilloscopes are export-controlled in the same way as sensitive current-measurement instruments, a restriction rooted in the era when the reason to want a very fast oscilloscope was nuclear or weapons instrumentation rather than communications.[119]

### Buying and the used market

Second-hand test equipment markets are badly mispriced at the edges: a 300 MHz four-channel Tektronix DPO4034 with probes plus a Fluke 87 multimeter, a current-model combination listing near ten thousand dollars, sold for five hundred dollars on a local classifieds site, and a 300 MHz four-channel instrument realising six to seven thousand dollars on resale could be replaced by a second-hand 500 MHz Agilent of comparable sample rate for around four thousand, leaving three to four thousand dollars in hand.[18] A standard purchasing rule for test equipment is to "go as cheap as you can until you can't use it anymore," because most work uses only base functionality and the real limitation announces itself clearly when it arrives, which also identifies exactly which specification the replacement must improve.[18] Bandwidth that will not be used daily should be bought second-hand rather than new, since RF work at the hobby level is usually done with a pre-certified module.[606]

A near-free older analogue oscilloscope is a rational first instrument: it teaches the fundamentals of what the display is showing, after which a buyer can name the specific features the next instrument must have instead of guessing from specifications, and a triggered-sweep model is the minimum worth acquiring even when older analogue oscilloscopes can be had for the cost of shipping.[117] About 10 MHz of bandwidth is enough for a beginner's instrument, and USB oscilloscope families including Picoscope, Tektronix and Keysight offer an upgrade path from there without changing the working method.[549] A first oscilloscope should be a model that many other people own, because community knowledge is the only support available when something goes wrong with an unbranded instrument, and the bottom of the market brings noise and instability that a beginner cannot distinguish from circuit faults.[567] The bottom of the market is not a cheaper version of a usable instrument: pocket and toy oscilloscopes bottom out around a hundred kilohertz of bandwidth, a beginner on substandard equipment meets artefacts such as aliasing before having the experience to recognise them as instrument behaviour, and even a free or fifty-dollar old analogue oscilloscope is generally not worth taking on for that reason.[606] Before buying bandwidth to see the analogue behaviour of a fast digital signal, the question to settle is whether a logic analyzer answers the actual question, since most work on a 100 MHz microcontroller concerns bus content rather than edge shape.[606] For a shared or teaching laboratory, ten 100 MHz oscilloscopes are a better use of the same budget than one 1 GHz instrument at around fifteen thousand dollars, because gigahertz bandwidth serves only a handful of specialists.[287] The case for owning more than one oscilloscope is concurrent measurement: motor work may need a channel on every winding, and without an eight-channel instrument that means several oscilloscopes running together.[545]

## History

Early wartime radar such as the SCR-270 presented its output on an oscilloscope screen showing range and amplitude only, with interpretation left entirely to the operator's skill.[729] The Tektronix 511 was the company's first oscilloscope; the later 556 was a dual-beam plug-in instrument whose two beams and two plug-in slots gave it unusual measurement flexibility.[119] Tektronix's late-1970s purely analogue 1 GHz oscilloscope was developed to order for the United States Atomic Energy Commission's nuclear weapons testing, on terms of roughly 30,000 dollars each, a committed order of a thousand units, and the right to sell the instrument commercially afterwards.[119] Tektronix was vertically integrated to an unusual degree, manufacturing its own cathode ray tubes and supplying items such as ceramic terminal strips and matched silver solder with the instrument.[421] The TM500 modular mainframe supplies power and a chassis into which single-function plug-ins are inserted, including signal generators, multimeters, calibration fixtures, and modules such as a bare op amp with front-panel terminals that can be wired externally into an integrator, differentiator or summer.[655]

Consumer tube oscilloscopes of the 1960s from makers such as RCA, Eico and Heathkit have bandwidths around 100 kilohertz and contain roughly six active devices, so they cannot be used to test anything by modern standards; their resale value rests on appearance rather than capability, unlike a Tektronix 465, which is worth more because it remains usable.[655] The entry price for a real oscilloscope has been roughly stable in nominal terms while the capability behind it has grown enormously: eight hundred dollars bought a dual-channel 20 MHz analogue instrument from a reputable brand in the 1980s.[606] Support for writing captures to USB mass storage arrived on bench oscilloscopes only around the mid-2000s, because the file-system and USB host stack that a general-purpose operating system supplies for free had to be built from scratch on an embedded platform.[325] The design techniques inside classic HP, Agilent, Keysight and Tektronix instruments were largely never documented in one place; what exists is deliberately scattered across application notes, schematics and patents, because in a specialised market there is no benefit in explaining methods to competitors.[459] On the software side, Sigrok began as a logic analyzer project and grew into an open-source layer that talks to multimeters and oscilloscopes for real-time capture across many vendors' hardware, targeting Windows, Linux and macOS and far too heavy to port onto a low-power microcontroller without being stripped down.[665]

## Maintenance and repair

Intermittent or dead front-panel controls on an older oscilloscope are commonly failed rotary encoders, often from stored moisture ingress; the encoders are frequently generic catalogue parts available from a distributor, and manufacturers have supplied full service manuals on request, making the repair routine.[229] Serviceability of vintage instruments falls off sharply with date of manufacture: between Tektronix oscilloscopes of the 1970s and those of the 1980s the complexity rose steeply with switching power supplies and proprietary custom ICs, and parts that no longer exist make the later ones much harder to repair.[655]
