Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) expresses how strongly a desired signal stands above the noise accompanying it, determining whether information can be converted, communicated, recovered, or resolved.<sup><a href="#ref-185" title="Ep 185: An Interview with Hank Zumbahlen - Zoppa Zumbahlen Zateticism (February 17, 2014)">[185]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-352" title="Ep 352: Conning with Michael Ossmann (July 17, 2017)">[352]</a></sup> It functions as an engineering budget: available ratio can be exchanged for data rate, integration time, measurement resolution, modulation complexity, or operating range.<sup><a href="#ref-179" title="Ep 179: Greg Charvat Returns With A Book! - Laboratory Literature Laureate (January 6, 2014)">[179]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-427" title="Ep 427: An Interview with Maarten Engelen (January 27, 2019)">[427]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-483" title="Ep 483: An Interview with Adrian Tang">[483]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-533" title="Ep 533: Microwave measurement with Joel Dunsmore (March 7, 2021)">[533]</a></sup> Because both the signal and noise sides can be engineered, improving SNR may involve increasing signal energy, reducing noise, narrowing bandwidth, integrating longer, or using a waveform that separates the wanted signal from interference.<sup><a href="#ref-107" title="Ep 107: An interview with Tony Long - Millimeter Microwave Magician (August 5, 2012)">[107]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-376" title="Ep 376: An Interview with Richard Ginus (January 21, 2018)">[376]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-443" title="Ep 443: An Interview with JP Norair (May 19, 2019)">[443]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-560" title="Ep 560: High End Audio with Remco Stoutjesdijk (October 3, 2021)">[560]</a></sup>

## Data conversion and measurement

Resolution and accuracy are distinct converter properties: resolution describes how many output bits change, while accuracy describes how many of those bits contain meaningful information.<sup><a href="#ref-185" title="Ep 185: An Interview with Hank Zumbahlen - Zoppa Zumbahlen Zateticism (February 17, 2014)">[185]</a></sup> A converter specified as 24-bit therefore has to be interpreted through its signal-to-noise performance; grounding its inputs does not guarantee that its lower bits will remain stationary.<sup><a href="#ref-185" title="Ep 185: An Interview with Hank Zumbahlen - Zoppa Zumbahlen Zateticism (February 17, 2014)">[185]</a></sup>

Effective number of bits and SNR contain equivalent information expressed in different units, with the conversion represented by 6.02 times the bit count minus 1.76.<sup><a href="#ref-185" title="Ep 185: An Interview with Hank Zumbahlen - Zoppa Zumbahlen Zateticism (February 17, 2014)">[185]</a></sup> The choice between stating performance as effective bits or as a ratio often reflects an engineer's background rather than a difference in the underlying measurement.<sup><a href="#ref-185" title="Ep 185: An Interview with Hank Zumbahlen - Zoppa Zumbahlen Zateticism (February 17, 2014)">[185]</a></sup>

Converters with the same architecture and nominal bit count are not necessarily interchangeable.<sup><a href="#ref-65" title="Ep 65: Silego, ADCs & Seismic Detection - Dave's Dingo Dystocia">[65]</a></sup> An audio-oriented converter is optimized for dynamic performance such as distortion and signal-to-noise ratio across its band, whereas a strain-gauge bridge requires accurate DC measurement; using the audio part for the bridge can produce invalid results despite an identical 24-bit delta-sigma designation.<sup><a href="#ref-65" title="Ep 65: Silego, ADCs & Seismic Detection - Dave's Dingo Dystocia">[65]</a></sup>

The sigma-delta architecture obtains very high SNR at the cost of latency.<sup><a href="#ref-474" title="Ep 474: An Interview with Nash Reilly (January 12, 2020)">[474]</a></sup> This exchange is compatible with audio because delays on the order of a hundred milliseconds are not perceptually important, but the same delay can be unacceptable in a control loop.<sup><a href="#ref-474" title="Ep 474: An Interview with Nash Reilly (January 12, 2020)">[474]</a></sup>

Process-voltage scaling directly constrains analog SNR: reduced voltage swing across a resistor produces less signal for the same noise, lowering the achievable ratio.<sup><a href="#ref-579" title="Ep 579: ADC Chip Design with Anthony Wall (February 27, 2022)">[579]</a></sup> This constraint makes high-performance analog circuitry difficult and expensive to implement on processes optimized primarily for digital devices.<sup><a href="#ref-579" title="Ep 579: ADC Chip Design with Anthony Wall (February 27, 2022)">[579]</a></sup>

Similar considerations apply inside memory circuits, where a sense amplifier consists of two cross-coupled inverters.<sup><a href="#ref-297" title="Ep 297: An Interview with Jake Baker (May 4, 2016)">[297]</a></sup> Much of the subsequent engineering reduces the amplifier's offset, improving SNR and thereby increasing the yield of the complete device.<sup><a href="#ref-297" title="Ep 297: An Interview with Jake Baker (May 4, 2016)">[297]</a></sup>

## Communications capacity and link design

Shannon's channel-capacity theorem sets a theoretical ceiling on the number of bits per second that can pass through a channel of specified bandwidth at a specified SNR.<sup><a href="#ref-352" title="Ep 352: Conning with Michael Ossmann (July 17, 2017)">[352]</a></sup> The ratio therefore operates as a currency that can be spent on greater throughput.<sup><a href="#ref-352" title="Ep 352: Conning with Michael Ossmann (July 17, 2017)">[352]</a></sup>

Because the ratio buys throughput, anything that caps it removes options rather than merely degrading performance. High crosstalk in a connector limits the achievable SNR, and in doing so eliminates signalling strategies that would otherwise have been available — which makes improving the connector, rather than devising cleverer modulation, the change that unlocks a higher data rate.<sup><a href="#ref-77" title="Ep 77: An Interview with Dr. Howard Johnson - Winsome Waveform Wizardry (January 9, 2012)">[77]</a></sup>

A link budget is the difference, in decibels, between transmitted power and the receiver's minimum usable sensitivity.<sup><a href="#ref-443" title="Ep 443: An Interview with JP Norair (May 19, 2019)">[443]</a></sup> For example, a transmitter supplying 20 dBm and a receiver with a real-world sensitivity of −121 dBm provide approximately 141 dB for distance, antenna effects, and obstructions.<sup><a href="#ref-443" title="Ep 443: An Interview with JP Norair (May 19, 2019)">[443]</a></sup>

Received signal strength and SNR are separate measurements.<sup><a href="#ref-443" title="Ep 443: An Interview with JP Norair (May 19, 2019)">[443]</a></sup> A strong wireless signal is generally better than about −60 dBm, but its SNR can still deteriorate when other devices raise the noise level in the same band; the lower interference typical of sub-gigahertz spectrum consequently gives those bands better outdoor behavior than the more heavily used 2.4 GHz band.<sup><a href="#ref-443" title="Ep 443: An Interview with JP Norair (May 19, 2019)">[443]</a></sup>

Higher-order modulation places more information into each hertz but requires a higher SNR for successful decoding.<sup><a href="#ref-430" title="Ep 430: Shahriar Discusses 5G (February 17, 2019)">[430]</a></sup> If that ratio is unavailable, the transmitter and receiver must be brought closer together or the system must spend more resources on processing; exhaustion of these options contributed to the use of millimetre-wave frequencies and beam-forming.<sup><a href="#ref-430" title="Ep 430: Shahriar Discusses 5G (February 17, 2019)">[430]</a></sup>

Adaptive radio links continuously trade throughput for margin by selecting lower modulation rates when vehicles, rain cells, or other obstructions reduce path quality.<sup><a href="#ref-533" title="Ep 533: Microwave measurement with Joel Dunsmore (March 7, 2021)">[533]</a></sup> Lower-rate modulation requires less SNR, while the highest-rate modes may operate with the signal only slightly above the noise, causing degradation to appear as intermittent pixelation rather than as an abrupt loss of service.<sup><a href="#ref-533" title="Ep 533: Microwave measurement with Joel Dunsmore (March 7, 2021)">[533]</a></sup>

The same exchange appears at extremely low data rates: a one-megabit-per-second link requires a comparatively good SNR, while a text-message-sized packet sent once per day requires very little.<sup><a href="#ref-427" title="Ep 427: An Interview with Maarten Engelen (January 27, 2019)">[427]</a></sup> When an application tolerates that low rate, reducing throughput can extend communication into locations inaccessible to a faster link.<sup><a href="#ref-427" title="Ep 427: An Interview with Maarten Engelen (January 27, 2019)">[427]</a></sup>

A broadband noise source acts as a jammer regardless of intent.<sup><a href="#ref-391" title="Ep 391: Only A Transmitter (May 6, 2018)">[391]</a></sup> Moving the receiver next to the transmitter does not necessarily restore the link, because successful decoding depends on the ratio between signal and noise rather than on absolute received level alone; a coded digital signal cannot be recovered once the noise floor has been raised above it.<sup><a href="#ref-391" title="Ep 391: Only A Transmitter (May 6, 2018)">[391]</a></sup>

## Recovering signals from noise

Averaging improves SNR because repeated signal components accumulate in proportion to the number of measurements, while uncorrelated noise accumulates only in proportion to the square root of that number.<sup><a href="#ref-483" title="Ep 483: An Interview with Adrian Tang">[483]</a></sup> Sufficient repetitions can therefore reveal a signal that was initially buried below the noise.<sup><a href="#ref-483" title="Ep 483: An Interview with Adrian Tang">[483]</a></sup>

Weak-signal radio operation applies the same principle by using minimum bandwidth and modes slow enough that transmitting a call sign can take a minute.<sup><a href="#ref-107" title="Ep 107: An interview with Tony Long - Millimeter Microwave Magician (August 5, 2012)">[107]</a></sup> Receiver integration over that interval substantially improves noise performance without increasing transmitter power.<sup><a href="#ref-107" title="Ep 107: An interview with Tony Long - Millimeter Microwave Magician (August 5, 2012)">[107]</a></sup>

Satellite navigation uses processing gain to recover signals below the receiver's noise floor: each information bit is spread across 1023 chips, and the receiver correlates the received waveform with the known chip pattern.<sup><a href="#ref-352" title="Ep 352: Conning with Michael Ossmann (July 17, 2017)">[352]</a></sup> This spreading makes the low-power signal from a distant satellite recoverable despite an initially unfavorable ratio.<sup><a href="#ref-352" title="Ep 352: Conning with Michael Ossmann (July 17, 2017)">[352]</a></sup>

In dense urban environments, one transmission can arrive repeatedly after reflections from buildings, producing copies with different delays and amplitudes.<sup><a href="#ref-376" title="Ep 376: An Interview with Richard Ginus (January 21, 2018)">[376]</a></sup> Chirped spread-spectrum modulation allows the intended signal to be separated from these reflections, improving the effective SNR without additional transmit power.<sup><a href="#ref-376" title="Ep 376: An Interview with Richard Ginus (January 21, 2018)">[376]</a></sup>

A lock-in amplifier exploits advance knowledge of a signal's frequency to recover a carrier from noise thousands of times larger.<sup><a href="#ref-455" title="Ep 455: Bill and Dave's Excellent Equipment (August 19, 2019)">[455]</a></sup> It can extract microvolt-level signals from volt-level noise and, in extreme cases, recover signals extending into the nanovolt range.<sup><a href="#ref-455" title="Ep 455: Bill and Dave's Excellent Equipment (August 19, 2019)">[455]</a></sup>

Reducing each observation to one bit—recording only whether a clock sampled a one or a zero—substantially worsens the SNR of each individual sample.<sup><a href="#ref-693" title="Ep 693: Small Scale Electronics Manufacturing with Colin O'Flynn (May 13, 2025)">[693]</a></sup> A sufficiently large number of such samples can nevertheless recover information that no individual sample preserves, illustrating the same quantity-versus-quality exchange used by other one-bit receivers.<sup><a href="#ref-693" title="Ep 693: Small Scale Electronics Manufacturing with Colin O'Flynn (May 13, 2025)">[693]</a></sup>

Large, properly spaced phased arrays provide a direct means of imaging at frequencies where many channels are needed to pull genuine signals out of noise.<sup><a href="#ref-729" title="Ep 729: The Terahertz Frontier with Greg Charvat of Teradar (July 22, 2026)">[729]</a></sup> This physical recovery is distinct from allowing an algorithm to generate a plausible output where no corresponding signal was actually recovered.<sup><a href="#ref-729" title="Ep 729: The Terahertz Frontier with Greg Charvat of Teradar (July 22, 2026)">[729]</a></sup>

## Radar and imaging

Through-wall radar can use long, low-peak-power frequency-modulated continuous-wave chirps instead of extremely short, high-power impulses.<sup><a href="#ref-115" title="Ep 115: An Interview with Dr Greg Charvat - Watcher of Wraithlike Walls (September 30, 2012)">[115]</a></sup> The principal difficulty is rejecting the very strong reflection from the wall, because that return can saturate the receiver and distort signals from objects behind it.<sup><a href="#ref-115" title="Ep 115: An Interview with Dr Greg Charvat - Watcher of Wraithlike Walls (September 30, 2012)">[115]</a></sup>

The impulse alternative requires approximately 10 kW in a two-nanosecond pulse and digitization at several gigasamples per second.<sup><a href="#ref-115" title="Ep 115: An Interview with Dr Greg Charvat - Watcher of Wraithlike Walls (September 30, 2012)">[115]</a></sup> Faster digitizers provide poorer performance than slower ones, and the resulting data volume makes real-time processing impractical; long waveforms instead shift more of the problem into signal processing, where it is less expensive.<sup><a href="#ref-115" title="Ep 115: An Interview with Dr Greg Charvat - Watcher of Wraithlike Walls (September 30, 2012)">[115]</a></sup>

Angular resolution can also be purchased with SNR.<sup><a href="#ref-179" title="Ep 179: Greg Charvat Returns With A Book! - Laboratory Literature Laureate (January 6, 2014)">[179]</a></sup> By comparing amplitude and phase across four sub-arrays, a system with a 20 dB SNR can subdivide a nominal ten-degree beam by a factor of one hundred, resolving angles to approximately one-tenth of a degree although the physical antenna alone cannot resolve better than ten degrees.<sup><a href="#ref-179" title="Ep 179: Greg Charvat Returns With A Book! - Laboratory Literature Laureate (January 6, 2014)">[179]</a></sup>

For a distant target, spot size is proportional to wavelength multiplied by distance and divided by antenna radius, so resolution improves with aperture.<sup><a href="#ref-483" title="Ep 483: An Interview with Adrian Tang">[483]</a></sup> When a sufficiently large antenna cannot be carried on an airborne platform, trajectory and integration time become the remaining design variables; flying closer improves resolution but increases relative velocity, shortening the available integration time at the point where more SNR is needed.<sup><a href="#ref-483" title="Ep 483: An Interview with Adrian Tang">[483]</a></sup>

## Audio and sensing media

Analog playback can contain harmonic distortion that should not be confused with improved fidelity.<sup><a href="#ref-270" title="Ep 270: An Interview With Dafydd Roche (October 7, 2015)">[270]</a></sup> Measured SNR places vinyl above cassette but below compact disc.<sup><a href="#ref-270" title="Ep 270: An Interview With Dafydd Roche (October 7, 2015)">[270]</a></sup>

As reproduction equipment has approached the point at which further improvements to the signal produce little practical benefit, the noise side of the ratio becomes more significant.<sup><a href="#ref-560" title="Ep 560: High End Audio with Remco Stoutjesdijk (October 3, 2021)">[560]</a></sup> In listening systems, this shifts the remaining engineering problem toward room and environmental noise rather than toward the playback equipment alone.<sup><a href="#ref-560" title="Ep 560: High End Audio with Remco Stoutjesdijk (October 3, 2021)">[560]</a></sup>

Image-sensor age and commercial category do not by themselves determine measurement quality.<sup><a href="#ref-325" title="Ep 325: An Interview with David Kronstein (Tesla500) (November 30, 2016)">[325]</a></sup> An older sensor technology can retain better noise performance than newer commercial alternatives, making it the more suitable scientific or space instrument where SNR is the governing requirement.<sup><a href="#ref-325" title="Ep 325: An Interview with David Kronstein (Tesla500) (November 30, 2016)">[325]</a></sup>
