Artificial intelligence, as the term is applied in electronics practice, covers machine-learning systems that derive their behaviour from training data rather than from hand-written rules.<sup><a href="#ref-435" data-tip="Ep 435: An Interview with Andreas Spiess (March 24, 2019)" title="Ep 435: An Interview with Andreas Spiess (March 24, 2019)">[435]</a></sup> The field divides cleanly into an expensive rule-creation phase, exemplified by training frameworks such as TensorFlow, and a cheap rule-application phase that even a small microcontroller can execute; the difference from a hand-written expert system is that no human ever knows what the derived rules are.<sup><a href="#ref-435" data-tip="Ep 435: An Interview with Andreas Spiess (March 24, 2019)" title="Ep 435: An Interview with Andreas Spiess (March 24, 2019)">[435]</a></sup> The release of ChatGPT marks the point at which the field entered public consciousness and companies began renaming products and businesses around it.<sup><a href="#ref-685" data-tip="Ep 685: Data Provenance in the Home, Server, and Fab (December 23, 2024)" title="Ep 685: Data Provenance in the Home, Server, and Fab (December 23, 2024)">[685]</a></sup> For hardware engineers the practical consequences run in two directions: machine-learning systems consume sensor inputs, which places electronics designers in the role of producing data that downstream decision-making systems can digest,<sup><a href="#ref-428" data-tip="Ep 428: Setting Fire To The Tracks (February 3, 2019)" title="Ep 428: Setting Fire To The Tracks (February 3, 2019)">[428]</a></sup> and the same tools are increasingly applied to the design work itself.<sup><a href="#ref-626" data-tip="Ep 626: Intelligent Routing with Sergiy Nesterenko (April 2, 2023)" title="Ep 626: Intelligent Routing with Sergiy Nesterenko (April 2, 2023)">[626]</a></sup>

## Historical background

The Eliza conversational program was implemented in roughly forty lines of BASIC, showing that convincing dialogue behaviour could be produced with almost no underlying intelligence.<sup><a href="#ref-258" data-tip="Ep 258: An Interview with Bertrand Irrisou and Gerald Friedland of Audeme (July 14, 2015)" title="Ep 258: An Interview with Bertrand Irrisou and Gerald Friedland of Audeme (July 14, 2015)">[258]</a></sup> Claims that electronic design automation tools use artificial intelligence are likewise not new; vendors have made the same marketing claim for around thirty years.<sup><a href="#ref-470" data-tip="Ep 470: Just Add Salt (December 8, 2019)" title="Ep 470: Just Add Salt (December 8, 2019)">[470]</a></sup>

Expectations about the underlying hardware have also shifted. Field-programmable gate arrays were initially expected to dominate machine-learning hardware because of their versatility, but graphics processors proved better suited and displaced them for that workload.<sup><a href="#ref-660" data-tip="Ep 660: My Toothbrush Is Broadcasting (March 4, 2024)" title="Ep 660: My Toothbrush Is Broadcasting (March 4, 2024)">[660]</a></sup> The economic argument against gate-array-based inference at scale is chip count and unit cost: the number of devices required makes them expensive relative to graphics processors, which are themselves not cheap.<sup><a href="#ref-660" data-tip="Ep 660: My Toothbrush Is Broadcasting (March 4, 2024)" title="Ep 660: My Toothbrush Is Broadcasting (March 4, 2024)">[660]</a></sup> NVIDIA's shift from the GTX to the RTX architecture made its consumer cards inference-capable, enabling locally hosted assistants that run on a user's own graphics card and index the user's own files and video.<sup><a href="#ref-660" data-tip="Ep 660: My Toothbrush Is Broadcasting (March 4, 2024)" title="Ep 660: My Toothbrush Is Broadcasting (March 4, 2024)">[660]</a></sup> Intel's competitive position eroded on several fronts at once over the same period: TSMC pulled ahead on process, Arm and AMD pressed the server market, RISC-V grew from a small base, and the graphics and accelerator segment was ceded to NVIDIA.<sup><a href="#ref-685" data-tip="Ep 685: Data Provenance in the Home, Server, and Fab (December 23, 2024)" title="Ep 685: Data Provenance in the Home, Server, and Fab (December 23, 2024)">[685]</a></sup> Intel's earlier acquisition of Movidius had been aimed at the spatial half of machine vision, combining stereo depth sensing and inference on the same silicon.<sup><a href="#ref-517" data-tip="Ep 517: Depth and AI with Brandon Gilles and Brian Weinstein (November 15, 2020)" title="Ep 517: Depth and AI with Brandon Gilles and Brian Weinstein (November 15, 2020)">[517]</a></sup>

## Training and inference

A model is not a self-contained algorithm; it must be trained on large volumes of real-world data, which is why Tesla built its own processors and supercomputer racks to run that training.<sup><a href="#ref-604" data-tip="Ep 604: Robo Fry Guy (October 9, 2022)" title="Ep 604: Robo Fry Guy (October 9, 2022)">[604]</a></sup> Training hardware pushes power delivery to extremes: Tesla's training racks were presented as containing one of the densest power supplies in the world measured in watts per square centimetre.<sup><a href="#ref-604" data-tip="Ep 604: Robo Fry Guy (October 9, 2022)" title="Ep 604: Robo Fry Guy (October 9, 2022)">[604]</a></sup> A characteristic failure mode of massively parallel training racks is intermittent dropout of individual processors among thousands, which disrupts the distributed job rather than crashing the machine outright.<sup><a href="#ref-604" data-tip="Ep 604: Robo Fry Guy (October 9, 2022)" title="Ep 604: Robo Fry Guy (October 9, 2022)">[604]</a></sup>

Training custom models remains out of reach for most individual developers, who are limited to using models built by others.<sup><a href="#ref-651" data-tip="Ep 651: Learning Computing with Jeff Geerling (November 20, 2023)" title="Ep 651: Learning Computing with Jeff Geerling (November 20, 2023)">[651]</a></sup> Useful competence with these tools does not require building or training models at all; knowing which tool suits which application and how to drive it is sufficient skill for most workplace needs.<sup><a href="#ref-631" data-tip="Ep 631: A Noisy Rude Bus (May 7, 2023)" title="Ep 631: A Noisy Rude Bus (May 7, 2023)">[631]</a></sup> For engineers without a data science background, the fast.ai course is an accessible entry point.<sup><a href="#ref-428" data-tip="Ep 428: Setting Fire To The Tracks (February 3, 2019)" title="Ep 428: Setting Fire To The Tracks (February 3, 2019)">[428]</a></sup>

Labelling is often simpler than it appears. A domain expert can produce labelled training data by physically performing the event — repeatedly opening and closing a door while watching the sensor waveform — without understanding the data itself.<sup><a href="#ref-525" data-tip="Ep 525: Open FPGA Toolchains and Machine Learning with Brian Faith of QuickLogic (January 10, 2021)" title="Ep 525: Open FPGA Toolchains and Machine Learning with Brian Faith of QuickLogic (January 10, 2021)">[525]</a></sup> This inverts conventional event detection, which forces the engineer to impose a mathematical model such as fixed thresholds or slope detection onto a natural signal and iterate by guess and check; a trained classifier instead starts from the desired output and derives the detector.<sup><a href="#ref-525" data-tip="Ep 525: Open FPGA Toolchains and Machine Learning with Brian Faith of QuickLogic (January 10, 2021)" title="Ep 525: Open FPGA Toolchains and Machine Learning with Brian Faith of QuickLogic (January 10, 2021)">[525]</a></sup> A machine-learning sensor proof of concept can be assembled for about fifty dollars, using a fifty-dollar development board with a free community edition of the model-building tooling.<sup><a href="#ref-525" data-tip="Ep 525: Open FPGA Toolchains and Machine Learning with Brian Faith of QuickLogic (January 10, 2021)" title="Ep 525: Open FPGA Toolchains and Machine Learning with Brian Faith of QuickLogic (January 10, 2021)">[525]</a></sup>

## Inference at the edge

Low-cost single-board modules costing around eighteen dollars combine graphics-class silicon with camera input to perform inference on board rather than in the cloud.<sup><a href="#ref-428" data-tip="Ep 428: Setting Fire To The Tracks (February 3, 2019)" title="Ep 428: Setting Fire To The Tracks (February 3, 2019)">[428]</a></sup> The Raspberry Pi 5, with on-chip inference acceleration, performs roughly like a good laptop of five to eight years earlier, making it practical to ingest camera data, compress video and stream it from a small embedded board.<sup><a href="#ref-651" data-tip="Ep 651: Learning Computing with Jeff Geerling (November 20, 2023)" title="Ep 651: Learning Computing with Jeff Geerling (November 20, 2023)">[651]</a></sup> Dedicated audio inference accelerators run a programmed model over a live microphone stream to classify background acoustic events such as typing, several people talking, a person shouting, or a plate being thrown.<sup><a href="#ref-661" data-tip="Ep 661: Blogging Electronics with Pallav Aggarwal (March 10, 2024)" title="Ep 661: Blogging Electronics with Pallav Aggarwal (March 10, 2024)">[661]</a></sup> Custom hardware accelerators for image and audio processing are often commissioned as exploratory projects, built first and kept only if the measured benefit justifies it.<sup><a href="#ref-661" data-tip="Ep 661: Blogging Electronics with Pallav Aggarwal (March 10, 2024)" title="Ep 661: Blogging Electronics with Pallav Aggarwal (March 10, 2024)">[661]</a></sup>

Arduino's move into industrial control paired familiar Arduino programming with terminal-block termination and ladder diagram support, with the claimed differentiator over conventional programmable logic controllers being the ability to run tiny machine-learning models on the same controller.<sup><a href="#ref-620" data-tip="Ep 620: Engineering Education with Dr Don Wilcher (February 20, 2023)" title="Ep 620: Engineering Education with Dr Don Wilcher (February 20, 2023)">[620]</a></sup>

## Robotics and autonomous systems

Onboard learning for aerial robotics is limited less by the algorithms than by resources: it demands heavy statistics plus enough memory to record flight data and process it in real time.<sup><a href="#ref-105" data-tip="Ep 105: An Interview with Chris Anderson - Deambulatory Daedal Drones (July 23, 2012)" title="Ep 105: An Interview with Chris Anderson - Deambulatory Daedal Drones (July 23, 2012)">[105]</a></sup> Where a drone has a fast telemetry link, the learning and heavy processing can be run on the ground station instead of onboard, with a self-contained airborne implementation being the harder ideal.<sup><a href="#ref-105" data-tip="Ep 105: An Interview with Chris Anderson - Deambulatory Daedal Drones (July 23, 2012)" title="Ep 105: An Interview with Chris Anderson - Deambulatory Daedal Drones (July 23, 2012)">[105]</a></sup> The Skydio drone performs onboard obstacle avoidance built on NVIDIA processors, allowing it to follow a person through a forest while avoiding branches and twigs.<sup><a href="#ref-538" data-tip="Ep 538: Missle Man with Bruce Simson (April 12, 2021)" title="Ep 538: Missle Man with Bruce Simson (April 12, 2021)">[538]</a></sup>

Tesla's own stated position was that full self-driving requires a gigantic leap in artificial intelligence before it becomes viable, while the feature was still sold as an option for around ten thousand dollars.<sup><a href="#ref-582" data-tip="Ep 582: The Same Wavelength (March 20, 2022)" title="Ep 582: The Same Wavelength (March 20, 2022)">[582]</a></sup> At the consumer end, a pool-cleaning robot sold on its intelligence failed to climb the pool walls, moved largely at random, and reset its own algorithm mid-run, with only partial mapping behaviour observed across repeated timed tests.<sup><a href="#ref-656" data-tip="Ep 656: Pneumatic Tubes, Straight To The Home (January 22, 2024)" title="Ep 656: Pneumatic Tubes, Straight To The Home (January 22, 2024)">[656]</a></sup>

## Machine vision and sensing

Most spatial-vision systems are assembled from two or three separate pieces — a neural accelerator, a general processor and a depth camera — whereas an integrated part combines the depth and inference functions.<sup><a href="#ref-517" data-tip="Ep 517: Depth and AI with Brandon Gilles and Brian Weinstein (November 15, 2020)" title="Ep 517: Depth and AI with Brandon Gilles and Brian Weinstein (November 15, 2020)">[517]</a></sup> Depth-and-inference boards are designed so that retargeting a reference example to a different tracked object takes roughly three lines of changed code, and some products boot straight into a person-tracking demonstration.<sup><a href="#ref-517" data-tip="Ep 517: Depth and AI with Brandon Gilles and Brian Weinstein (November 15, 2020)" title="Ep 517: Depth and AI with Brandon Gilles and Brian Weinstein (November 15, 2020)">[517]</a></sup>

Computed imaging is now the norm rather than the exception: modern phone cameras and large scientific instruments no longer capture an image directly but compute one by averaging and combining many frames or datasets in software.<sup><a href="#ref-618" data-tip="Ep 618: Refrigerators and Robots with Amitabh Shrivastava (February 5, 2023)" title="Ep 618: Refrigerators and Robots with Amitabh Shrivastava (February 5, 2023)">[618]</a></sup> The distinction still matters at the high end. High-end imaging radar is moving to full lambda-over-two phased arrays with thousands of channels at chip scale, pulling real signals out of the noise, rather than to sparse compromise arrays whose gaps are filled by inference.<sup><a href="#ref-729" data-tip="Ep 729: The Terahertz Frontier with Greg Charvat of Teradar (July 22, 2026)" title="Ep 729: The Terahertz Frontier with Greg Charvat of Teradar (July 22, 2026)">[729]</a></sup>

## Electronic design automation

DeepPCB is a cloud auto-routing service marketed on deep learning that accepts KiCad board files.<sup><a href="#ref-470" data-tip="Ep 470: Just Add Salt (December 8, 2019)" title="Ep 470: Just Add Salt (December 8, 2019)">[470]</a></sup> One proposed route to a machine-learning auto-router is supervised training on thousands of known-good board layouts with substantial graphics-processor resources behind it.<sup><a href="#ref-412" data-tip="Ep 412: 3 Cent Micros And 1000s of LEDs (October 21, 2018)" title="Ep 412: 3 Cent Micros And 1000s of LEDs (October 21, 2018)">[412]</a></sup> The structural objection is that board layout quality is dominated by component placement rather than track routing, estimated at about ninety percent placement, so an automated router that does not solve placement addresses the smaller part of the problem.<sup><a href="#ref-412" data-tip="Ep 412: 3 Cent Micros And 1000s of LEDs (October 21, 2018)" title="Ep 412: 3 Cent Micros And 1000s of LEDs (October 21, 2018)">[412]</a></sup>

Automated layout services such as Quilter are best understood as an incremental tool for board layout rather than as general intelligence, and their value judged on that basis.<sup><a href="#ref-654" data-tip="Ep 654: Pseudo Code...Pseudo Good (December 18, 2023)" title="Ep 654: Pseudo Code...Pseudo Good (December 18, 2023)">[654]</a></sup> One such service treats machine learning as one tool among several in service of a finished, verified board, and accepts human intervention in the loop when the software struggles, since the customer bought a layout rather than an algorithm.<sup><a href="#ref-626" data-tip="Ep 626: Intelligent Routing with Sergiy Nesterenko (April 2, 2023)" title="Ep 626: Intelligent Routing with Sergiy Nesterenko (April 2, 2023)">[626]</a></sup>

There are limits to what automation surfaces. A practising engineer's mental catalogue of parts covers cross-cutting facts — such as an eight-bit microcontroller in a five-pin package that also carries an I2C output — a kind of lateral part substitution that automated part suggestion does not surface.<sup><a href="#ref-262" data-tip="Ep 262: Jobs For Weirdos (August 12, 2015)" title="Ep 262: Jobs For Weirdos (August 12, 2015)">[262]</a></sup> Time spent personally laying out a board and studying its schematic also builds the mental model that makes hard debugging possible, such as tracing an unexplained result in an EMC chamber; delegating generation to a tool forfeits that context.<sup><a href="#ref-722" data-tip="Ep 722: AI Tooling with Matt Liberty and Luke Beno (April 22, 2026)" title="Ep 722: AI Tooling with Matt Liberty and Luke Beno (April 22, 2026)">[722]</a></sup>

## Software development

Vibe coding describes delegating the writing of code to a model while the human acts as product manager, supplying the specification and feedback on what works and what does not.<sup><a href="#ref-694" data-tip="Ep 694: Voltage, Vibes, and VOCs (May 21, 2025)" title="Ep 694: Voltage, Vibes, and VOCs (May 21, 2025)">[694]</a></sup> Generated code still requires a competent programmer to integrate the pieces and verify the result, because models confidently report success on tasks they have not actually completed.<sup><a href="#ref-631" data-tip="Ep 631: A Noisy Rude Bus (May 7, 2023)" title="Ep 631: A Noisy Rude Bus (May 7, 2023)">[631]</a></sup> Mistakes in generated code are harder to catch than mistakes in one's own work, both because the author of hand-written code has already formed a mental model of it and because the confident phrasing of model output lends the answer more weight than it merits.<sup><a href="#ref-614" data-tip="Ep 614: Reunion Impedance Matching and 2023 Predictions (January 8, 2023)" title="Ep 614: Reunion Impedance Matching and 2023 Predictions (January 8, 2023)">[614]</a></sup>

A concrete and modest use for a coding assistant is getting past the blank editor: producing something that at least semi-compiles, which the engineer then tweaks into working form.<sup><a href="#ref-713" data-tip="Ep 713: Rubber Duck Incarnate (January 25, 2026)" title="Ep 713: Rubber Duck Incarnate (January 25, 2026)">[713]</a></sup> On one personal project a coding assistant produced practically all of the code, an application the same practitioner distinguishes sharply from generative tools for board layout.<sup><a href="#ref-683" data-tip="Ep 683: Troubleshooting is the skill (November 20, 2024)" title="Ep 683: Troubleshooting is the skill (November 20, 2024)">[683]</a></sup> For students who have not previously written C, assistants are useful for getting syntax close enough to what they logically intend, which lets them cross from intent to compiling code.<sup><a href="#ref-711" data-tip="Ep 711: Medical Electronics Education with Mark Palmeri (December 21, 2025)" title="Ep 711: Medical Electronics Education with Mark Palmeri (December 21, 2025)">[711]</a></sup>

## Limits and failure modes of language models

Language models answer well-trodden questions competently but cannot be asked to extend past the boundary of what is documented, because they have no knowledge beyond their training material.<sup><a href="#ref-693" data-tip="Ep 693: Small Scale Electronics Manufacturing with Colin O'Flynn (May 13, 2025)" title="Ep 693: Small Scale Electronics Manufacturing with Colin O'Flynn (May 13, 2025)">[693]</a></sup> Current systems do not originate ideas, and building genuine machine intelligence would first require defining and encompassing what real intelligence is, a task barely begun.<sup><a href="#ref-684" data-tip="Ep 684: Lee Felsenstein: The Computer Revolution & Counterculture" title="Ep 684: Lee Felsenstein: The Computer Revolution & Counterculture">[684]</a></sup> A practical way to calibrate how much to trust a model is to question it on the subject in which one has the deepest personal expertise, which exposes the limits of its answers.<sup><a href="#ref-704" data-tip="Ep 704: Applied Embedded Electronics with Jerry Twomey (October 2, 2025)" title="Ep 704: Applied Embedded Electronics with Jerry Twomey (October 2, 2025)">[704]</a></sup> Taking a confidently phrased answer on faith, without checking references, multiple sources and primary texts, is hazardous in domains such as signal grounding where established best practice governs the result.<sup><a href="#ref-704" data-tip="Ep 704: Applied Embedded Electronics with Jerry Twomey (October 2, 2025)" title="Ep 704: Applied Embedded Electronics with Jerry Twomey (October 2, 2025)">[704]</a></sup>

Training-data bias shows up directly in engineering answers. Asked to design a circuit, general-purpose models converge on the ESP32 roughly eight times out of ten, a bias that follows from how heavily that part appears in the online projects the models were trained on.<sup><a href="#ref-724" data-tip="Ep 724: All Heat, No Useful Work (May 25, 2026)" title="Ep 724: All Heat, No Useful Work (May 25, 2026)">[724]</a></sup>

Staleness is a related problem. Assistants that scrape the web propose deprecated Zephyr APIs, because the real-time operating system evolves faster than the scraped material and has introduced new constructs such as hardware models; students are shown this failure in the second week of one course so they are not led down an outdated path.<sup><a href="#ref-711" data-tip="Ep 711: Medical Electronics Education with Mark Palmeri (December 21, 2025)" title="Ep 711: Medical Electronics Education with Mark Palmeri (December 21, 2025)">[711]</a></sup> Gemini's web index was dated January 2025, an age that matters for fast-moving frameworks, and the practical outcome was abandoning language-model assistance for Zephyr work entirely.<sup><a href="#ref-711" data-tip="Ep 711: Medical Electronics Education with Mark Palmeri (December 21, 2025)" title="Ep 711: Medical Electronics Education with Mark Palmeri (December 21, 2025)">[711]</a></sup>

Fabrication is the blocking failure mode for control applications: the model invents events and states that never occurred and treats them as legitimate.<sup><a href="#ref-654" data-tip="Ep 654: Pseudo Code...Pseudo Good (December 18, 2023)" title="Ep 654: Pseudo Code...Pseudo Good (December 18, 2023)">[654]</a></sup> In a home automation stack, device control sits below a scheduling layer that handles priorities and external inputs, and any language-model layer belongs above that scheduler rather than in the control path.<sup><a href="#ref-654" data-tip="Ep 654: Pseudo Code...Pseudo Good (December 18, 2023)" title="Ep 654: Pseudo Code...Pseudo Good (December 18, 2023)">[654]</a></sup>

Media tooling shows similar limits. Tools that claim to cut long-form video into viral short-form clips produced no usable output across repeated trials, a zero percent hit rate; the clips are correctly formatted but lack the editorial quality that makes a short work.<sup><a href="#ref-668" data-tip="Ep 668: 50.0000 Ohms (May 30, 2024)" title="Ep 668: 50.0000 Ohms (May 30, 2024)">[668]</a></sup> Voice cloning trained on a speaker's own recordings, including a guided online session reading prescribed words, produced unusable results across three or four different services.<sup><a href="#ref-677" data-tip="Ep 677: Watt Is The Deal (September 23, 2024)" title="Ep 677: Watt Is The Deal (September 23, 2024)">[677]</a></sup> Image generators reconstruct a subject rather than reusing the supplied photographs, and results degrade badly unless the input is a pristine photograph of a single person; human faces remain the weakest case.<sup><a href="#ref-613" data-tip="Ep 613: It's a Keyzermas Miracle! ## Defensible applications

Generated illustration has been used in place of commissioned artwork on the basis that the output carries no royalty obligation, although the legal position on such licensing remains contested.<sup><a href="#ref-613" data-tip="Ep 613: It's a Keyzermas Miracle! Converting an existing data centre to inference and training workloads required an entire additional building section devoted to power, on roughly the same computing floor area, with new trenched feeders and external transformers installed to supply it.<sup><a href="#ref-724" data-tip="Ep 724: All Heat, No Useful Work (May 25, 2026)" title="Ep 724: All Heat, No Useful Work (May 25, 2026)">[724]</a></sup> The additions are dominated by cooling plant, power distribution and diesel generators rather than by extra server floor space, reflecting an order-of-magnitude rise in power density.<sup><a href="#ref-724" data-tip="Ep 724: All Heat, No Useful Work (May 25, 2026)" title="Ep 724: All Heat, No Useful Work (May 25, 2026)">[724]</a></sup>
