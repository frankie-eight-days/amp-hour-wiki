Test equipment is any instrument used to measure other equipment, a definition broad enough to include inexpensive educational instruments when they are used to debug hardware.<sup><a href="#ref-87" data-tip="Ep 87: An Interview with Ian Daniher - ## Industry structure

Hewlett-Packard's founding product, made in the Palo Alto garage that anchors the Silicon Valley startup story, was a piece of test equipment rather than a computing product.<sup><a href="#ref-369" data-tip="Ep 369: An Interview with Jason Huggins (November 26, 2017)" title="Ep 369: An Interview with Jason Huggins (November 26, 2017)">[369]</a></sup> When the company split in 1999 the computer and printer business retained the HP name and the test and measurement business was renamed Agilent, so instruments carrying decades of brand equity had to be rebranded.<sup><a href="#ref-3" data-tip="Ep 3: HP, IEEE, and Human Interface" title="Ep 3: HP, IEEE, and Human Interface">[3]</a></sup> Agilent later separated its electronic measurement business, which became Keysight, following from test and measurement being a lower-margin segment than its rapidly growing life sciences division.<sup><a href="#ref-164" data-tip="Ep 164: Agilent's New Name, Molex's New Owner and PCB artwork - Nonsensical Naming Neolatry (September 23, 2013)" title="Ep 164: Agilent's New Name, Molex's New Owner and PCB artwork - Nonsensical Naming Neolatry (September 23, 2013)">[164]</a></sup> A standalone test and measurement company can serve instrument users differently from a division inside a conglomerate, because its chief executive's attention is on measurement rather than on a larger unrelated business.<sup><a href="#ref-164" data-tip="Ep 164: Agilent's New Name, Molex's New Owner and PCB artwork - Nonsensical Naming Neolatry (September 23, 2013)" title="Ep 164: Agilent's New Name, Molex's New Owner and PCB artwork - Nonsensical Naming Neolatry (September 23, 2013)">[164]</a></sup> Tektronix in its classic era was vertically integrated to an unusual degree, manufacturing its own cathode ray tubes and supplying the silver solder needed to work on the ceramic terminal strips inside its instruments.<sup><a href="#ref-421" data-tip="Ep 421: The Legend of Keyzermas (December 23, 2018)" title="Ep 421: The Legend of Keyzermas (December 23, 2018)">[421]</a></sup>

Incumbent instrument makers repeat a pattern in which they retreat toward ever more expensive high-margin products and abandon the low end, leaving that segment to cheaper entrants.<sup><a href="#ref-134" data-tip="Ep 134: Intel, EPA & Brown Field - Google's Ground Gurgitation (February 25, 2013)" title="Ep 134: Intel, EPA & Brown Field - Google's Ground Gurgitation (February 25, 2013)">[134]</a></sup> Deliberate feature withholding sustains the tiering: performance that would cannibalise a flagship line is kept out of lower tiers, which receive secondary conveniences instead.<sup><a href="#ref-127" data-tip="Ep 127: FPGA, Xess, 32 Bit - Quirky Qualitative Questions (January 7, 2013)" title="Ep 127: FPGA, Xess, 32 Bit - Quirky Qualitative Questions (January 7, 2013)">[127]</a></sup> Entering the low end is correspondingly difficult, because a new product must ship with a constrained feature set while competing against established Chinese manufacturers who have already reduced the function to an ASIC.<sup><a href="#ref-554" data-tip="Ep 554: PLEASE be a die shrink (August 15, 2021)" title="Ep 554: PLEASE be a die shrink (August 15, 2021)">[554]</a></sup> Good Will Instek is a Taiwanese instrument maker whose mid-range oscilloscopes and function generators are sold under the Goodwill or Instek brand depending on the country of sale; such makers found themselves undercut by as much as 50 percent by mainland Chinese competitors selling copied products, despite already having a low-cost manufacturing base.<sup><a href="#ref-57" data-tip="Ep 57: An Interview with Alan Yates - Recondite Radiation Raconteur" title="Ep 57: An Interview with Alan Yates - Recondite Radiation Raconteur">[57]</a></sup> Hameg is a European brand with little presence in the United States but a near-leading position in Europe, particularly in education and in low-cost affordable instruments.<sup><a href="#ref-205" data-tip="Ep 205: Solar Factories and HVDC Lines - Pollent Power Pushing (June 30, 2014)" title="Ep 205: Solar Factories and HVDC Lines - Pollent Power Pushing (June 30, 2014)">[205]</a></sup> Entire categories of instruments are made and sold only inside domestic markets such as China and South Korea, including meters that cannot be bought elsewhere.<sup><a href="#ref-593" data-tip="Ep 593: Publicly Traded Hobby with Ben Jordan (June 14, 2022)" title="Ep 593: Publicly Traded Hobby with Ben Jordan (June 14, 2022)">[593]</a></sup>

Where the value sits inside an instrument has shifted. In a modern logic analyser it lies in the software rather than the FPGA and front-end silicon, which is why a vendor differentiating on user interface can move up market and raise prices.<sup><a href="#ref-554" data-tip="Ep 554: PLEASE be a die shrink (August 15, 2021)" title="Ep 554: PLEASE be a die shrink (August 15, 2021)">[554]</a></sup> Hardware security and protocol testing has likewise moved from proprietary add-on modules bought for oscilloscopes to open-source tools with Python interfaces.<sup><a href="#ref-442" data-tip="Ep 442: An Interview with Travis Goodspeed (May 12, 2019)" title="Ep 442: An Interview with Travis Goodspeed (May 12, 2019)">[442]</a></sup>

## Capability, bandwidth and price

The cost of an instrument is usually set by the frequency range required rather than by any general need for the most expensive model, so specifying the frequency span honestly is the main lever on price.<sup><a href="#ref-470" data-tip="Ep 470: Just Add Salt (December 8, 2019)" title="Ep 470: Just Add Salt (December 8, 2019)">[470]</a></sup> Contrary to the assumption that rising signal speeds would push everyone upward, the bulk of instrument users remain below 100 MHz while a smaller group needs six-and-a-half, seven-and-a-half and eight-and-a-half digit multimeters, so vendors serve a broadening base and a rising top end at once.<sup><a href="#ref-199" data-tip="Ep 199: The 2014 Maker Faire Show - Traveling Technology Trangam (May 19, 2014)" title="Ep 199: The 2014 Maker Faire Show - Traveling Technology Trangam (May 19, 2014)">[199]</a></sup> Measurement above roughly 3.2 GHz sits beyond the price most engineers will pay, which confines characterisation at those frequencies to laboratories holding vector network analysers.<sup><a href="#ref-474" data-tip="Ep 474: An Interview with Nash Reilly (January 12, 2020)" title="Ep 474: An Interview with Nash Reilly (January 12, 2020)">[474]</a></sup>

At the low end, reaching 50 MHz of analog bandwidth with off-the-shelf parts is difficult at a low price point, and this is the central engineering constraint on inexpensive USB and student instruments; later work in that segment targeted 250 MHz of analog bandwidth while holding roughly the same price bracket.<sup><a href="#ref-302" data-tip="Ep 302: An Interview with Clint Cole of Digilent (June 8, 2016)" title="Ep 302: An Interview with Clint Cole of Digilent (June 8, 2016)">[302]</a></sup> A survey of three decades of electronics magazine advertising shows the entry-level oscilloscope price point holding steady at roughly 700 to 800 dollars, a level maintained so that cheap models do not undercut the tiers above.<sup><a href="#ref-186" data-tip="Ep 186: Someone is watching...we think - Horme Hostility Hypochondriac (February 25, 2014)" title="Ep 186: Someone is watching...we think - Horme Hostility Hypochondriac (February 25, 2014)">[186]</a></sup> Even so, the market changed sharply over the late 2000s and early 2010s, to the point where a 500-dollar instrument offers capability that previously required far more expensive equipment.<sup><a href="#ref-199" data-tip="Ep 199: The 2014 Maker Faire Show - Traveling Technology Trangam (May 19, 2014)" title="Ep 199: The 2014 Maker Faire Show - Traveling Technology Trangam (May 19, 2014)">[199]</a></sup>

Headline specifications can mislead, because performance is limited by the weakest subsystem: an expensive high-bandwidth front end delivers little if the trigger system has excessive jitter, since rare events cannot then be reliably captured.<sup><a href="#ref-178" data-tip="Ep 178: A 2013 Recap - Year-end Yarn Yakking (December 30, 2013)" title="Ep 178: A 2013 Recap - Year-end Yarn Yakking (December 30, 2013)">[178]</a></sup> High-end instruments are built in low volume and therefore receive far less user-interface engineering than mass-market low-cost instruments, whose large user base generates constant usability feedback.<sup><a href="#ref-104" data-tip="Ep 104: Ceramic capacitors & High end scopes - Kempt Kickstarter Kakorrhaphiophobia (July 15, 2012)" title="Ep 104: Ceramic capacitors & High end scopes - Kempt Kickstarter Kakorrhaphiophobia (July 15, 2012)">[104]</a></sup> The feature set of a flagship follows from its purpose: a high-end oscilloscope is designed primarily for automated compliance analysis of serial standards such as SATA rather than simply displaying a waveform, which explains both its capabilities and its interface complexity.<sup><a href="#ref-104" data-tip="Ep 104: Ceramic capacitors & High end scopes - Kempt Kickstarter Kakorrhaphiophobia (July 15, 2012)" title="Ep 104: Ceramic capacitors & High end scopes - Kempt Kickstarter Kakorrhaphiophobia (July 15, 2012)">[104]</a></sup> Instrument user interfaces have historically lagged behind other product categories, which is part of why engineers still favour physical knobs over menu-driven front panels.<sup><a href="#ref-382" data-tip="Ep 382: The Toggle Boggle (March 4, 2018)" title="Ep 382: The Toggle Boggle (March 4, 2018)">[382]</a></sup>

Precision instruments carry their specifications in their component selection. A single precision resistor can cost four dollars in volume against roughly twelve dollars at single-piece price, so unit economics at low volume consume most of the margin.<sup><a href="#ref-554" data-tip="Ep 554: PLEASE be a die shrink (August 15, 2021)" title="Ep 554: PLEASE be a die shrink (August 15, 2021)">[554]</a></sup> Designers of high-end instruments routinely reject integrated single-chip signal-chain solutions in favour of discrete implementations, because instrument specifications are hard requirements that a general-purpose integrated part cannot be trimmed to meet.<sup><a href="#ref-348" data-tip="Ep 348: An Interview with Art Kay (June 18, 2017)" title="Ep 348: An Interview with Art Kay (June 18, 2017)">[348]</a></sup> Open-source precision designs are frequently rebuilt with lower-precision components, and the resulting units do not meet the original specification even though the schematic is identical.<sup><a href="#ref-554" data-tip="Ep 554: PLEASE be a die shrink (August 15, 2021)" title="Ep 554: PLEASE be a die shrink (August 15, 2021)">[554]</a></sup> Selling to a stated specification requires custom-built test gear to verify each individual unit, and traceability back to a national standard adds further cost.<sup><a href="#ref-554" data-tip="Ep 554: PLEASE be a die shrink (August 15, 2021)" title="Ep 554: PLEASE be a die shrink (August 15, 2021)">[554]</a></sup>

## Buying, renting and the used market

Instruments needed only occasionally are better rented than bought, and companies developing high-speed products often find test equipment consuming a large share of the research and development budget.<sup><a href="#ref-104" data-tip="Ep 104: Ceramic capacitors & High end scopes - Kempt Kickstarter Kakorrhaphiophobia (July 15, 2012)" title="Ep 104: Ceramic capacitors & High end scopes - Kempt Kickstarter Kakorrhaphiophobia (July 15, 2012)">[104]</a></sup> The same reasoning applies to a large modern oscilloscope wanted for a single project.<sup><a href="#ref-613" data-tip="Ep 613: It's a Keyzermas Miracle! (December 18, 2022)" title="Ep 613: It's a Keyzermas Miracle! (December 18, 2022)">[613]</a></sup> Well-equipped labs typically contain many instruments used only a small fraction of the time, which is the standing argument for weighing occasional-use purchases against rental or borrowing.<sup><a href="#ref-216" data-tip="Ep 216: Last Minute Decisions - Obdurate Onepercenter Obstacles (September 15, 2014)" title="Ep 216: Last Minute Decisions - Obdurate Onepercenter Obstacles (September 15, 2014)">[216]</a></sup> Rental has its own failure mode: a unit taken for a few days stays on the books for years, because nobody revisits the arrangement once the immediate need has passed.<sup><a href="#ref-104" data-tip="Ep 104: Ceramic capacitors & High end scopes - Kempt Kickstarter Kakorrhaphiophobia (July 15, 2012)" title="Ep 104: Ceramic capacitors & High end scopes - Kempt Kickstarter Kakorrhaphiophobia (July 15, 2012)">[104]</a></sup> In smaller markets distributors carry little or no stock of obscure or high-end instruments and their demonstration loan units are often already out with another customer, which forces buyers into rental in the first place.<sup><a href="#ref-104" data-tip="Ep 104: Ceramic capacitors & High end scopes - Kempt Kickstarter Kakorrhaphiophobia (July 15, 2012)" title="Ep 104: Ceramic capacitors & High end scopes - Kempt Kickstarter Kakorrhaphiophobia (July 15, 2012)">[104]</a></sup>

Purchases are best tied to a specific project, since buying instruments can become a substitute for building anything with them.<sup><a href="#ref-328" data-tip="Ep 328: The Ghost of Keyzermas Past (December 21, 2016)" title="Ep 328: The Ghost of Keyzermas Past (December 21, 2016)">[328]</a></sup> Buying a multi-thousand-dollar instrument is reasonably deferred until a job actually requires it, given how much constrained low-cost tools cover.<sup><a href="#ref-199" data-tip="Ep 199: The 2014 Maker Faire Show - Traveling Technology Trangam (May 19, 2014)" title="Ep 199: The 2014 Maker Faire Show - Traveling Technology Trangam (May 19, 2014)">[199]</a></sup> Against that, persisting with an inadequate low-end instrument can consume far more effort than reaching for the right one at the outset, even though the struggle has some teaching value.<sup><a href="#ref-203" data-tip="Ep 203: Tesla, Checklists and Bullies - Emerging External Eupsychics (June 16, 2014)" title="Ep 203: Tesla, Checklists and Bullies - Emerging External Eupsychics (June 16, 2014)">[203]</a></sup> Non-recurring engineering costs such as tooling and test equipment are significant on a hardware programme, but staff cost usually dominates, which is why firms under pressure shed people before they shed instruments.<sup><a href="#ref-104" data-tip="Ep 104: Ceramic capacitors & High end scopes - Kempt Kickstarter Kakorrhaphiophobia (July 15, 2012)" title="Ep 104: Ceramic capacitors & High end scopes - Kempt Kickstarter Kakorrhaphiophobia (July 15, 2012)">[104]</a></sup>

### Used and surplus

Access to surplus is strongly regional: the United States has a deep surplus market where a lab of nominally high list-price instruments can be assembled for a small fraction of that, while Australia has almost no equivalent supply.<sup><a href="#ref-22" data-tip="Ep 22: The Hard Work Hypothesis (December 21, 2010)" title="Ep 22: The Hard Work Hypothesis (December 21, 2010)">[22]</a></sup> The regional gap sustains a durable arbitrage, since buyers in some countries will not risk overseas purchases; an importer who takes that risk, repairs and cleans the unit and resells locally can realise around three times the purchase price.<sup><a href="#ref-490" data-tip="Ep 490: An Interview with Ben Heck(endorn) (April 27, 2020)" title="Ep 490: An Interview with Ben Heck(endorn) (April 27, 2020)">[490]</a></sup> It also imposes costs, with imported faulty and used instruments carrying shipping charges that can reach around 500 dollars per unit, which changes what is worth repairing.<sup><a href="#ref-643" data-tip="Ep 643: Calibration & Repair with Ian Johnston (August 22, 2023)" title="Ep 643: Calibration & Repair with Ian Johnston (August 22, 2023)">[643]</a></sup> A related hobbyist pattern is to buy instruments cheaply on eBay, refurbish them and resell, cycling through a large number of units over the years.<sup><a href="#ref-18" data-tip="Ep 18: Transistor Types and Where To Get Electronic Gear" title="Ep 18: Transistor Types and Where To Get Electronic Gear">[18]</a></sup>

Surplus dealers differ in what they stock: some are large undifferentiated piles of mixed junk, while smaller curated dealers concentrate on industrial gear and instruments that stand a reasonable chance of still working.<sup><a href="#ref-81" data-tip="Ep 81: Jersey Jeff Jactitation (February 6, 2012)" title="Ep 81: Jersey Jeff Jactitation (February 6, 2012)">[81]</a></sup> All Electronics, a United States catalogue component supplier, also operates a walk-in retail store in Van Nuys, California.<sup><a href="#ref-81" data-tip="Ep 81: Jersey Jeff Jactitation (February 6, 2012)" title="Ep 81: Jersey Jeff Jactitation (February 6, 2012)">[81]</a></sup> When an established test and measurement service business closes, its warehouse stock of older instruments typically cannot be sold individually and is disposed of by the pallet load or scrapped as electronic waste; such liquidations release warehouses of equipment, and local enthusiasts strip the highest-value items first so that later buyers see picked-over stock.<sup><a href="#ref-727" data-tip="Ep 727: Boat Anchor Warehouse (July 1, 2026)" title="Ep 727: Boat Anchor Warehouse (July 1, 2026)">[727]</a></sup>

Screening a used unit rests on a few reliable tests. Because bench instruments carry comprehensive built-in self-test, a unit that powers up and passes self-test can be judged 80 to 90 percent likely to be usable.<sup><a href="#ref-449" data-tip="Ep 449: Pulled From A Working Environment (June 30, 2019)" title="Ep 449: Pulled From A Working Environment (June 30, 2019)">[449]</a></sup> A seller's claim that a unit was pulled from a working environment is unreliable when it comes from an individual seller, whereas instruments liquidated in bulk by an auction house after a lab or factory closure are near-certain to have been in service.<sup><a href="#ref-449" data-tip="Ep 449: Pulled From A Working Environment (June 30, 2019)" title="Ep 449: Pulled From A Working Environment (June 30, 2019)">[449]</a></sup> Refurbished dealers test what they sell and typically charge about double the price of an as-is private seller, which is the price of removing the risk that the unit is faulty.<sup><a href="#ref-449" data-tip="Ep 449: Pulled From A Working Environment (June 30, 2019)" title="Ep 449: Pulled From A Working Environment (June 30, 2019)">[449]</a></sup> Instruments bought from another mains region are often not switchable between supply voltages and require modification at the printed circuit board level, a hidden cost of importing.<sup><a href="#ref-419" data-tip="Ep 419: Feels over reals (December 9, 2018)" title="Ep 419: Feels over reals (December 9, 2018)">[419]</a></sup> Some vendors police the secondhand and clone market by searching eBay listings and filing reports to have them removed, which affects what used buyers can find.<sup><a href="#ref-273" data-tip="Ep 273: Part Choice Triathlon (October 28, 2015)" title="Ep 273: Part Choice Triathlon (October 28, 2015)">[273]</a></sup> Prices also respond to visibility: public attention to a particular vintage model raises its price on the used market.<sup><a href="#ref-613" data-tip="Ep 613: It's a Keyzermas Miracle! (December 18, 2022)" title="Ep 613: It's a Keyzermas Miracle! (December 18, 2022)">[613]</a></sup> Even faulty high-end instruments hold substantial value, with broken examples routinely selling for thousands of dollars, so repair-for-profit at the top of the market requires real capital.<sup><a href="#ref-470" data-tip="Ep 470: Just Add Salt (December 8, 2019)" title="Ep 470: Just Add Salt (December 8, 2019)">[470]</a></sup>

Some brands resell better than others. Stanford Research Systems instruments hold their value unusually well because the company still builds the same designs decades later, uses through-hole construction, and publishes service manuals, all of which keep old units repairable.<sup><a href="#ref-613" data-tip="Ep 613: It's a Keyzermas Miracle! (December 18, 2022)" title="Ep 613: It's a Keyzermas Miracle! (December 18, 2022)">[613]</a></sup>

## Composition of a bench

A typical beginner's progression runs from multimeters and a simple kit oscilloscope to a two-channel oscilloscope and a signal analyser, driven by work such as PWM debugging that a single-channel instrument cannot resolve.<sup><a href="#ref-278" data-tip="Ep 278: Our Second Callin Show(ish) (December 16, 2015)" title="Ep 278: Our Second Callin Show(ish) (December 16, 2015)">[278]</a></sup> Modern embedded development often needs only a debugger and a known-good board rather than a large setup, because contemporary instruments are small, comparatively inexpensive and very capable.<sup><a href="#ref-489" data-tip="Ep 489: An Interview with Jack Ganssle (2nd) (April 19, 2020)" title="Ep 489: An Interview with Jack Ganssle (2nd) (April 19, 2020)">[489]</a></sup> Paying for instruments whose problem is already solved in software, such as a logic analyser with a well-designed capture and decode interface, buys back engineering time even though the hardware itself is expensive.<sup><a href="#ref-373" data-tip="Ep 373: Pedantic or Andrantic (January 2, 2018)" title="Ep 373: Pedantic or Andrantic (January 2, 2018)">[373]</a></sup> Instrument design has trended toward consolidation, with mixed-domain products absorbing functions that were once separate boxes.<sup><a href="#ref-244" data-tip="Ep 244: The Art Of Staying Interested In Electronics - Exponible Electronics Ennui (April 7, 2015)" title="Ep 244: The Art Of Staying Interested In Electronics - Exponible Electronics Ennui (April 7, 2015)">[244]</a></sup> All-in-one instruments illustrate the price of that: National Instruments' VirtualBench combines several instruments in one box at a price around two thousand dollars, above the reach of most hobby and educational buyers, and at around 5,000 dollars targets academic benches that need adequate acquisition rather than standards-class accuracy and cannot house a full set of separate instruments.<sup><a href="#ref-242" data-tip="Ep 242: Can't We All Just Get Arduino? - Tardiloquent Trademark Tirade (March 24, 2015)" title="Ep 242: Can't We All Just Get Arduino? - Tardiloquent Trademark Tirade (March 24, 2015)">[242]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-354" data-tip="Ep 354: A Meeting Of The Davids (August 7, 2017)" title="Ep 354: A Meeting Of The Davids (August 7, 2017)">[354]</a></sup>

Engineers who travel frequently need instrumentation that is not heavy and bench-based, which is the design driver behind small USB-host-powered oscilloscopes and analysers.<sup><a href="#ref-302" data-tip="Ep 302: An Interview with Clint Cole of Digilent (June 8, 2016)" title="Ep 302: An Interview with Clint Cole of Digilent (June 8, 2016)">[302]</a></sup> Small self-contained instruments such as thermocouple data loggers now make continuous retroactive measurement of physical variables cheap, replacing the guesswork that previously accompanied intermittent faults.<sup><a href="#ref-442" data-tip="Ep 442: An Interview with Travis Goodspeed (May 12, 2019)" title="Ep 442: An Interview with Travis Goodspeed (May 12, 2019)">[442]</a></sup>

Physical arrangement matters on a working bench. A common convention places less frequently used instruments on higher shelves and keeps daily-use instruments at eye or hand level.<sup><a href="#ref-676" data-tip="Ep 676: Moving House (And Lab) (September 2, 2024)" title="Ep 676: Moving House (And Lab) (September 2, 2024)">[676]</a></sup> Suspending an oscilloscope beneath a shelf, alongside the bench lighting, recovers bench area in a small workspace while keeping the display in view, and mounting a source measure unit on a bracket at the back of the bench so its terminals sit at board level shortens the lead path to the device under test.<sup><a href="#ref-676" data-tip="Ep 676: Moving House (And Lab) (September 2, 2024)" title="Ep 676: Moving House (And Lab) (September 2, 2024)">[676]</a></sup> In shared lab space, marking an instrument with a prominent fault label is an effective way to stop it being borrowed and to keep a known-good reference available.<sup><a href="#ref-404" data-tip="Ep 404: Proof Of Blink (August 26, 2018)" title="Ep 404: Proof Of Blink (August 26, 2018)">[404]</a></sup> A working collection accumulates duplicates and project-specific instruments that outlive their purpose, and periodically moving those on keeps the bench usable.<sup><a href="#ref-655" data-tip="Ep 655: The Twelfth Day of Keyzermas (January 8, 2024)" title="Ep 655: The Twelfth Day of Keyzermas (January 8, 2024)">[655]</a></sup> Individual collections can nonetheless reach industrial scale: one documented inventory listed 88 multimeters, 38 oscilloscopes, 11 logic analysers and 58 function and pulse generators, catalogued down to counts of knobs, switches and BNC jacks.<sup><a href="#ref-688" data-tip="Ep 688: The Tandy Train (February 11, 2025)" title="Ep 688: The Tandy Train (February 11, 2025)">[688]</a></sup>

Practising RF engineering requires a large quantity of expensive instrumentation, which is a structural reason engineers in the field gravitate to employers who already own the gear rather than working from home labs; home RF benches tend to be assembled from vintage instruments whose frequency coverage and noise performance are inadequate for modern work on Bluetooth or Wi-Fi.<sup><a href="#ref-93" data-tip="Ep 93: An Interview with Tom LeMense - Cacaesthestic Chronometric Carriwitchet (April 29, 2012)" title="Ep 93: An Interview with Tom LeMense - Cacaesthestic Chronometric Carriwitchet (April 29, 2012)">[93]</a></sup> Work that depends on shared instrumentation argues for co-locating a team rather than distributing it, because the equipment cannot be duplicated at every remote desk.<sup><a href="#ref-101" data-tip="Ep 101: An Interview with Matt Ettus - Quality Quadrature Quidam (June 24, 2012)" title="Ep 101: An Interview with Matt Ettus - Quality Quadrature Quidam (June 24, 2012)">[101]</a></sup> Engineers who produce public measurement content while employed by a research laboratory can keep the two strictly separate by owning all the instruments used for the public work.<sup><a href="#ref-228" data-tip="Ep 228: An Interview with Shahriar from The Signal Path - Quisquous Quivering Quadripole (December 16, 2014)" title="Ep 228: An Interview with Shahriar from The Signal Path - Quisquous Quivering Quadripole (December 16, 2014)">[228]</a></sup>

## Calibration and upkeep

Calibration laboratories must hold environmental conditions inside defined limits to issue certification, typically a temperature window of about 5 degrees Celsius and relative humidity in the region of 30 to 80 percent.<sup><a href="#ref-132" data-tip="Ep 132: Melbourne, Hackerspace & Calibration - Vacuuous Vortex Verification (February 11, 2013)" title="Ep 132: Melbourne, Hackerspace & Calibration - Vacuuous Vortex Verification (February 11, 2013)">[132]</a></sup> Environment of use is itself a factor in condition and drift: instruments returned for calibration from harsh industrial sites are often found coated internally with contamination.<sup><a href="#ref-132" data-tip="Ep 132: Melbourne, Hackerspace & Calibration - Vacuuous Vortex Verification (February 11, 2013)" title="Ep 132: Melbourne, Hackerspace & Calibration - Vacuuous Vortex Verification (February 11, 2013)">[132]</a></sup> Stored instruments should be powered up periodically, because equipment left unused for long stretches is more likely to fail when it is finally switched on.<sup><a href="#ref-688" data-tip="Ep 688: The Tandy Train (February 11, 2025)" title="Ep 688: The Tandy Train (February 11, 2025)">[688]</a></sup> Owning a second identical instrument gives a benchmark to compare against while modifying or repairing the first, which is the practical way to confirm that a change improved rather than degraded performance.<sup><a href="#ref-643" data-tip="Ep 643: Calibration & Repair with Ian Johnston (August 22, 2023)" title="Ep 643: Calibration & Repair with Ian Johnston (August 22, 2023)">[643]</a></sup>

## Repair and vintage instruments

Repairing test equipment is an effective way to learn both electronics design and instrument design, because earlier equipment solves the same measurement problems from a different angle than a modern instrument that hides its method behind software.<sup><a href="#ref-655" data-tip="Ep 655: The Twelfth Day of Keyzermas (January 8, 2024)" title="Ep 655: The Twelfth Day of Keyzermas (January 8, 2024)">[655]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-613" data-tip="Ep 613: It's a Keyzermas Miracle! (December 18, 2022)" title="Ep 613: It's a Keyzermas Miracle! (December 18, 2022)">[613]</a></sup> Instruments from the 1960s and 1970s were simpler in construction than modern equivalents, so opening one exposes the design decisions and manufacturing process in a way a current instrument does not.<sup><a href="#ref-421" data-tip="Ep 421: The Legend of Keyzermas (December 23, 2018)" title="Ep 421: The Legend of Keyzermas (December 23, 2018)">[421]</a></sup> Studying category-defining instruments reveals the measurement philosophy behind them; the HP 8753 occupies that position among comparatively low-cost vector network analysers.<sup><a href="#ref-613" data-tip="Ep 613: It's a Keyzermas Miracle! (December 18, 2022)" title="Ep 613: It's a Keyzermas Miracle! (December 18, 2022)">[613]</a></sup>

The work has become genuinely hard, because instruments now around forty years old have no factory parts support left, a much larger technology gap than earlier restoration work spanned.<sup><a href="#ref-655" data-tip="Ep 655: The Twelfth Day of Keyzermas (January 8, 2024)" title="Ep 655: The Twelfth Day of Keyzermas (January 8, 2024)">[655]</a></sup> When a repair requires fabricating an unobtainable part, cutting a batch of ten to thirty costs little more than cutting one on a laser cutter, and the surplus can be sold to other restorers.<sup><a href="#ref-655" data-tip="Ep 655: The Twelfth Day of Keyzermas (January 8, 2024)" title="Ep 655: The Twelfth Day of Keyzermas (January 8, 2024)">[655]</a></sup> Routine tasks include replacing the perished foam filter behind the rear cooling fan of a vintage oscilloscope, which keeps dust, dirt and hair out of the instrument.<sup><a href="#ref-655" data-tip="Ep 655: The Twelfth Day of Keyzermas (January 8, 2024)" title="Ep 655: The Twelfth Day of Keyzermas (January 8, 2024)">[655]</a></sup> The TekScopes groups.io mailing list is the principal community for vintage Tektronix repair and is notably more active than the equivalent HP and Agilent list, and the Tektronix community also maintains a museum for its vintage equipment.<sup><a href="#ref-655" data-tip="Ep 655: The Twelfth Day of Keyzermas (January 8, 2024)" title="Ep 655: The Twelfth Day of Keyzermas (January 8, 2024)">[655]</a></sup> A first oscilloscope should be a known-working unit, even a low-end one, because repairing a second, broken instrument requires a trusted reference to measure against.<sup><a href="#ref-449" data-tip="Ep 449: Pulled From A Working Environment (June 30, 2019)" title="Ep 449: Pulled From A Working Environment (June 30, 2019)">[449]</a></sup>

Vintage instruments carry their own conventions. Equipment predating the 1960s is marked in kilocycles and megacycles rather than kilohertz and megahertz, which can confuse users.<sup><a href="#ref-407" data-tip="Ep 407: Gregory Charvat and Three New Companies (September 16, 2018)" title="Ep 407: Gregory Charvat and Three New Companies (September 16, 2018)">[407]</a></sup> Analog circuit designers of the 1960s and 1970s worked without simulators, computers or even pocket calculators, relying on slide rules and on far more limited instrumentation than is now routine.<sup><a href="#ref-47" data-tip="Ep 47: Apple HQ and Vintage Arcade Games - The Mothership Manifesto (June 15, 2011)" title="Ep 47: Apple HQ and Vintage Arcade Games - The Mothership Manifesto (June 15, 2011)">[47]</a></sup>

## Production and factory test

In a mature production line the marginal cost of testing can approach zero once the automated test equipment is fully depreciated, at which point line uptime rather than test cost governs the economics.<sup><a href="#ref-71" data-tip="Ep 71: An Interview with John Edmond - Luciferous LED Lucubrator" title="Ep 71: An Interview with John Edmond - Luciferous LED Lucubrator">[71]</a></sup> Throughput is capped by the number of test stands available, since each unit occupies a stand for the duration of its test, which is the economic argument for built-in self-test.<sup><a href="#ref-393" data-tip="Ep 393: I've bitten myself (May 20, 2018)" title="Ep 393: I've bitten myself (May 20, 2018)">[393]</a></sup> High-speed production test equipment is often designed in-house to test subassemblies on the factory floor before final assembly, as with audio modules verified before being fitted into greeting cards.<sup><a href="#ref-424" data-tip="Ep 424: An Interview with Julia Truchsess (January 6, 2019)" title="Ep 424: An Interview with Julia Truchsess (January 6, 2019)">[424]</a></sup> Calibration and functional test equipment is not part of a normal contract manufacturer's capability, so a product requiring per-unit calibration obliges its designer to supply and maintain that equipment at the CM.<sup><a href="#ref-607" data-tip="Ep 607: The Joulescope Upgrade with Matt Liberty (October 30, 2022)" title="Ep 607: The Joulescope Upgrade with Matt Liberty (October 30, 2022)">[607]</a></sup>

Production test systems commonly remain on obsolete operating systems such as Windows 3.11 or DOS for decades, because the equipment still produces working product and there is no forcing reason to revalidate a new platform.<sup><a href="#ref-263" data-tip="Ep 263: An Interview with Fran Blanche (August 19, 2015)" title="Ep 263: An Interview with Fran Blanche (August 19, 2015)">[263]</a></sup> Knowledge of how the gear works and how to maintain it often ends up concentrated in a single employee, which is a maintenance risk for the organisation.<sup><a href="#ref-108" data-tip="Ep 108: Mars, Makerbot & Power Outages - Reprobate Replicator Replication (August 12, 2012)" title="Ep 108: Mars, Makerbot & Power Outages - Reprobate Replicator Replication (August 12, 2012)">[108]</a></sup> Experienced production technicians carry empirical fault knowledge about specific boards, so consulting them before building simulations saves time that theorising alone will waste.<sup><a href="#ref-108" data-tip="Ep 108: Mars, Makerbot & Power Outages - Reprobate Replicator Replication (August 12, 2012)" title="Ep 108: Mars, Makerbot & Power Outages - Reprobate Replicator Replication (August 12, 2012)">[108]</a></sup>

Analysing the resulting data has its own discipline. Grouping production measurement data by fixture, chamber or operator exposes systematic offsets, such as one chamber consistently reading higher, that aggregate pass or fail rates would never reveal.<sup><a href="#ref-328" data-tip="Ep 328: The Ghost of Keyzermas Past (December 21, 2016)" title="Ep 328: The Ghost of Keyzermas Past (December 21, 2016)">[328]</a></sup> Gauge repeatability and reproducibility studies are worth doing religiously on factory fixtures as process control, but must not replace first checking that the equipment is connected, in calibration and physically sound, or weeks of data collection will be wasted.<sup><a href="#ref-328" data-tip="Ep 328: The Ghost of Keyzermas Past (December 21, 2016)" title="Ep 328: The Ghost of Keyzermas Past (December 21, 2016)">[328]</a></sup> Test equipment is also a determinant of part lifetime upstream: semiconductor parts are usually obsoleted only when something physical forces it, such as a fab closing or the production test equipment for that part becoming unusable, at which point customers are offered a last-time buy.<sup><a href="#ref-270" data-tip="Ep 270: An Interview With Dafydd Roche (October 7, 2015)" title="Ep 270: An Interview With Dafydd Roche (October 7, 2015)">[270]</a></sup>

Independent product testing at credible scale requires major capital infrastructure, including RF anechoic chambers, acoustic chambers and dedicated power supply test benches, plus engineers hired specifically to run them.<sup><a href="#ref-569" data-tip="Ep 569: Electric Fields, Son. (December 5, 2021)" title="Ep 569: Electric Fields, Son. (December 5, 2021)">[569]</a></sup>

## Building and automating instruments

Measurements for which no commercial instrument exists require building custom gear, as in a purpose-built drift counter used to track oscillator frequency drift over time; the absence of any commercial instrument capable of a measurement is a useful signal that the work has crossed from engineering into science.<sup><a href="#ref-127" data-tip="Ep 127: FPGA, Xess, 32 Bit - Quirky Qualitative Questions (January 7, 2013)" title="Ep 127: FPGA, Xess, 32 Bit - Quirky Qualitative Questions (January 7, 2013)">[127]</a></sup> Historically this was the normal case, and electronics magazines of earlier decades were filled with test equipment construction projects because affordable commercial instruments did not exist — a digital storage oscilloscope kit sold in the thousands simply because a ready-made DSO could not be bought.<sup><a href="#ref-229" data-tip="Ep 229: MightyHohm For The Holidays - Kaiser Keyzer's Kits (December 23, 2014)" title="Ep 229: MightyHohm For The Holidays - Kaiser Keyzer's Kits (December 23, 2014)">[229]</a></sup> That has reversed for common bench instruments: a conventional 30 volt 3 amp linear bench supply can be bought for around 50 dollars, leaving DIY justified only for niche requirements.<sup><a href="#ref-229" data-tip="Ep 229: MightyHohm For The Holidays - Kaiser Keyzer's Kits (December 23, 2014)" title="Ep 229: MightyHohm For The Holidays - Kaiser Keyzer's Kits (December 23, 2014)">[229]</a></sup> A standardised open hardware platform with interchangeable analog front ends can act as a general-purpose instrument chassis, letting analog designers contribute front ends while software work is shared.<sup><a href="#ref-198" data-tip="Ep 198: Mike Ossmann Returns! - Planetic Portalab Packaging (May 12, 2014)" title="Ep 198: Mike Ossmann Returns! - Planetic Portalab Packaging (May 12, 2014)">[198]</a></sup> Low-cost exploratory instruments intended for learning are not substitutes for production instrumentation, which must do things like curve-trace transistors coming off a line to confirm they meet specification.<sup><a href="#ref-87" data-tip="Ep 87: An Interview with Ian Daniher - Nascent Nonolith Numquid" title="Ep 87: An Interview with Ian Daniher - Nascent Nonolith Numquid">[87]</a></sup>

Regulation treats instruments as a special case: radio rules in the United States and across Europe contain exceptions for test equipment, which is how a broadband transmitter covering roughly 1 MHz to 6 GHz can legally be sold when it would not be permitted as a consumer device.<sup><a href="#ref-265" data-tip="Ep 265: A Security Update with Michael Ossmann (September 2, 2015)" title="Ep 265: A Security Update with Michael Ossmann (September 2, 2015)">[265]</a></sup>

Automation has become broadly available. Many mainstream instruments expose Python control libraries, including Rigol oscilloscopes and the Saleae logic analyser, which allows instrumentation to be driven from a continuous integration harness rather than by hand.<sup><a href="#ref-461" data-tip="Ep 461: An Interview with Jonathan Georgino (October 6, 2019)" title="Ep 461: An Interview with Jonathan Georgino (October 6, 2019)">[461]</a></sup> sigrok is an open-source driver layer providing a common interface to a very wide range of oscilloscopes, multimeters and logic analysers, including Bluetooth-connected handheld meters, with separate graphical front ends built on top of it.<sup><a href="#ref-665" data-tip="Ep 665: Really long needle nose pliers (April 24, 2024)" title="Ep 665: Really long needle nose pliers (April 24, 2024)">[665]</a></sup>

## Instruments and skill

Acquiring an oscilloscope is a turning point for a self-taught engineer because seeing signals in the time domain converts abstract circuit behaviour into something observable.<sup><a href="#ref-127" data-tip="Ep 127: FPGA, Xess, 32 Bit - Quirky Qualitative Questions (January 7, 2013)" title="Ep 127: FPGA, Xess, 32 Bit - Quirky Qualitative Questions (January 7, 2013)">[127]</a></sup> Hands-on use builds intuition and mental models that theory alone does not produce, because the operator can vary a parameter and immediately observe the effect.<sup><a href="#ref-470" data-tip="Ep 470: Just Add Salt (December 8, 2019)" title="Ep 470: Just Add Salt (December 8, 2019)">[470]</a></sup> Bringing an instrument into a lecture and showing the mathematics applied to a real measurement, such as an FFT displayed on a spectrum instrument, makes the theory concrete.<sup><a href="#ref-465" data-tip="Ep 465: An Interview with Ted Yapo (November 3, 2019)" title="Ep 465: An Interview with Ted Yapo (November 3, 2019)">[465]</a></sup> Being able to walk up to an unfamiliar instrument and navigate its menus is a distinct professional skill, complementary to having a small set of personally owned tools used for every job.<sup><a href="#ref-551" data-tip="Ep 551: Feed the Mouse (July 25, 2021)" title="Ep 551: Feed the Mouse (July 25, 2021)">[551]</a></sup>

Instruments also shape what work is possible on a given target. Ease of hardware modification depends heavily on whether a board exposes labelled test points; some game controller boards provide clearly labelled test pads while others route button contacts as carbon pads on a printed silkscreen circuit that offers nothing to probe or solder to, in which case a third-party equivalent product may be far more tractable.<sup><a href="#ref-490" data-tip="Ep 490: An Interview with Ben Heck(endorn) (April 27, 2020)" title="Ep 490: An Interview with Ben Heck(endorn) (April 27, 2020)">[490]</a></sup>

## The high-frequency extreme

At the top of the frequency range the instruments themselves become the limiting infrastructure. On-wafer probing extends to about 750 GHz in dedicated probe rooms, and probes for those frequencies use MEMS contactors that are fragile and can only be repaired by returning them to the manufacturer.<sup><a href="#ref-729" data-tip="Ep 729: The Terahertz Frontier with Greg Charvat of Teradar (July 22, 2026)" title="Ep 729: The Terahertz Frontier with Greg Charvat of Teradar (July 22, 2026)">[729]</a></sup> The most expensive instruments and probes tend to be the most fragile, because performance at extreme frequencies is achieved through delicate mechanical structures with no margin for mishandling.<sup><a href="#ref-729" data-tip="Ep 729: The Terahertz Frontier with Greg Charvat of Teradar (July 22, 2026)" title="Ep 729: The Terahertz Frontier with Greg Charvat of Teradar (July 22, 2026)">[729]</a></sup> At very high data rates small impedance discontinuities accumulate quickly and erode link performance, so installed high-speed infrastructure must be measured rather than assumed to meet the rate it was specified for.<sup><a href="#ref-533" data-tip="Ep 533: Microwave measurement with Joel Dunsmore (March 7, 2021)" title="Ep 533: Microwave measurement with Joel Dunsmore (March 7, 2021)">[533]</a></sup> The International Microwave Symposium carries a large industry exhibition showing state-of-the-art instruments from every manufacturer, whereas circuit and ASIC conferences have little physical hardware to demonstrate.<sup><a href="#ref-228" data-tip="Ep 228: An Interview with Shahriar from The Signal Path - Quisquous Quivering Quadripole (December 16, 2014)" title="Ep 228: An Interview with Shahriar from The Signal Path - Quisquous Quivering Quadripole (December 16, 2014)">[228]</a></sup>
