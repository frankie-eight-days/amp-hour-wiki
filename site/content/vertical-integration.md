**Vertical integration** is the ownership or direct control of multiple successive stages of a product's value chain by a single firm, with observed configurations spanning raw-material processing through component fabrication, assembly, and retail sale to the end user.<sup><a href="#ref-71" data-tip="Ep 71: An Interview with John Edmond - Luciferous LED Lucubrator" title="Ep 71: An Interview with John Edmond - Luciferous LED Lucubrator">[71]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-338" data-tip="Ep 338: An Interview with Jørgen Jakobsen (March 5, 2017)" title="Ep 338: An Interview with Jørgen Jakobsen (March 5, 2017)">[338]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-510" data-tip="Ep 510: Knob and Tube Wiring (September 28, 2020)" title="Ep 510: Knob and Tube Wiring (September 28, 2020)">[510]</a></sup> In electronics manufacturing the arrangement appears at scales ranging from a one-person product company operating a pick-and-place machine in a garage to corporations that peaked at hundreds of thousands of employees running their own semiconductor fabs.<sup><a href="#ref-169" data-tip="Ep 169: An Interview with Vincent Himpe - ## Forms and degrees

Integration is rarely absolute, and electronics firms occupy a range of intermediate positions. PCB service businesses tend to integrate downstream in stages, beginning with bare-board fabrication and only later adding assembly.<sup><a href="#ref-41" data-tip="Ep 41: Contests, Ham Radio & TWIT.tv - Ham, Spam, Thank You Ma'am (May 4, 2011)" title="Ep 41: Contests, Ham Radio & TWIT.tv - Ham, Spam, Thank You Ma'am (May 4, 2011)">[41]</a></sup> A common high-volume arrangement fabs bare boards offsite while co-locating surface-mount assembly and final assembly at one site, a structure adopted because board-level defects reliably surface on the final assembly line and engineers need to move physically between the two operations.<sup><a href="#ref-437" data-tip="Ep 437: An Interview with Chrissy Meyer (April 7, 2019)" title="Ep 437: An Interview with Chrissy Meyer (April 7, 2019)">[437]</a></sup> A single-site integrated operation typically combines machining with in-house electronics assembly, including its own pick-and-place line.<sup><a href="#ref-253" data-tip="Ep 253: Consolidate All The Things - Zonked Zelotic Zaitech (June 9, 2015)" title="Ep 253: Consolidate All The Things - Zonked Zelotic Zaitech (June 9, 2015)">[253]</a></sup> Campus-style integration extends this further, placing injection molding, camera module assembly, and cable build within a building or two of final assembly, a structure credited with efficiencies at firms such as DJI that are difficult to replicate elsewhere.<sup><a href="#ref-437" data-tip="Ep 437: An Interview with Chrissy Meyer (April 7, 2019)" title="Ep 437: An Interview with Chrissy Meyer (April 7, 2019)">[437]</a></sup>

At the fullest extent, a firm runs the chain end to end: Cree operated from raw powders and gases through to finished retail light fixtures.<sup><a href="#ref-71" data-tip="Ep 71: An Interview with John Edmond - Luciferous LED Lucubrator" title="Ep 71: An Interview with John Edmond - Luciferous LED Lucubrator">[71]</a></sup> The Starlink factory in Texas covers bare PCB fabrication, surface mount, functional test, injection molding, and CNC box build on one site, shipping roughly a thousand finished units per day.<sup><a href="#ref-699" data-tip="Ep 699: CircuitHub, 12 Years Later with Andrew Seddon (July 31, 2025)" title="Ep 699: CircuitHub, 12 Years Later with Andrew Seddon (July 31, 2025)">[699]</a></sup> Integration can also run forward to the customer: hearing-aid manufacturers have acquired the retail shops that dispense their devices, and a component maker integrated far enough downstream can end up consuming its own components internally rather than selling them on the merchant market.<sup><a href="#ref-71" data-tip="Ep 71: An Interview with John Edmond - Luciferous LED Lucubrator" title="Ep 71: An Interview with John Edmond - Luciferous LED Lucubrator">[71]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-338" data-tip="Ep 338: An Interview with Jørgen Jakobsen (March 5, 2017)" title="Ep 338: An Interview with Jørgen Jakobsen (March 5, 2017)">[338]</a></sup> Unless a firm owns the end-user installation step, customers will mix and match identified components as they see fit, which is one reason some products incorporate systems that detect and flag such mismatches.<sup><a href="#ref-266" data-tip="Ep 266: An Interview with Ronald Sousa of Hash Define Electronics (September 8, 2015)" title="Ep 266: An Interview with Ronald Sousa of Hash Define Electronics (September 8, 2015)">[266]</a></sup>

Backward integration reaches toward raw materials, with carmakers purchasing land or mines to secure lithium supply and some semiconductor startups founded explicitly on the premise of owning the entire process from raw silicon upward because the parts they need cannot be bought.<sup><a href="#ref-510" data-tip="Ep 510: Knob and Tube Wiring (September 28, 2020)" title="Ep 510: Knob and Tube Wiring (September 28, 2020)">[510]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-621" data-tip="Ep 621: The Magic of Calipers (February 26, 2023)" title="Ep 621: The Magic of Calipers (February 26, 2023)">[621]</a></sup> Partial forms are more common: a firm that designs its own chips without owning fabs remains dependent on a foundry's scheduling, and a dedicated in-house production line functions as a miniature form of integration in which parts stay on hand, feeders stay loaded, and the process is already proven.<sup><a href="#ref-255" data-tip="Ep 255: Inspirations and Aspirations - Recanting Rocket Rationale (June 24, 2015)" title="Ep 255: Inspirations and Aspirations - Recanting Rocket Rationale (June 24, 2015)">[255]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-559" data-tip="Ep 559: Occam's Engineering Razor (September 26, 2021)" title="Ep 559: Occam's Engineering Razor (September 26, 2021)">[559]</a></sup> Integration also exists on the software side: shipping a host computer alongside an instrument eliminates version-dependency failures on unknown customer machines, and commercial CAD suites are constructed as integrated stacks so the vendor can sell the entire solution, whereas open tools remain flat and separable.<sup><a href="#ref-448" data-tip="Ep 448: An Interview with Jean Rintoul (June 23, 2019)" title="Ep 448: An Interview with Jean Rintoul (June 23, 2019)">[448]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-713" data-tip="Ep 713: Rubber Duck Incarnate (January 25, 2026)" title="Ep 713: Rubber Duck Incarnate (January 25, 2026)">[713]</a></sup>

## Historical examples

Western Electric carried integration to the raw-material level, grinding its own coal for the carbon microphones in telephone handsets and fabricating its own integrated circuits; the culture was sufficiently in-house that a 1979 Bell Labs project had to reach outside for a Motorola 6801 microprocessor when the best available part was external.<sup><a href="#ref-232" data-tip="Ep 232: Impedance Matching&quot; with Davidson and Vandenbout - Presbytes Pushing Portfolios" title="Ep 232: Impedance Matching&quot; with Davidson and Vandenbout - Presbytes Pushing Portfolios">[232]</a></sup> Alcatel etched its own boards and wrote its own board-layout software rather than buying either, and at its peak employed 216,000 people, with subsidiaries producing its capacitors and resistors and its own semiconductor fabs.<sup><a href="#ref-169" data-tip="Ep 169: An Interview with Vincent Himpe - Escaped Electron Elocution (October 28, 2013)" title="Ep 169: An Interview with Vincent Himpe - Escaped Electron Elocution (October 28, 2013)">[169]</a></sup> Tektronix became integrated to the point that engineers could order custom desks built internally, an instance of integration extending past anything strategically useful.<sup><a href="#ref-510" data-tip="Ep 510: Knob and Tube Wiring (September 28, 2020)" title="Ep 510: Knob and Tube Wiring (September 28, 2020)">[510]</a></sup>

Cree's silicon carbide LED business was vertically integrated from its founding, and the firm extended forward into fixtures by acquiring Rude Lighting, a street-lighting company in Racine, Wisconsin employing 800 people building product in the United States.<sup><a href="#ref-71" data-tip="Ep 71: An Interview with John Edmond - Luciferous LED Lucubrator" title="Ep 71: An Interview with John Edmond - Luciferous LED Lucubrator">[71]</a></sup> Over roughly a decade, China shifted from being the place to set up a supply chain and build to a design and engineering base of integrated firms.<sup><a href="#ref-437" data-tip="Ep 437: An Interview with Chrissy Meyer (April 7, 2019)" title="Ep 437: An Interview with Chrissy Meyer (April 7, 2019)">[437]</a></sup> CircuitHub began as a software network layered on other companies' factories and only opened its own facility in 2017, using common off-the-shelf equipment wrapped in software, and built its integrated prototype and small-batch operation by importing semiconductor-fabrication process techniques into board assembly rather than copying conventional EMS practice.<sup><a href="#ref-699" data-tip="Ep 699: CircuitHub, 12 Years Later with Andrew Seddon (July 31, 2025)" title="Ep 699: CircuitHub, 12 Years Later with Andrew Seddon (July 31, 2025)">[699]</a></sup>

## Motivations and mechanisms

The accounting case for designing in-house silicon combines control with margin capture, though the depth of the commitment is tested by whether a firm owns the underlying instruction-set architecture rather than licensing it.<sup><a href="#ref-118" data-tip="Ep 118: Kickstarter, Open Source RC & Modelsource - Facinorous Financial Foulness (October 21, 2012)" title="Ep 118: Kickstarter, Open Source RC & Modelsource - Facinorous Financial Foulness (October 21, 2012)">[118]</a></sup> Cree's defensibility in silicon carbide LEDs came only partly from patents; owning the whole silicon carbide process was the barrier competitors could not replicate.<sup><a href="#ref-71" data-tip="Ep 71: An Interview with John Edmond - Luciferous LED Lucubrator" title="Ep 71: An Interview with John Edmond - Luciferous LED Lucubrator">[71]</a></sup> Manufacturing a critical subsystem such as battery packs internally conveys ownership of the underlying technology and its patents, not merely production capacity, and some organizations operate an in-house chip fab purely to keep intellectual property from leaving the building rather than for cost or capacity reasons.<sup><a href="#ref-205" data-tip="Ep 205: Solar Factories and HVDC Lines - Pollent Power Pushing (June 30, 2014)" title="Ep 205: Solar Factories and HVDC Lines - Pollent Power Pushing (June 30, 2014)">[205]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-559" data-tip="Ep 559: Occam's Engineering Razor (September 26, 2021)" title="Ep 559: Occam's Engineering Razor (September 26, 2021)">[559]</a></sup>

Owning production converts a supply problem into a manufacturing engineering problem of yield and quality, in exchange for removing delivery risk from outside vendors.<sup><a href="#ref-205" data-tip="Ep 205: Solar Factories and HVDC Lines - Pollent Power Pushing (June 30, 2014)" title="Ep 205: Solar Factories and HVDC Lines - Pollent Power Pushing (June 30, 2014)">[205]</a></sup> Owning both the hardware and software organizations produces a better joint result than contracting either out, because the interface between them can be negotiated internally, and owning every layer removes much of the need for formal specifications and standards because interfaces can be changed without negotiating across organizations.<sup><a href="#ref-430" data-tip="Ep 430: Shahriar Discusses 5G (February 17, 2019)" title="Ep 430: Shahriar Discusses 5G (February 17, 2019)">[430]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-519" data-tip="Ep 519: Simulating Embedded Hardware with Michael Gielda (November 29, 2020)" title="Ep 519: Simulating Embedded Hardware with Michael Gielda (November 29, 2020)">[519]</a></sup> Specifying a submodule to a third party can be self-defeating when the technology moves faster than the spec, build, and test cycle, so the delivered part is already behind; a distributed firmware-update path likewise only works if every module in the chain implements the update mechanism, which is far easier to guarantee when one company owns all the modules.<sup><a href="#ref-518" data-tip="Ep 518: Satellites and EVs with Joris Aerts (November 22, 2020)" title="Ep 518: Satellites and EVs with Joris Aerts (November 22, 2020)">[518]</a></sup> An integrated manufacturer can change a design without renegotiating with an outside supplier, whereas contracts with third-party manufacturers often penalize changes outright.<sup><a href="#ref-546" data-tip="Ep 546: Thousands Of Dependencies (June 21, 2021)" title="Ep 546: Thousands Of Dependencies (June 21, 2021)">[546]</a></sup>

Linking a design tool to a factory allows manufacturing data to be pushed back into the layout, so issues such as heat shadowing from a capacitor placed too close to a BGA surface during design rather than at build.<sup><a href="#ref-545" data-tip="Ep 545: Fear of Banjos (June 6, 2021)" title="Ep 545: Fear of Banjos (June 6, 2021)">[545]</a></sup> Design improvements frequently originate from observations made on the manufacturing floor, so handing a product to an outside manufacturer degrades the design itself, and losing domestic prototyping capacity costs a country the ability to iterate products rapidly.<sup><a href="#ref-699" data-tip="Ep 699: CircuitHub, 12 Years Later with Andrew Seddon (July 31, 2025)" title="Ep 699: CircuitHub, 12 Years Later with Andrew Seddon (July 31, 2025)">[699]</a></sup> SpaceX treats how much of its parts it makes internally as a deliberate strategic choice and a point of pride, and its example shows that user-experience control cannot be the sole driver of walled-garden integration, since a launch-vehicle firm integrates just as hard with no consumer experience at stake.<sup><a href="#ref-255" data-tip="Ep 255: Inspirations and Aspirations - Recanting Rocket Rationale (June 24, 2015)" title="Ep 255: Inspirations and Aspirations - Recanting Rocket Rationale (June 24, 2015)">[255]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-518" data-tip="Ep 518: Satellites and EVs with Joris Aerts (November 22, 2020)" title="Ep 518: Satellites and EVs with Joris Aerts (November 22, 2020)">[518]</a></sup> Building a competitive programmable-logic product requires developing hardware, software, and architecture together, which effectively demands direct control of the whole stack.<sup><a href="#ref-535" data-tip="Ep 535: Efinix FPGAs with Sammy Cheung (March 21, 2021)" title="Ep 535: Efinix FPGAs with Sammy Cheung (March 21, 2021)">[535]</a></sup>

## Costs and failure modes

The supplier margin eliminated by owning a step was payment for that supplier solving problems and for the ability to shop around, and both disappear with the margin; the accounting case is deceptively simple because the saved payment to the outside vendor is visible while the absorbed costs are not.<sup><a href="#ref-510" data-tip="Ep 510: Knob and Tube Wiring (September 28, 2020)" title="Ep 510: Knob and Tube Wiring (September 28, 2020)">[510]</a></sup> Teardown cost estimates capture only chip prices, whereas the real cost of an integrated product includes assembly, transportation, and warehousing.<sup><a href="#ref-502" data-tip="Ep 502: Lowest Common Denominator Design (July 26, 2020)" title="Ep 502: Lowest Common Denominator Design (July 26, 2020)">[502]</a></sup> Full turnkey outsourcing hides recurring costs of its own, with the customer returning with more money each time parts go unsourceable or firmware nobody wrote in-house fails.<sup><a href="#ref-114" data-tip="Ep 114: Kickstarter, Manufacturing, Open Hardware - Judging Jurisdictional Junctures (September 23, 2012)" title="Ep 114: Kickstarter, Manufacturing, Open Hardware - Judging Jurisdictional Junctures (September 23, 2012)">[114]</a></sup>

Owning factories and staff is a cash-heavy operation with non-trivial carrying costs, which is why in-house manufacturing is abandoned first, though in safety-critical industries the cost of quality and returns can justify keeping it.<sup><a href="#ref-666" data-tip="Ep 666: Good Energy Citizen (May 8, 2024)" title="Ep 666: Good Energy Citizen (May 8, 2024)">[666]</a></sup> Owning production concentrates risk: a downturn in the firm's single industry leaves capacity idle, whereas a merchant module supplier spreads that exposure across markets.<sup><a href="#ref-518" data-tip="Ep 518: Satellites and EVs with Joris Aerts (November 22, 2020)" title="Ep 518: Satellites and EVs with Joris Aerts (November 22, 2020)">[518]</a></sup> Without owning the board house, compressing turnaround means paying substantial expedite fees, and board houses quote in working days while customers experience calendar days, so holidays silently stretch promised lead times; the prototype service PCB:NG standardized on a 12-calendar-day promise as the balance point between speed and price for assembled prototypes.<sup><a href="#ref-299" data-tip="Ep 299: An Interview with Jonathan Hirschman of PCB:NG (May 18, 2016)" title="Ep 299: An Interview with Jonathan Hirschman of PCB:NG (May 18, 2016)">[299]</a></sup> Outsourced aerospace programs slip schedule because requirements flow down through many subcontractors, whereas keeping the build in-house has protected timelines.<sup><a href="#ref-666" data-tip="Ep 666: Good Energy Citizen (May 8, 2024)" title="Ep 666: Good Energy Citizen (May 8, 2024)">[666]</a></sup>

Heavy integration drifts toward everything being custom, including custom alloys, and that steady move away from standard parts creates its own long-run problem; owning the whole product likewise encourages merging USB, video, and audio into one proprietary connector, saving connectors on the product but stranding users without standard cables.<sup><a href="#ref-510" data-tip="Ep 510: Knob and Tube Wiring (September 28, 2020)" title="Ep 510: Knob and Tube Wiring (September 28, 2020)">[510]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-548" data-tip="Ep 548: The Last Line of Defense (July 5, 2021)" title="Ep 548: The Last Line of Defense (July 5, 2021)">[548]</a></sup> A tightly optimized in-house flow, such as scripts that pull the bill of materials and program the pick-and-place machine directly, makes any deviation from the standard process an entire project rather than a small change.<sup><a href="#ref-453" data-tip="Ep 453: Vertically Integrated Design Engineering (August 4, 2019)" title="Ep 453: Vertically Integrated Design Engineering (August 4, 2019)">[453]</a></sup> An integrated IoT platform feels effortless because it hides every layer, but leaving the ecosystem means restarting the project from scratch.<sup><a href="#ref-526" data-tip="Ep 526: Why IoT Is Difficult with Jonathan Beri (January 18, 2021)" title="Ep 526: Why IoT Is Difficult with Jonathan Beri (January 18, 2021)">[526]</a></sup> Moving into custom silicon is unlike adding custom mechanical parts, because a new die must traverse the entire fabrication process rather than reusing tooling the way plastics and stamping do.<sup><a href="#ref-502" data-tip="Ep 502: Lowest Common Denominator Design (July 26, 2020)" title="Ep 502: Lowest Common Denominator Design (July 26, 2020)">[502]</a></sup> An in-house-only culture becomes a liability when the best available part is external, as the 1979 Bell Labs microprocessor selection demonstrated.<sup><a href="#ref-232" data-tip="Ep 232: Impedance Matching&quot; with Davidson and Vandenbout - Presbytes Pushing Portfolios" title="Ep 232: Impedance Matching&quot; with Davidson and Vandenbout - Presbytes Pushing Portfolios">[232]</a></sup> There is nonetheless value in keeping specific steps of the production line under a firm's own control rather than handing the entire product to an outside house.<sup><a href="#ref-114" data-tip="Ep 114: Kickstarter, Manufacturing, Open Hardware - Judging Jurisdictional Junctures (September 23, 2012)" title="Ep 114: Kickstarter, Manufacturing, Open Hardware - Judging Jurisdictional Junctures (September 23, 2012)">[114]</a></sup>

## Preconditions and limits

Bringing a step in-house requires enough revenue at the back end to fund the scale, which is why small companies cannot pursue integration even when it would help them technically.<sup><a href="#ref-518" data-tip="Ep 518: Satellites and EVs with Joris Aerts (November 22, 2020)" title="Ep 518: Satellites and EVs with Joris Aerts (November 22, 2020)">[518]</a></sup> Past a certain company size, making one's own components stops being a strategic decision and becomes the default outcome, and large firms genuinely evaluate acquiring a supplier as a remedy for an obsolete part because at multi-billion-dollar scale the purchase price is affordable relative to a redesign.<sup><a href="#ref-289" data-tip="Ep 289: Documentation Is A Waste Of Time (March 2, 2016)" title="Ep 289: Documentation Is A Waste Of Time (March 2, 2016)">[289]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-421" data-tip="Ep 421: The Legend of Keyzermas (December 23, 2018)" title="Ep 421: The Legend of Keyzermas (December 23, 2018)">[421]</a></sup> No realistic company spans silicon design through cloud software, so engineers necessarily depend on interfaces they do not understand internally.<sup><a href="#ref-615" data-tip="Ep 615: Augmented Engineering (January 16, 2023)" title="Ep 615: Augmented Engineering (January 16, 2023)">[615]</a></sup>

Tight size, weight, and power constraints make off-the-shelf components hard to fit, pushing robotics designs toward integrated in-house hardware, and a market gap exists between building everything in-house and buying off-the-shelf parts designed for other markets, which is where purpose-built component suppliers position themselves.<sup><a href="#ref-614" data-tip="Ep 614: Reunion Impedance Matching and 2023 Predictions (January 8, 2023)" title="Ep 614: Reunion Impedance Matching and 2023 Predictions (January 8, 2023)">[614]</a></sup> An integrated EDA data flow only works if every component vendor supplies data for it, and vendors selling into many tool ecosystems supply that data inconsistently or not at all.<sup><a href="#ref-393" data-tip="Ep 393: I've bitten myself (May 20, 2018)" title="Ep 393: I've bitten myself (May 20, 2018)">[393]</a></sup> Open-source tooling lets a small firm integrate its whole toolchain without owning the vendors, achieving the benefits of integration while others validate the code.<sup><a href="#ref-519" data-tip="Ep 519: Simulating Embedded Hardware with Michael Gielda (November 29, 2020)" title="Ep 519: Simulating Embedded Hardware with Michael Gielda (November 29, 2020)">[519]</a></sup> Domestic in-house manufacturing survives mainly in higher-margin niches such as telecom equipment, where the margin covers the cost of keeping the line at home.<sup><a href="#ref-279" data-tip="Ep 279: Merry Keyzermas! (December 22, 2015)" title="Ep 279: Merry Keyzermas! (December 22, 2015)">[279]</a></sup>

## Industry and regional structure

Owning manufacturing is close to a structural requirement in the automotive sector rather than a distinguishing strategy of any one carmaker, and carmakers have pushed integration back to raw materials with purchases of lithium-bearing land and mines.<sup><a href="#ref-510" data-tip="Ep 510: Knob and Tube Wiring (September 28, 2020)" title="Ep 510: Knob and Tube Wiring (September 28, 2020)">[510]</a></sup> Newer electric-vehicle makers converge on a small number of platform variants, differing mainly by battery stacking and motor sizing, which pairs naturally with owning the whole build.<sup><a href="#ref-662" data-tip="Ep 662: The non-Stinky Car (March 20, 2024)" title="Ep 662: The non-Stinky Car (March 20, 2024)">[662]</a></sup> Demand at specialty foundries is expected to grow from carmakers deciding they must own their silicon rather than from traditional chip companies consolidating into each other, while an analog chip company that outsources not just fabrication but its process technology to a foundry lowers operating budgets at the cost of surrendering the process advantage that constituted its moat.<sup><a href="#ref-559" data-tip="Ep 559: Occam's Engineering Razor (September 26, 2021)" title="Ep 559: Occam's Engineering Razor (September 26, 2021)">[559]</a></sup>

Chinese factories commonly hold SMT, injection molding, mold making, final assembly, test, and packaging under one roof, the largest structural difference from other regions.<sup><a href="#ref-451" data-tip="Ep 451: An Interview with Scott Miller (2nd) (July 21, 2019)" title="Ep 451: An Interview with Scott Miller (2nd) (July 21, 2019)">[451]</a></sup> The Shenzhen manufacturing model is a dense mesh of small, hyper-specialized firms partnering closely, in contrast to the American instinct to grow one firm that owns every step, and even the largest contract manufacturers subcontract work to small and medium specialist shops, so apparent integration at the top conceals a fragmented supplier base.<sup><a href="#ref-414" data-tip="Ep 414: An Interview with Scotty Allen (Strangeparts) (November 5, 2018)" title="Ep 414: An Interview with Scotty Allen (Strangeparts) (November 5, 2018)">[414]</a></sup> US and European manufacturing is typically horizontal, so a product may need one EMS for SMT, another for molding, and a third for assembly; the horizontal base costs more coordination effort but rewards firms that maintain databases of which factories work well together and already have payment terms in place.<sup><a href="#ref-451" data-tip="Ep 451: An Interview with Scott Miller (2nd) (July 21, 2019)" title="Ep 451: An Interview with Scott Miller (2nd) (July 21, 2019)">[451]</a></sup> Domestic volume manufacturing of a commodity consumer product remains viable with sustained automation and process optimization, as with a UK vacuum-cleaner maker whose product holds about half its home market, while much of the remaining electronics and mechanical shop base is owned by operators nearing retirement, a condition that has fed private-equity roll-ups of PCB fabrication and assembly capacity.<sup><a href="#ref-699" data-tip="Ep 699: CircuitHub, 12 Years Later with Andrew Seddon (July 31, 2025)" title="Ep 699: CircuitHub, 12 Years Later with Andrew Seddon (July 31, 2025)">[699]</a></sup>

A firm designing its own chips is chasing control and margin, but standardizing on a shared instruction-set architecture for cost reasons pushes the industry away from single-company silicon stacks toward parts that cross vendor boundaries.<sup><a href="#ref-118" data-tip="Ep 118: Kickstarter, Open Source RC & Modelsource - Facinorous Financial Foulness (October 21, 2012)" title="Ep 118: Kickstarter, Open Source RC & Modelsource - Facinorous Financial Foulness (October 21, 2012)">[118]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-534" data-tip="Ep 534: Firmware Update Capabilities (March 14, 2021)" title="Ep 534: Firmware Update Capabilities (March 14, 2021)">[534]</a></sup> If a chip company owned the EDA tools, competing silicon vendors would refuse to design in tools controlled by a rival, which limits how far up the value chain a component maker can integrate; an EDA vendor instead took an equity stake in a contract manufacturer after previously acquiring another, a common pattern in which investment provides visibility into the books ahead of a possible purchase.<sup><a href="#ref-545" data-tip="Ep 545: Fear of Banjos (June 6, 2021)" title="Ep 545: Fear of Banjos (June 6, 2021)">[545]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-659" data-tip="Ep 659: Altium...Acquired! (February 20, 2024)" title="Ep 659: Altium...Acquired! (February 20, 2024)">[659]</a></sup> Headcount figures understate the real size of a product organization, because an unintegrated company does not employ the contract manufacturer building its hardware.<sup><a href="#ref-645" data-tip="Ep 645: Moving Down The Stack with Scott Williams (September 4, 2023)" title="Ep 645: Moving Down The Stack with Scott Williams (September 4, 2023)">[645]</a></sup> In connectivity, a network-operator model installs and owns the low-power base stations and sells connectivity as a service, whereas an alliance-based radio standard such as LoRaWAN leaves deployment to a heterogeneous set of independent network companies.<sup><a href="#ref-443" data-tip="Ep 443: An Interview with JP Norair (May 19, 2019)" title="Ep 443: An Interview with JP Norair (May 19, 2019)">[443]</a></sup> One-person product companies can be effectively integrated at the smallest scale: Piotr Esden-Tempski runs design, firmware, and assembly for his product line himself, including a pick-and-place machine in his garage.<sup><a href="#ref-409" data-tip="Ep 409: Electronics Consultant Impedance Matching (September 30, 2018)" title="Ep 409: Electronics Consultant Impedance Matching (September 30, 2018)">[409]</a></sup>
