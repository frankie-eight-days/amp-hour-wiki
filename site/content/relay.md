A **relay** is an electrically operated switch in which a magnetic coil physically moves a strip of metal into contact with another piece of metal to make or break a connection.<sup><a href="#ref-94" title="Ep 94: Gnomic Gazumping Gobemouche (May 6, 2012)">[94]</a></sup> Because the control side and the switched side are separate, a relay provides galvanic isolation between the driving circuit and the load, and in one of its states a mechanical relay consumes no current at all.<sup><a href="#ref-93" title="Ep 93: An Interview with Tom LeMense - Cacaesthestic Chronometric Carriwitchet (April 29, 2012)">[93]</a></sup> Relays formed the basis of early computing and telephone switching and remain in use wherever contacts must carry large currents, exhibit near-zero leakage, or be driven from simple low-power control signals.<sup><a href="#ref-77" title="Ep 77: An Interview with Dr. Howard Johnson - Winsome Waveform Wizardry (January 9, 2012)">[77]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-94" title="Ep 94: Gnomic Gazumping Gobemouche (May 6, 2012)">[94]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-607" title="Ep 607: The Joulescope Upgrade with Matt Liberty (October 30, 2022)">[607]</a></sup>

## Construction and operation

A conventional electromechanical relay consists of a coil and a contact assembly: energising the coil generates a magnetic field that pulls an armature, closing (or opening) the contacts.<sup><a href="#ref-94" title="Ep 94: Gnomic Gazumping Gobemouche (May 6, 2012)">[94]</a></sup> The coil and the switched contacts are electrically distinct, a point that practitioners identify as a common source of confusion: a university lab supervisor reports that students routinely cannot operate a relay because they do not realise a separately driven coil exists inside the package.<sup><a href="#ref-127" title="Ep 127: FPGA, Xess, 32 Bit - Quirky Qualitative Questions (January 7, 2013)">[127]</a></sup> Unknown switch or relay terminals can be identified empirically by ringing them out with an ohmmeter rather than relying on pole-and-throw nomenclature.<sup><a href="#ref-127" title="Ep 127: FPGA, Xess, 32 Bit - Quirky Qualitative Questions (January 7, 2013)">[127]</a></sup>

At extreme current levels the same principle scales up physically: switching 5,000 amps required a purpose-built pneumatic contactor in which a large ram forced one metal plate against another, the plates themselves forming the relay contact.<sup><a href="#ref-546" title="Ep 546: Thousands Of Dependencies (June 21, 2021)">[546]</a></sup>

The switching hardware in common use spans several distinct families with different terminal arrangements: miniature reed relays, solid-state relays, and conventional electromechanical relays in a range of sizes.<sup><a href="#ref-127" title="Ep 127: FPGA, Xess, 32 Bit - Quirky Qualitative Questions (January 7, 2013)">[127]</a></sup>

### Reed relays

A reed switch on its own is a sealed glass tube containing two contacts, with no coil; only the coil-plus-switch assembly constitutes a reed relay.<sup><a href="#ref-88" title="Ep 88: Yonderly Yodeling Yobbos (March 25, 2012)">[88]</a></sup> Reed switches are a specialist product category with dedicated manufacturers, such as Mida, that build essentially nothing else, and the manufacturing process involves a surprising number of high-precision steps.<sup><a href="#ref-88" title="Ep 88: Yonderly Yodeling Yobbos (March 25, 2012)">[88]</a></sup>

Reed relays are the preferred choice for switching low-level signals, where contact quality and low leakage matter more than current-carrying capacity.<sup><a href="#ref-94" title="Ep 94: Gnomic Gazumping Gobemouche (May 6, 2012)">[94]</a></sup> A good low-power reed relay needs only about five milliamps of coil drive current, which sets the benchmark any miniature relay alternative has to beat.<sup><a href="#ref-94" title="Ep 94: Gnomic Gazumping Gobemouche (May 6, 2012)">[94]</a></sup>

### MEMS relays

MEMS relays exist as very small single-pole double-throw parts in LGA packages, offering mechanical contact behaviour at chip scale.<sup><a href="#ref-94" title="Ep 94: Gnomic Gazumping Gobemouche (May 6, 2012)">[94]</a></sup>

## History

Early computing was built from relay logic, and engineers pushed relay technology to the upper limits of its speed capability before moving to vacuum tubes.<sup><a href="#ref-77" title="Ep 77: An Interview with Dr. Howard Johnson - Winsome Waveform Wizardry (January 9, 2012)">[77]</a></sup> Relay-based machines were limited not only by speed but by heat dissipation and packaging problems, the same class of physical limits that later recurred with tubes, transistors and integrated circuits.<sup><a href="#ref-77" title="Ep 77: An Interview with Dr. Howard Johnson - Winsome Waveform Wizardry (January 9, 2012)">[77]</a></sup>

Magnetically shielded ultra-miniature relays were developed for telephone switching exchanges, where thousands of lines had to be cross-connected in a small volume.<sup><a href="#ref-94" title="Ep 94: Gnomic Gazumping Gobemouche (May 6, 2012)">[94]</a></sup>

Electromechanical pinball machines implemented scorekeeping, carry, reset and target logic entirely from relays, stepper units and solenoids, with no processor of any kind.<sup><a href="#ref-485" title="Ep 485: An Interview with John Day (March 22, 2020)">[485]</a></sup> The programmable logic controller was later created to replace plant control panels that had been built from relays, switches and indicator lamps with a single reprogrammable box, and ladder logic was chosen as the PLC programming model specifically so that plant electricians and technicians could apply their existing relay-logic knowledge without learning a new discipline.<sup><a href="#ref-620" title="Ep 620: Engineering Education with Dr Don Wilcher (February 20, 2023)">[620]</a></sup>

## Failure modes

### Mechanical contact phenomena

The intrinsic speed limit of relay logic came from mechanical contact bounce: the moving levers ring rather than settling cleanly, which corrupts the switched signal.<sup><a href="#ref-77" title="Ep 77: An Interview with Dr. Howard Johnson - Winsome Waveform Wizardry (January 9, 2012)">[77]</a></sup> Synchronously switching a large bank of relays couples mechanically through the chassis; the whole machine can vibrate hard enough to cause bit errors in unrelated parts of the system.<sup><a href="#ref-77" title="Ep 77: An Interview with Dr. Howard Johnson - Winsome Waveform Wizardry (January 9, 2012)">[77]</a></sup>

Relay contacts wear mechanically from repeated impact, and any air inside the package allows the contact surfaces to oxidise over time.<sup><a href="#ref-94" title="Ep 94: Gnomic Gazumping Gobemouche (May 6, 2012)">[94]</a></sup> Arcing at the contacts pits the metal and alters the surface material, so a worn relay can develop non-linear, rectifying behaviour instead of acting as a clean switch.<sup><a href="#ref-94" title="Ep 94: Gnomic Gazumping Gobemouche (May 6, 2012)">[94]</a></sup> In fault diagnosis of switched drive systems, a permanently running load such as an ABS pump points to a shorted switching element — a shorted motor, a shorted drive transistor pair, or welded relay contacts, depending on which drive topology the unit uses.<sup><a href="#ref-664" title="Ep 664: Simulating doors falling off (April 3, 2024)">[664]</a></sup>

Where cycling is continuous, contact ratings dominate design: a domestic PIR alarm sensor uses a relay rated for ten million operations, because the contact cycles every time motion is detected, around the clock for years.<sup><a href="#ref-94" title="Ep 94: Gnomic Gazumping Gobemouche (May 6, 2012)">[94]</a></sup>

### Magnetic interference

Because the contact armature is moved magnetically, a stray external magnetic field can pull the contacts together and close a relay that was never commanded on.<sup><a href="#ref-94" title="Ep 94: Gnomic Gazumping Gobemouche (May 6, 2012)">[94]</a></sup> Packing relays tightly, as in a switching matrix, lets their coil magnetic fields couple into neighbouring relays and interfere with them; the fix is to specify magnetically shielded relays rather than trying to space ordinary parts apart.<sup><a href="#ref-94" title="Ep 94: Gnomic Gazumping Gobemouche (May 6, 2012)">[94]</a></sup> High-specification shielded relays of this class cost on the order of ten dollars each even in volume.<sup><a href="#ref-94" title="Ep 94: Gnomic Gazumping Gobemouche (May 6, 2012)">[94]</a></sup>

### Leakage in matrices

Off-state leakage adds in parallel across a matrix: an individual relay may offer about two gigaohms of open impedance, but a thousand of them in parallel behave like a much lower resistance and degrade signal integrity.<sup><a href="#ref-94" title="Ep 94: Gnomic Gazumping Gobemouche (May 6, 2012)">[94]</a></sup>

## Driving relays

Relay coils are inductive loads and need a freewheeling, or flyback, diode across the coil to clamp the kickback transient generated when drive current is removed.<sup><a href="#ref-551" title="Ep 551: Feed the Mouse (July 25, 2021)">[551]</a></sup> Inductive kickback transients are a credible cause of downstream damage: a Raspberry Pi CM4 already running near its voltage limit failed, with spikes from the upstream switching circuit the suspected trigger.<sup><a href="#ref-551" title="Ep 551: Feed the Mouse (July 25, 2021)">[551]</a></sup> An open-drain output can sink current into relay coils and solenoids, but it cannot drive an H-bridge, which requires both high-side and low-side drive.<sup><a href="#ref-600" title="Ep 600: The Custodial Arts (August 21, 2022)">[600]</a></sup>

## Relays versus solid-state switching

A latching or unenergised mechanical relay draws literally zero current in one of its states, which is why solid-state switches have not displaced relays despite vendors wanting to sell the replacement.<sup><a href="#ref-93" title="Ep 93: An Interview with Tom LeMense - Cacaesthestic Chronometric Carriwitchet (April 29, 2012)">[93]</a></sup> Mechanical relays remain attractive in precision analog signal paths because they have effectively zero leakage and very few parasitics, their main penalty being some added capacitance.<sup><a href="#ref-607" title="Ep 607: The Joulescope Upgrade with Matt Liberty (October 30, 2022)">[607]</a></sup>

The countervailing limitation is speed. For current-measurement range switching there are three options — relays, N-channel MOSFETs, or a mix — and relays, while clean, are far too slow for fast automatic ranging.<sup><a href="#ref-293" title="Ep 293: Call In Show #4 (March 30, 2016)">[293]</a></sup> The audible clicking of a bench multimeter changing ranges is relay actuation, and it is a direct indicator of how slow relay-based range switching is compared with FET switching.<sup><a href="#ref-607" title="Ep 607: The Joulescope Upgrade with Matt Liberty (October 30, 2022)">[607]</a></sup> Replacing relay range switching with MOSFETs allows the range decision to be made in under a microsecond using dedicated comparators and a separate analog channel feeding an FPGA, though the solid-state switches must be driven fast but with a controlled edge, because uncontrolled MOSFET gate drive injects charge into the measured signal.<sup><a href="#ref-607" title="Ep 607: The Joulescope Upgrade with Matt Liberty (October 30, 2022)">[607]</a></sup> Integrated parts with "current shunt" in the name are designed for power monitoring, not low-current measurement, making them a poor starting point for a microamp-capable instrument.<sup><a href="#ref-293" title="Ep 293: Call In Show #4 (March 30, 2016)">[293]</a></sup>

Digital isolator chips provide an alternative to relays for galvanic isolation by placing two dies in one package with a pair of coils between them, forming an on-package transformer; the same technique now supports isolated DC-to-DC conversion as well as isolated data links such as USB.<sup><a href="#ref-94" title="Ep 94: Gnomic Gazumping Gobemouche (May 6, 2012)">[94]</a></sup>

In power electronics, automotive ABS pump drive moved from a relay in older designs to a totem-pole MOSFET pair in newer ones.<sup><a href="#ref-664" title="Ep 664: Simulating doors falling off (April 3, 2024)">[664]</a></sup>

## Applications

In industrial equipment the standby current budget is dominated not by the processor but by sensors, actuators and relays.<sup><a href="#ref-93" title="Ep 93: An Interview with Tom LeMense - Cacaesthestic Chronometric Carriwitchet (April 29, 2012)">[93]</a></sup> Industrial carrier boards offer relay modules as a standard option so a compute module can drive conventional 24-volt DC machine interfaces alongside opto-isolated IO and serial links.<sup><a href="#ref-608" title="Ep 608: Vapor Phase with Saber Kaygusuz (November 7, 2022)">[608]</a></sup>

Switching the output of a rotary phase converter into a step-up transformer required a relay rated at 14 kVA, deliberately oversized from a calculated requirement of roughly four to four and a half kVA to add margin; the start-up sequence matters, because the transformer must be switched in only after the converter is already spinning, otherwise energising it can destroy the converter.<sup><a href="#ref-220" title="Ep 220: An Interview with Shaun Meehan - Doctiloquent Dove Deployer (October 13, 2014)">[220]</a></sup> A safety interlock on delivery trucks uses a relay driven by one controller to signal a second controller that it is safe to release the handbrake, preventing rollaway while the engine idles to run the delivery pump.<sup><a href="#ref-266" title="Ep 266: An Interview with Ronald Sousa of Hash Define Electronics (September 8, 2015)">[266]</a></sup>

A Wi-Fi-connected garden controller switched AC outlets for pumps and lights through Omron relays, driven by temperature, humidity, light and soil-conductivity readings.<sup><a href="#ref-268" title="Ep 268: An Interview with Luke Iseman of yCombinator (September 22, 2015)">[268]</a></sup> Off-the-shelf home automation relay boards are commonly specified around four channels rated for 240 volts at 20 amps.<sup><a href="#ref-683" title="Ep 683: Troubleshooting is the skill (November 20, 2024)">[683]</a></sup> A relay can also serve as a hard power kill for a networked appliance, physically cutting mains to a smart speaker so it cannot listen regardless of its firmware state.<sup><a href="#ref-335" title="Ep 335: When the TV watches you (February 8, 2017)">[335]</a></sup> Grid-tied solar inverters curtail output by simply opening relay contacts inside the box to disconnect from the grid, rather than by any sophisticated regulation.<sup><a href="#ref-702" title="Ep 702: Test Point Accupuncture (September 14, 2025)">[702]</a></sup>

In a home-built transcranial magnetic stimulation rig, a relay is used to abruptly interrupt the current in a heavy-gauge air-core stimulating coil; the collapsing field induces current in nearby tissue, producing a visible muscle twitch. Because field strength falls off with the square of distance, the coil must be held essentially against the target to be effective.<sup><a href="#ref-75" title="Ep 75: An Interview with Ben Krasnow - Sprauncy Saccadic Spintherism">[75]</a></sup>

## Design and practice

Breadboard prototyping remains acceptable provided the builder respects its limits, and slow loads such as relay circuits fall comfortably inside those limits.<sup><a href="#ref-551" title="Ep 551: Feed the Mouse (July 25, 2021)">[551]</a></sup> In a layered automation system, the firmware that actually closes the relay should be kept trivially simple and stable — listen for a signal, flip the relay — with changeable scheduling and priority logic pushed up to a higher-level interface.<sup><a href="#ref-654" title="Ep 654: Pseudo Code...Pseudo Good (December 18, 2023)">[654]</a></sup>

Distributor parametric search will not surface a part from qualitative requirements such as small and cheap; finding the right relay can mean guessing the one technology keyword under which the vendor indexes it.<sup><a href="#ref-94" title="Ep 94: Gnomic Gazumping Gobemouche (May 6, 2012)">[94]</a></sup>

Debugging relay logic is a schematic-tracing exercise rather than a software one: the technician follows the circuit through each relay contact in turn, probing with clip leads and indicators to localise the fault.<sup><a href="#ref-485" title="Ep 485: An Interview with John Day (March 22, 2020)">[485]</a></sup> The relay has been identified alongside the BJT and the SCR as a core device an electrical engineer must understand, with the observation that formal courses skip connections between them, such as treating an SCR as a solid-state relay.<sup><a href="#ref-109" title="Ep 109: An Interview with Larry Sears - Hexagram Hardware Holism (August 19, 2012)">[109]</a></sup>
