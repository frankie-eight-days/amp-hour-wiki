A **voltage regulator** is an electronic circuit or component that maintains a constant output voltage despite variation in its input supply or load.<sup><a href="#ref-18" data-tip="Ep 18: Transistor Types and Where To Get Electronic Gear" title="Ep 18: Transistor Types and Where To Get Electronic Gear">[18]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-120" data-tip="Ep 120: Prototyping, Machining & Accelerators- Mugwumps Mulling Milling (November 4, 2012)" title="Ep 120: Prototyping, Machining & Accelerators- Mugwumps Mulling Milling (November 4, 2012)">[120]</a></sup> Regulators fall into two broad families—linear regulators, which dissipate the difference between input and output as heat, and switching converters, which use an inductor to transfer energy—and the two can be distinguished on a board by eye, since an inductor adjacent to the power device is a definitive indicator of the switching type.<sup><a href="#ref-120" data-tip="Ep 120: Prototyping, Machining & Accelerators- Mugwumps Mulling Milling (November 4, 2012)" title="Ep 120: Prototyping, Machining & Accelerators- Mugwumps Mulling Milling (November 4, 2012)">[120]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-591" data-tip="Ep 591: Olive-a The World" title="Ep 591: Olive-a The World">[591]</a></sup> The category spans an enormous range: a simple three-terminal linear part costing roughly one cent and a modern processor power regulator that amounts to a complete mixed-signal system in a single package are both technically voltage regulators, despite a difference in specification and performance comparable to that between two very different vehicles that are both technically cars.<sup><a href="#ref-493" data-tip="Ep 493: PITA Package (May 17, 2020)" title="Ep 493: PITA Package (May 17, 2020)">[493]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-566" data-tip="Ep 566: Switching Converter Engineering with Carmen Parisi (November 14, 2021)" title="Ep 566: Switching Converter Engineering with Carmen Parisi (November 14, 2021)">[566]</a></sup> Because fixed-output regulators in the common voltages cost around a cent, one appears in almost every design, and the engineering decision is typically which regulator to use rather than whether to use one.<sup><a href="#ref-493" data-tip="Ep 493: PITA Package (May 17, 2020)" title="Ep 493: PITA Package (May 17, 2020)">[493]</a></sup>

## Types and identification

Regulator type can be read off a board visually: a small three-terminal package surrounded by capacitors could be either a linear regulator or a switching converter, but an inductor sitting next to it settles the question in favour of the switching type.<sup><a href="#ref-591" data-tip="Ep 591: Olive-a The World" title="Ep 591: Olive-a The World">[591]</a></sup> Recognising circuit blocks by their physical layout in this way is a learned skill rather than an innate one; the shapes and arrangements mean nothing at first and become obvious with exposure, which is why experienced engineers can identify a function from a photograph.<sup><a href="#ref-591" data-tip="Ep 591: Olive-a The World" title="Ep 591: Olive-a The World">[591]</a></sup>

Linear regulation is the cruder approach but remains useful when a rail is needed immediately: a linear regulator from the parts drawer and a stack of batteries will produce five volts, at the cost of dissipating the entire input-to-output difference as heat.<sup><a href="#ref-120" data-tip="Ep 120: Prototyping, Machining & Accelerators- Mugwumps Mulling Milling (November 4, 2012)" title="Ep 120: Prototyping, Machining & Accelerators- Mugwumps Mulling Milling (November 4, 2012)">[120]</a></sup> Switching converters dominate where efficiency matters, and a modern processor power regulator is best understood as a complete mixed-signal system inside one package, carrying sensitive analog lines, digital buses, and power transistors within a few square millimetres.<sup><a href="#ref-566" data-tip="Ep 566: Switching Converter Engineering with Carmen Parisi (November 14, 2021)" title="Ep 566: Switching Converter Engineering with Carmen Parisi (November 14, 2021)">[566]</a></sup> Switching converters intended to sit near a radio are designed around the spectrum as well as efficiency: pushing the switching frequency to two megahertz places it above the AM band, and spreading the switching frequency reduces peak emissions, both measures existing to ease the customer's compliance testing.<sup><a href="#ref-635" data-tip="Ep 635: Low Power Connected Devices with Andrea Longobardi (June 4, 2023)" title="Ep 635: Low Power Connected Devices with Andrea Longobardi (June 4, 2023)">[635]</a></sup>

The distance between a three-terminal linear part and modern processor power delivery is reflected in the engineering roles around them: there is an architecture function between applications and circuit design that specifies what a regulator's control loop requires—error amplifier gain and bandwidth—then negotiates against what the designers can deliver on schedule and judges whether the regulation specification still holds with what comes back.<sup><a href="#ref-566" data-tip="Ep 566: Switching Converter Engineering with Carmen Parisi (November 14, 2021)" title="Ep 566: Switching Converter Engineering with Carmen Parisi (November 14, 2021)">[566]</a></sup>

## Operating principles

### Adjustable regulators and the reference pin

A widespread misunderstanding concerns the adjustable three-terminal regulator: its reference is a fixed voltage developed between the output pin and the adjust pin, not a feedback node held at that voltage relative to ground.<sup><a href="#ref-574" data-tip="Ep 574: Bubblegum Tap Shoes (January 23, 2022)" title="Ep 574: Bubblegum Tap Shoes (January 23, 2022)">[574]</a></sup> Tying the adjust pin to the output through a series resistor therefore turns the part into a current source, with the reference voltage divided by that resistor setting the output current.<sup><a href="#ref-574" data-tip="Ep 574: Bubblegum Tap Shoes (January 23, 2022)" title="Ep 574: Bubblegum Tap Shoes (January 23, 2022)">[574]</a></sup>

### Shutdown, quiescent current, and wake-up

Shutdown current and quiescent current describe different states and are frequently confused.<sup><a href="#ref-635" data-tip="Ep 635: Low Power Connected Devices with Andrea Longobardi (June 4, 2023)" title="Ep 635: Low Power Connected Devices with Andrea Longobardi (June 4, 2023)">[635]</a></sup> In shutdown the regulator is off and the output is not regulated at all; quiescent current is what the part draws while holding the output in regulation with no load, so that the rail is ready the instant the load wakes up.<sup><a href="#ref-635" data-tip="Ep 635: Low Power Connected Devices with Andrea Longobardi (June 4, 2023)" title="Ep 635: Low Power Connected Devices with Andrea Longobardi (June 4, 2023)">[635]</a></sup> Wake-up time is specified alongside that current, typically requiring the rail to return to full regulation within microseconds to milliseconds, because a low-power state is only useful if leaving it is fast.<sup><a href="#ref-635" data-tip="Ep 635: Low Power Connected Devices with Andrea Longobardi (June 4, 2023)" title="Ep 635: Low Power Connected Devices with Andrea Longobardi (June 4, 2023)">[635]</a></sup>

### Control loops

A supply can regulate voltage and current simultaneously by running two control loops in parallel and letting whichever loop demands the smaller pulse width take control.<sup><a href="#ref-522" data-tip="Ep 522: High Current Power Supplies with Fredrik Kensander (December 20, 2020)" title="Ep 522: High Current Power Supplies with Fredrik Kensander (December 20, 2020)">[522]</a></sup> That arrangement handles a load whose resistance changes during operation, such as a plating bath being loaded and unloaded.<sup><a href="#ref-522" data-tip="Ep 522: High Current Power Supplies with Fredrik Kensander (December 20, 2020)" title="Ep 522: High Current Power Supplies with Fredrik Kensander (December 20, 2020)">[522]</a></sup>

## Rails and the proliferation of regulators

Modern boards often require many regulators. As process geometry has pushed core voltages down to around 0.9 volts while input and output pins remain near 3.3 volts, whether a part integrates its own core regulator has become one of the first selection questions rather than a detail.<sup><a href="#ref-217" data-tip="Ep 217: 3D Printed Shark Jumps - Edifying Edison's Energy (September 22, 2014)" title="Ep 217: 3D Printed Shark Jumps - Edifying Edison's Energy (September 22, 2014)">[217]</a></sup> A cheaply priced 32-bit microcontroller can carry a hidden cost here: a part needing a separate low core voltage cannot run directly from an existing rail, so an extra regulator joins the design and the system cost stops resembling the part price.<sup><a href="#ref-54" data-tip="Ep 54: An Interview with Jack Ganssle - Embedded Elchee Epexegesis" title="Ep 54: An Interview with Jack Ganssle - Embedded Elchee Epexegesis">[54]</a></sup> The economics underneath are that cost tracks silicon area, so with all else equal an 8-bit part remains cheaper than a 32-bit one regardless of headline prices.<sup><a href="#ref-54" data-tip="Ep 54: An Interview with Jack Ganssle - Embedded Elchee Epexegesis" title="Ep 54: An Interview with Jack Ganssle - Embedded Elchee Epexegesis">[54]</a></sup> Some parts remove the problem with an on-chip core regulator: the die runs internally at 1.5 or 1.8 volts while the package is powered from an ordinary rail, and the designer's only obligation is a bypass capacitor.<sup><a href="#ref-53" data-tip="Ep 53: Biarchy Birthday Bavardage" title="Ep 53: Biarchy Birthday Bavardage">[53]</a></sup>

The cumulative effect is substantial: five separate regulators can be needed just to run one processor, each with its own passives and board area.<sup><a href="#ref-326" data-tip="Ep 326: Magical Fire Bags (December 7, 2016)" title="Ep 326: Magical Fire Bags (December 7, 2016)">[326]</a></sup> Some processors add further requirements—a supply that varies with clock speed, in one case between 1.2 and 1.35 volts, which cannot be met by sharing an existing rail and forces a dedicated adjustable regulator.<sup><a href="#ref-325" data-tip="Ep 325: An Interview with David Kronstein (Tesla500) (November 30, 2016)" title="Ep 325: An Interview with David Kronstein (Tesla500) (November 30, 2016)">[325]</a></sup> Conversely, removing a required rail simplifies a board disproportionately: when one FPGA family dropped its 2.5-volt requirement, the design could run from USB five volts with a couple of regulators and nothing else.<sup><a href="#ref-181" data-tip="Ep 181: An Interview with Dave Vandenbout - Xceptional XESS Xenagogue" title="Ep 181: An Interview with Dave Vandenbout - Xceptional XESS Xenagogue">[181]</a></sup> That rail had existed only for auxiliary configuration circuitry, making it pure overhead—a whole regulator and its passives supporting something incidental to the part's actual function.<sup><a href="#ref-181" data-tip="Ep 181: An Interview with Dave Vandenbout - Xceptional XESS Xenagogue" title="Ep 181: An Interview with Dave Vandenbout - Xceptional XESS Xenagogue">[181]</a></sup>

### Distributed and local regulation

Local regulation is what lets an awkward part live on a board whose supply does not suit it: placing a small regulator next to a device that needs an unusual rail means the board can be fed from whatever is available.<sup><a href="#ref-18" data-tip="Ep 18: Transistor Types and Where To Get Electronic Gear" title="Ep 18: Transistor Types and Where To Get Electronic Gear">[18]</a></sup> Older instruments distribute regulation widely rather than centrally, taking plus and minus 18-volt rails down to plus and minus 15 for the analog sections and eight volts down to five for the digital, which can put twenty regulators on a single board.<sup><a href="#ref-168" data-tip="Ep 168: Specialized and/or Open Source Test Gear and Dev Boards - Vacation Videography Vorboten (October 21, 2013)" title="Ep 168: Specialized and/or Open Source Test Gear and Dev Boards - Vacation Videography Vorboten (October 21, 2013)">[168]</a></sup> Distributing power across a network of interconnected boards turns the supply into a resistor-grid problem that is tedious to analyse; putting a regulator on each device absorbs about a volt of drop and removes the need for the analysis entirely.<sup><a href="#ref-330" data-tip="Ep 330: An Interview with Zach Fredin (January 4, 2017)" title="Ep 330: An Interview with Zach Fredin (January 4, 2017)">[330]</a></sup> Specifying generously at the supply removes a class of user problems: an eight-amp five-volt regulator on a USB hub lets every downstream port deliver two amps, which adds cost but means anything plugged in will power up.<sup><a href="#ref-425" data-tip="Ep 425: An Interview with Chris Osterwood (January 13, 2019)" title="Ep 425: An Interview with Chris Osterwood (January 13, 2019)">[425]</a></sup>

## Design considerations and failure modes

### Capacitors and stability

A classic stability failure comes from reading only half the capacitor specification: a datasheet calling for a minimum output capacitance may also assume a minimum equivalent series resistance, so substituting a modern ceramic of the correct value provides too little resistance and the regulator oscillates.<sup><a href="#ref-188" data-tip="Ep 188: Capacitors, Simulation and Closures - Deonerated Design Dealmaking (March 10, 2014)" title="Ep 188: Capacitors, Simulation and Closures - Deonerated Design Dealmaking (March 10, 2014)">[188]</a></sup>

### Minimum load

Some regulators require a minimum load to stay in regulation; a crude but effective production fix is a power resistor in parallel with the output, guaranteeing the part always draws its minimum current whatever the real load is doing.<sup><a href="#ref-222" data-tip="Ep 222: An Interview With Bil Herd - Zany Z80 Zygology (October 27, 2014)" title="Ep 222: An Interview With Bil Herd - Zany Z80 Zygology (October 27, 2014)">[222]</a></sup>

### Input rating and margin

A systematic field failure can be caused purely by margin: a five-volt regulator rated to 21 volts input running from a 24-volt supply will fail at scale, and the fix is simply fitting a properly rated part.<sup><a href="#ref-490" data-tip="Ep 490: An Interview with Ben Heck(endorn) (April 27, 2020)" title="Ep 490: An Interview with Ben Heck(endorn) (April 27, 2020)">[490]</a></sup> The absolute maximum input must be checked against the actual supply rather than the nominal one.<sup><a href="#ref-490" data-tip="Ep 490: An Interview with Ben Heck(endorn) (April 27, 2020)" title="Ep 490: An Interview with Ben Heck(endorn) (April 27, 2020)">[490]</a></sup>

### Feedback networks

A wrong resistor value in a regulator's feedback network produces a plausible but incorrect output rather than an obvious failure: the board comes up, the rail is simply not what it should be, and nothing points at the cause.<sup><a href="#ref-561" data-tip="Ep 561: Assembly Chat (October 10, 2021)" title="Ep 561: Assembly Chat (October 10, 2021)">[561]</a></sup>

### Control pins and part selection

Parametric search does not cover pin function, so there is no way to filter regulators by whether they have a shutdown pin; vendors name and implement those pins differently, leaving manual reading of datasheets as the only method.<sup><a href="#ref-68" data-tip="Ep 68: Radiation Chips & Old Package Types - Technocratic Toilet Troubleshooting" title="Ep 68: Radiation Chips & Old Package Types - Technocratic Toilet Troubleshooting">[68]</a></sup> An unwanted control pin is a small liability rather than a free feature—it must be tied off correctly, usually through a resistor, and is one more thing to get wrong—so designers actively filter such parts out when the function is not needed.<sup><a href="#ref-68" data-tip="Ep 68: Radiation Chips & Old Package Types - Technocratic Toilet Troubleshooting" title="Ep 68: Radiation Chips & Old Package Types - Technocratic Toilet Troubleshooting">[68]</a></sup>

### Supply risk

Supply risk concentrates in regulators despite their low cost: regulator packages are often specialised enough that an unavailable part is not footprint-compatible with anything else, so a shortage forces a redesign rather than a substitution.<sup><a href="#ref-530" data-tip="Ep 530: Living Through Chipageddon (February 15, 2021)" title="Ep 530: Living Through Chipageddon (February 15, 2021)">[530]</a></sup>

### Omission of regulation

A product built to an extreme price may omit regulation entirely, which is visible in use: the display fades whenever the device starts a high-current operation, because the supply sags and nothing is holding the rail up.<sup><a href="#ref-633" data-tip="Ep 633: Engineering Optimization (May 22, 2023)" title="Ep 633: Engineering Optimization (May 22, 2023)">[633]</a></sup> At the other extreme, a single defective part can end a whole product: adding external Schottky diodes in parallel with a regulator brought a dropout from 0.6 to 0.3 volts and back inside specification, but the unresolvable question was how to prove that a one-in-a-hundred failure rate did not remain.<sup><a href="#ref-140" data-tip="Ep 140: Project Management, Lasers & Robots - Staunch Specialty Sanctanimity (April 8, 2013)" title="Ep 140: Project Management, Lasers & Robots - Staunch Specialty Sanctanimity (April 8, 2013)">[140]</a></sup>

## Assembly and troubleshooting

The single most common kit assembly failure is a three-pin regulator fitted backwards, and it arrives reported as a design defect after the builder has checked everything else; the part, such as a 78L05 in a TO-92 package, is identifiable at a glance on a returned board.<sup><a href="#ref-477" data-tip="Ep 477: EcoWoke and Going Broke (February 2, 2020)" title="Ep 477: EcoWoke and Going Broke (February 2, 2020)">[477]</a></sup> The design response is clearer silkscreen marking, because orientation is genuinely not obvious to a beginner looking at a three-pin package, so the marking does real work rather than decorating the board.<sup><a href="#ref-477" data-tip="Ep 477: EcoWoke and Going Broke (February 2, 2020)" title="Ep 477: EcoWoke and Going Broke (February 2, 2020)">[477]</a></sup>

When hardware is reported broken, the discipline is to resist the reflex that it must be the firmware or the other party's fault, and to check all the supply voltages first; the reflex is strong and usually wrong.<sup><a href="#ref-176" data-tip="Ep 176: Funding New/Manufacturing Old Projects - Radical Robotic Requisition (December 16, 2013)" title="Ep 176: Funding New/Manufacturing Old Projects - Radical Robotic Requisition (December 16, 2013)">[176]</a></sup> Remote troubleshooting of such problems is impractical: every measurement is a round trip of hours by email, so either the person has an instrument and knows how to use it interactively, or the exchange goes nowhere.<sup><a href="#ref-82" data-tip="Ep 82: Vecordious Vacation Variorum (February 13, 2012)" title="Ep 82: Vecordious Vacation Variorum (February 13, 2012)">[82]</a></sup>

Powering a precision measurement circuit directly from batteries lets its offset drift as the cells discharge, which shows up as a slowly changing result rather than an obvious fault; adding a regulator is what makes the measurement stable over the life of the battery.<sup><a href="#ref-146" data-tip="Ep 146: Hamvention, Arduino and Intel - Burdensome Background Battology (May 21, 2013)" title="Ep 146: Hamvention, Arduino and Intel - Burdensome Background Battology (May 21, 2013)">[146]</a></sup>

## Protection and improvised uses

Regulators frequently act as sacrificial protection: when something upstream goes wrong they die and shield the circuitry behind them, which is why a repair on old equipment can mean replacing several regulators before the instrument comes back.<sup><a href="#ref-168" data-tip="Ep 168: Specialized and/or Open Source Test Gear and Dev Boards - Vacation Videography Vorboten (October 21, 2013)" title="Ep 168: Specialized and/or Open Source Test Gear and Dev Boards - Vacation Videography Vorboten (October 21, 2013)">[168]</a></sup>

Regulators have also been pressed into service outside their intended function. With no optocoupler available in the field, a five-volt regulator fed from a 24-volt supply has been used to provide level conversion, the current involved being low enough and a couple of extra passives handling the switching; in one case the temporary fix stayed in service for about two years.<sup><a href="#ref-266" data-tip="Ep 266: An Interview with Ronald Sousa of Hash Define Electronics (September 8, 2015)" title="Ep 266: An Interview with Ronald Sousa of Hash Define Electronics (September 8, 2015)">[266]</a></sup>
