A soldering iron is a hand tool used to melt solder and form joints between electronic components and a printed circuit board, consisting of a heated tip, a heating element, and a temperature-regulation mechanism.<sup><a href="#ref-384" data-tip="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)" title="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)">[384]</a></sup> Two constructions dominate the field: an older design in which the tip slides over a separate heating element with the temperature sensor buried in that assembly, and a newer design in which the element and sensor are integrated into the tip itself.<sup><a href="#ref-384" data-tip="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)" title="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)">[384]</a></sup> The property that matters most in use is thermal recovery — how quickly the tip returns to working temperature after each joint pulls heat from it — and this depends not on wattage alone but on the entire control loop, including the drive circuitry, the sensor's thermal response, and the physical design of the tip.<sup><a href="#ref-384" data-tip="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)" title="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)">[384]</a></sup> At the low end of the market, serviceable temperature-controlled irons are available for roughly thirty dollars, while production-grade systems cost around a thousand dollars and target instant heat and tightly held temperature rather than occasional use.<sup><a href="#ref-473" data-tip="Ep 473: An Interview with Greg Davill (January 5, 2020)" title="Ep 473: An Interview with Greg Davill (January 5, 2020)">[473]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-606" data-tip="Ep 606: Professional Scooter Charger (October 23, 2022)" title="Ep 606: Professional Scooter Charger (October 23, 2022)">[606]</a></sup>

## Construction

In the older construction, the tip slides over a separate heating element, with the temperature sensor buried somewhere in that assembly; the exact arrangement varies by manufacturer.<sup><a href="#ref-384" data-tip="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)" title="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)">[384]</a></sup> In the newer construction, the element and the sensor are built into the tip itself, so replacing the tip replaces the heater and the sensor with it, and the feedback path no longer runs through a mechanical contact or an air gap.<sup><a href="#ref-384" data-tip="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)" title="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)">[384]</a></sup> The two can be distinguished without disassembly: an old-technology tip is a bare piece of formed metal with no electrical contacts on it.<sup><a href="#ref-384" data-tip="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)" title="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)">[384]</a></sup>

A third architecture is the induction-heated cartridge iron, which works by induction rather than a resistive element and detects its cradle so that the iron shuts down when set down and is hot again by the time it reaches the work.<sup><a href="#ref-528" data-tip="Ep 528: New Year, New Gear (January 31, 2021)" title="Ep 528: New Year, New Gear (January 31, 2021)">[528]</a></sup> Because the temperature in such a system is a property of the cartridge rather than of the station, buying tips means choosing a shape and a temperature band together.<sup><a href="#ref-528" data-tip="Ep 528: New Year, New Gear (January 31, 2021)" title="Ep 528: New Year, New Gear (January 31, 2021)">[528]</a></sup> The cost of this architecture is the absence of a controller in the station: covering several tip shapes across several temperatures means keeping an array of perhaps twenty cartridges at ten to fifteen dollars each.<sup><a href="#ref-528" data-tip="Ep 528: New Year, New Gear (January 31, 2021)" title="Ep 528: New Year, New Gear (January 31, 2021)">[528]</a></sup>

## Thermal performance

The property that actually matters in use is recovery: each joint pulls heat out of the tip and drops its temperature, and the time taken to climb back is what limits working through a row of joints.<sup><a href="#ref-384" data-tip="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)" title="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)">[384]</a></sup> Recovery time is not simply a matter of wattage; peak power, the drive circuitry, the temperature sensor's own thermal response, and the physical design of the tip all sit inside the same control loop.<sup><a href="#ref-384" data-tip="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)" title="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)">[384]</a></sup> Integrated-tip designs perform better on this measure because the element, the heater, and the tip were engineered as one object, so the control loop sees the disturbance sooner and applies power sooner.<sup><a href="#ref-384" data-tip="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)" title="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)">[384]</a></sup>

Thermal mass illustrates the same principle at the other end of the trade: irons sold for copper plumbing run at a hundred and fifty watts with an enormous tip, purely because the work keeps taking heat away.<sup><a href="#ref-384" data-tip="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)" title="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)">[384]</a></sup> Conversely, old tip-over-element irons remain serviceable for low thermal mass work, and it is high-mass joints that expose the difference between the architectures and justify keeping a separate iron for them.<sup><a href="#ref-716" data-tip="Ep 716: Electronics Manufacturing History with David Ray (February 25, 2026)" title="Ep 716: Electronics Manufacturing History with David Ray (February 25, 2026)">[716]</a></sup>

## Tips

Tip design is a deliberate compromise between working life and thermal performance, set by the cladding material and the thermal resistance it adds; a long-life tip and a very low-resistance tip are not the same tip.<sup><a href="#ref-528" data-tip="Ep 528: New Year, New Gear (January 31, 2021)" title="Ep 528: New Year, New Gear (January 31, 2021)">[528]</a></sup> The control scheme and the tip technology are independent axes, and expensive irons do not all sit on the same one: a high-end station may still be dial-controlled with a direct-heat tip rather than using temperature-selecting cartridges.<sup><a href="#ref-606" data-tip="Ep 606: Professional Scooter Charger (October 23, 2022)" title="Ep 606: Professional Scooter Charger (October 23, 2022)">[606]</a></sup>

Tip selection matters more than the user interface, which on most stations amounts to setting a temperature once and leaving it.<sup><a href="#ref-311" data-tip="Ep 311: An Interview with Louis Rossmann (August 10, 2016)" title="Ep 311: An Interview with Louis Rossmann (August 10, 2016)">[311]</a></sup> In his repair practice, Louis Rossmann favours tips that serve two purposes at once — a large body carrying thermal mass that still ends in a fine point, or a knife shape usable on its edge for detail and on its full blade for bulk — and regards the conical tip that irons ship with by default as the least useful of the common shapes.<sup><a href="#ref-311" data-tip="Ep 311: An Interview with Louis Rossmann (August 10, 2016)" title="Ep 311: An Interview with Louis Rossmann (August 10, 2016)">[311]</a></sup> Very fine work may require a separate smaller handpiece rather than just a smaller tip, since the handle itself determines which tips will fit.<sup><a href="#ref-702" data-tip="Ep 702: Test Point Accupuncture (September 14, 2025)" title="Ep 702: Test Point Accupuncture (September 14, 2025)">[702]</a></sup>

## Operating temperature

The temperature an iron is set to is far above the melting point of the solder alloy, and the discrepancy against a datasheet's stated maximum is a recurring source of confusion.<sup><a href="#ref-183" data-tip="Ep 183: An Interview with Scott Driscoll - Impacable Interdisciplinary Inventor (February 3, 2014)" title="Ep 183: An Interview with Scott Driscoll - Impacable Interdisciplinary Inventor (February 3, 2014)">[183]</a></sup> The explanation is that the profiles in a datasheet describe mass reflow of the whole assembly, not a hand tool, and a hand tool has to overcome the heat running away from the part into the board.<sup><a href="#ref-183" data-tip="Ep 183: An Interview with Scott Driscoll - Impacable Interdisciplinary Inventor (February 3, 2014)" title="Ep 183: An Interview with Scott Driscoll - Impacable Interdisciplinary Inventor (February 3, 2014)">[183]</a></sup> A common working setting on a fixed-temperature cartridge is around 370 degrees Celsius, which is high but conventional for this reason.<sup><a href="#ref-528" data-tip="Ep 528: New Year, New Gear (January 31, 2021)" title="Ep 528: New Year, New Gear (January 31, 2021)">[528]</a></sup>

## Technique

Working with two irons is technique rather than indulgence: with one iron on each end of a small part, the part can be lifted straight off the board, a point stated flatly as equipment advice by practitioners who solder daily.<sup><a href="#ref-528" data-tip="Ep 528: New Year, New Gear (January 31, 2021)" title="Ep 528: New Year, New Gear (January 31, 2021)">[528]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-534" data-tip="Ep 534: Firmware Update Capabilities (March 14, 2021)" title="Ep 534: Firmware Update Capabilities (March 14, 2021)">[534]</a></sup> An oversized part on small pads can be soldered without the tip ever touching the pad — the lead is heated and conducts heat into the joint underneath.<sup><a href="#ref-488" data-tip="Ep 488: Sowing Discord (April 12, 2020)" title="Ep 488: Sowing Discord (April 12, 2020)">[488]</a></sup> Removing parts from old boards is a separate thermal problem that generally calls for a high-power iron.<sup><a href="#ref-110" data-tip="Ep 110: Armstrong, Camenzind & Museums - Outstanding Oneirophoros Obituaries (August 26, 2012)" title="Ep 110: Armstrong, Camenzind & Museums - Outstanding Oneirophoros Obituaries (August 26, 2012)">[110]</a></sup>

Setup matters as much as the tool. The reason to buy an optical microscope rather than a cheap camera-based one is working distance: the height above the board is what leaves room to get an iron underneath.<sup><a href="#ref-345" data-tip="Ep 345: Milling About (May 30, 2017)" title="Ep 345: Milling About (May 30, 2017)">[345]</a></sup> Beginners commonly try to do everything at once — holding the part, the board, the iron, and the solder — when the answer is to set the work up first and understand that the joint needs a moment to take heat.<sup><a href="#ref-413" data-tip="Ep 413: A House of FR4 (October 28, 2018)" title="Ep 413: A House of FR4 (October 28, 2018)">[413]</a></sup>

### Teaching

Instruction can be almost entirely hands-off: cover the safety points, demonstrate once, and let the learner do the work rather than delivering half an hour of instruction first.<sup><a href="#ref-413" data-tip="Ep 413: A House of FR4 (October 28, 2018)" title="Ep 413: A House of FR4 (October 28, 2018)">[413]</a></sup> Over a couple of days, a group with no engineering background can be taken from not recognising the hardware to soldering on it, probing it, and reverse engineering the board.<sup><a href="#ref-575" data-tip="Ep 575: New Life Skills with Joe Grand (January 30, 2022)" title="Ep 575: New Life Skills with Joe Grand (January 30, 2022)">[575]</a></sup>

## Equipment grades and market structure

The practical entry threshold is lower than enthusiasm suggests: any temperature-controlled iron will do the job.<sup><a href="#ref-413" data-tip="Ep 413: A House of FR4 (October 28, 2018)" title="Ep 413: A House of FR4 (October 28, 2018)">[413]</a></sup> The genuine failure case is an abused tool rather than a cheap one — an iron run all day at an event until it barely melts solder is a different proposition.<sup><a href="#ref-413" data-tip="Ep 413: A House of FR4 (October 28, 2018)" title="Ep 413: A House of FR4 (October 28, 2018)">[413]</a></sup> It is also worth resisting the inference that good work comes from a good iron; the defensible claim is only that a given tool is not a bad one.<sup><a href="#ref-413" data-tip="Ep 413: A House of FR4 (October 28, 2018)" title="Ep 413: A House of FR4 (October 28, 2018)">[413]</a></sup>

At the low end, an inexpensive pencil taking a widely copied cartridge standard costs about thirty dollars and handles lead-free work without complaint.<sup><a href="#ref-473" data-tip="Ep 473: An Interview with Greg Davill (January 5, 2020)" title="Ep 473: An Interview with Greg Davill (January 5, 2020)">[473]</a></sup> Portable irons powered over a USB connection exist in the same price range with reasonable build quality.<sup><a href="#ref-633" data-tip="Ep 633: Engineering Optimization (May 22, 2023)" title="Ep 633: Engineering Optimization (May 22, 2023)">[633]</a></sup> The limitation of running an iron from a small portable source is thermal capacity rather than temperature; an unregulated mains pencil or a gas iron at least has some heat behind it.<sup><a href="#ref-158" data-tip="Ep 158: Hyperloop, Upverter and Soldering - Unbelievable USB Ustulater (August 12, 2013)" title="Ep 158: Hyperloop, Upverter and Soldering - Unbelievable USB Ustulater (August 12, 2013)">[158]</a></sup>

Between the mid-range and the top of the market the price gap is large, and the question worth asking is not which iron is best but how long the tool is expected to last — a ten-year iron, a five-year iron, or a two-week iron.<sup><a href="#ref-288" data-tip="Ep 288: Call In Show #3 (February 24, 2016)" title="Ep 288: Call In Show #3 (February 24, 2016)">[288]</a></sup> Irons priced around a thousand dollars are aimed at production work — instant heat and tightly held temperature — rather than at occasional users.<sup><a href="#ref-606" data-tip="Ep 606: Professional Scooter Charger (October 23, 2022)" title="Ep 606: Professional Scooter Charger (October 23, 2022)">[606]</a></sup> The value position sits in between, where clones of the premium induction systems deliver most of the behaviour for a fraction of the price.<sup><a href="#ref-606" data-tip="Ep 606: Professional Scooter Charger (October 23, 2022)" title="Ep 606: Professional Scooter Charger (October 23, 2022)">[606]</a></sup> Upgrading from genuinely poor equipment is noticeable in the work itself: bad joints and frustration trace back to the tool.<sup><a href="#ref-528" data-tip="Ep 528: New Year, New Gear (January 31, 2021)" title="Ep 528: New Year, New Gear (January 31, 2021)">[528]</a></sup> There is also a defensible argument for preferring a copy of a current-technology iron over a genuine example of the older technology at the same price, if the newer heating architecture is what is being bought.<sup><a href="#ref-384" data-tip="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)" title="Ep 384: A++++++ Will Buy Again (March 18, 2018)">[384]</a></sup>

## Power and modification

Almost all soldering stations use a fixed transformer and cannot be switched between mains voltages, which is the trap when buying equipment from another region.<sup><a href="#ref-419" data-tip="Ep 419: Feels over reals (December 9, 2018)" title="Ep 419: Feels over reals (December 9, 2018)">[419]</a></sup> The internals are simple enough to recombine: a genuine handpiece, an aftermarket controller board, and a cordless drill battery can be assembled into a working portable station in a day.<sup><a href="#ref-343" data-tip="Ep 343: Road trip to the deep space network (April 17, 2017)" title="Ep 343: Road trip to the deep space network (April 17, 2017)">[343]</a></sup>

## Historical development

The market has moved from analogue dials to digital controls with preset temperatures, a loss for anyone who wants to grab a knob and see the setting at a glance.<sup><a href="#ref-122" data-tip="Ep 122: Processors, CEOs & Soldering irons - Plentiful Perfunctory Programs (November 19, 2012)" title="Ep 122: Processors, CEOs & Soldering irons - Plentiful Perfunctory Programs (November 19, 2012)">[122]</a></sup> USB-connected irons that take firmware updates have appeared, a genuinely new category of thing to go wrong in a hand tool.<sup><a href="#ref-298" data-tip="Ep 298: Don't Turn It On, Don't Take It Apart (May 11, 2016)" title="Ep 298: Don't Turn It On, Don't Take It Apart (May 11, 2016)">[298]</a></sup>
