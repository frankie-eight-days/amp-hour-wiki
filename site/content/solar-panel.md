A **solar panel** (photovoltaic panel) is a device that converts incident sunlight directly into electrical energy, with output rated against a standard insolation of 1000 watts per square metre so that any panel's nameplate figure is directly comparable with any other's.<sup><a href="#ref-639" title="Ep 639: Daaaamn We're Duuuummmb (July 17, 2023)">[639]</a></sup> Panels are characteristically wired in series strings producing several hundred volts of DC, converted to grid-synchronised AC by an inverter, and their real-world energy yield is dominated less by nameplate rating than by siting, orientation, temperature, and shading.<sup><a href="#ref-532" title="Ep 532: Recalling Recalls (February 28, 2021)">[532]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-548" title="Ep 548: The Last Line of Defense (July 5, 2021)">[548]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-555" title="Ep 555: Timing is Everything (August 30, 2021)">[555]</a></sup> Because photovoltaic conversion has no wear-out mechanism in the way chemistry and mechanisms do, panels routinely remain serviceable for decades, with twenty-year output warranties common on domestic hardware.<sup><a href="#ref-322" title="Ep 322: World Trade Futurity (WTF) (November 9, 2016)">[322]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-150" title="Ep 150: Solar, FPGAs and Maxim Integrated - Solar Shopper Sickness (June 17, 2013)">[150]</a></sup>

## Ratings and performance

Every panel datasheet rates output at a nominal 1000 watts per square metre of incident sunlight, a convention that makes the industry standard easy to hold in one's head and makes ratings directly comparable across products.<sup><a href="#ref-639" title="Ep 639: Daaaamn We're Duuuummmb (July 17, 2023)">[639]</a></sup> The nameplate figure is a realistic peak rather than an optimistic one: a well-sited array reaches slightly over its datasheet rating on a very good day with high insolation.<sup><a href="#ref-205" title="Ep 205: Solar Factories and HVDC Lines - Pollent Power Pushing (June 30, 2014)">[205]</a></sup>

Panels run more efficiently when cold. Peak output does not come on the hottest day of the year; a clear cold winter day with slight haze can beat midsummer, because the haze scatters light onto the panel from more directions while the cold keeps the cells efficient.<sup><a href="#ref-206" title="Ep 206: An Interview with Martin Lorton - Variegated Video Vagility (July 7, 2014)">[206]</a></sup> Installations in hot climates exploit this by dripping water over the array to cool it, powering the pump from the array's own output and still coming out ahead.<sup><a href="#ref-249" title="Ep 249: Wearables Might Have Limited Fashion Options - Lachrymogenic Lane Language (May 12, 2015)">[249]</a></sup>

Because a panel reaches its rating for only a short part of the day, systems are deliberately designed around energy rather than peak power. Fitting an inverter smaller than the panel it serves — 290 watts of inverter on a 370 watt panel — costs very little output, because the panel only reaches its rating for perhaps half an hour a day.<sup><a href="#ref-548" title="Ep 548: The Last Line of Defense (July 5, 2021)">[548]</a></sup> For the same reason, installers guarantee an annual energy figure rather than a power figure; one first measured year came in at 5.26 megawatt hours against a 4.8 megawatt hour guarantee, the guarantee being conservative by roughly ten percent.<sup><a href="#ref-112" title="Ep 112: An Interview with Bob Simpson - Ardent Automotive Artisan (September 9, 2012)">[112]</a></sup>

## Electrical characteristics

### Series strings

A single panel produces roughly forty volts, so string inverter systems wire panels in series — twelve panels giving something like 460 volts open circuit — which is why a domestic roof carries several hundred volts of DC.<sup><a href="#ref-532" title="Ep 532: Recalling Recalls (February 28, 2021)">[532]</a></sup> Series wiring has a systems consequence: a single degraded panel is bypassed and can pull down the output of the whole string, so physical handling damage during installation, producing microcracks invisible from the ground, shows up as a system-level shortfall.<sup><a href="#ref-559" title="Ep 559: Occam's Engineering Razor (September 26, 2021)">[559]</a></sup>

High-voltage DC strings are hazardous in a way a battery of the same energy is not. Four ordinary panels in series reach about 160 volts, and shorting a string at that voltage sustains a DC arc: unlike a low-voltage short, the plasma keeps burning once struck and can be pulled apart while still conducting.<sup><a href="#ref-447" title="Ep 447: Voltnuts for Flashlights (June 16, 2019)">[447]</a></sup> Despite this, converting an existing string installation to per-panel microinverters costs more than the old panels are worth, so the high-voltage DC string stays even where the owner would prefer not to have it — the retrofit economics, not the safety argument, decide the matter.<sup><a href="#ref-589" title="Ep 589: Mute Button Discipline (May 15, 2022)">[589]</a></sup>

Unused generation requires no disposal mechanism: with no current drawn, panels simply sit at their open-circuit voltage and produce nothing, so curtailment costs nothing but the energy foregone.<sup><a href="#ref-702" title="Ep 702: Test Point Accupuncture (September 14, 2025)">[702]</a></sup>

### Inverters

A grid-tied inverter is two stages: the panel string feeds a boost or buck-boost converter that establishes a four-to-five-hundred-volt DC bus, and an H-bridge then chops that bus into a sine wave synchronised to the grid.<sup><a href="#ref-671" title="Ep 671: NDA Sideshow (June 19, 2024)">[671]</a></sup> Whether a load is running on local generation is settled by where it sits relative to the meter and by which way current flows: with a charger wired ahead of the metering point and no import registering, the energy demonstrably came from the panels rather than the grid.<sup><a href="#ref-580" title="Ep 580: Electrical Archeology (March 6, 2022)">[580]</a></sup>

Regions that installed solar earliest have the least controllable fleet: the great majority of those inverters have no connectivity at all, so the capacity cannot be curtailed remotely no matter what the grid needs.<sup><a href="#ref-702" title="Ep 702: Test Point Accupuncture (September 14, 2025)">[702]</a></sup> Utilities accordingly demand extensive telemetry from distributed generation, and their security objections are not unreasonable — simultaneous remote shutdown of an entire district's generation on a sunny day would be a genuine grid event, and the realistic attack surface is the inverter rather than the panel.<sup><a href="#ref-630" title="Ep 630: Renewable Energy Policy with Ari Gerstman (May 2, 2023)">[630]</a></sup>

### Maximum power point tracking

Maximum power point tracking (MPPT) is an algorithm rather than a component: measure the panel's voltage and current, step the presented input impedance, and keep stepping until the output passes over the peak of the power curve, then repeat as the sun moves, because the peak moves with it.<sup><a href="#ref-512" title="Ep 512: Design For Longevity (October 11, 2020)">[512]</a></sup> A charger listed as supporting MPPT may only mean an application note exists describing how to implement it externally with a microcontroller; dedicated parts that do it internally are a different class of device, and the distributor's parametric field does not distinguish them.<sup><a href="#ref-512" title="Ep 512: Design For Longevity (October 11, 2020)">[512]</a></sup>

## Siting and installation

Roof aspect dominates nameplate rating. A three-kilowatt array relocated to a poorly oriented face of a roof delivered about one kilowatt at best in winter, a third of what the same hardware produced before it was moved.<sup><a href="#ref-548" title="Ep 548: The Last Line of Defense (July 5, 2021)">[548]</a></sup> Laying a panel flat rather than angling it at the sun costs twenty to thirty percent before anything else, and a flat installation compounds that with losses in any covering glass and with the lack of airflow behind the panel, which raises its temperature and lowers efficiency again.<sup><a href="#ref-249" title="Ep 249: Wearables Might Have Limited Fashion Options - Lachrymogenic Lane Language (May 12, 2015)">[249]</a></sup>

Roof suitability can be assessed before anyone visits, using tools that model usable roof area together with tree shading from a three-dimensional model — which will sometimes rule a site out immediately.<sup><a href="#ref-555" title="Ep 555: Timing is Everything (August 30, 2021)">[555]</a></sup> Geometry matters as much as area: panels are rectangles, and a roof cut into many small triangular faces cannot take them efficiently.<sup><a href="#ref-555" title="Ep 555: Timing is Everything (August 30, 2021)">[555]</a></sup> A panel is roughly 1.8 metres by 1 metre, which is the figure to measure a roof against when working out how many will physically fit before asking what they will generate.<sup><a href="#ref-688" title="Ep 688: The Tandy Train (February 11, 2025)">[688]</a></sup> Scale matters too: a three-kilowatt system of twelve panels falls well short of covering a house, and at around 250 watts per panel it takes twenty or more before an array meaningfully offsets household consumption.<sup><a href="#ref-555" title="Ep 555: Timing is Everything (August 30, 2021)">[555]</a></sup>

Where an existing array must be relocated to a suboptimal aspect, the move can still be worth doing once the original system has paid for itself, because the marginal cost is a few hundred dollars of labour and the alternative is scrapping working hardware.<sup><a href="#ref-559" title="Ep 559: Occam's Engineering Razor (September 26, 2021)">[559]</a></sup>

Installation economics extend beyond the panels themselves. A domestic battery-and-solar installation arrives as half a dozen separate sheet-metal boxes that have to be conduited together, commissioned and tied into the switchboard, and the electrician's time frequently costs more than the hardware itself.<sup><a href="#ref-487" title="Ep 487: An Interview with Kerry Scharfglass (April 5, 2020)">[487]</a></sup> Cable routing also carries consequences that are easy to miss: a long DC run from a detached structure back to the inverter carries a real voltage drop, and it is not something the installer surveying cable routes will necessarily raise, because the conduit and the electrical consequence are usually different people's expertise.<sup><a href="#ref-692" title="Ep 692: Like a steam engine in your house (April 15, 2025)">[692]</a></sup>

## Diagnostics and failure modes

A cloudless day should produce a smooth generation curve, so dips in that curve are diagnostic; comparing the same day across two arrays on the same roof separates a real fault from weather, because genuine cloud shows up on both.<sup><a href="#ref-559" title="Ep 559: Occam's Engineering Razor (September 26, 2021)">[559]</a></sup>

Energy measurement interacts with generation. A whole-house monitor that works by differencing imported against exported power cannot resolve small loads while the array is generating, because the differential is large; measuring at night, with generation at zero, recovers an extra digit of resolution and makes circuit-by-circuit measurement possible.<sup><a href="#ref-604" title="Ep 604: Robo Fry Guy (October 9, 2022)">[604]</a></sup>

## Economics

A premium domestic installation has been observed to pay back in about five years, against panels warranted for twenty years of output and an inverter warranted for ten, which is what makes the economics work even without a generous feed-in tariff.<sup><a href="#ref-150" title="Ep 150: Solar, FPGAs and Maxim Integrated - Solar Shopper Sickness (June 17, 2013)">[150]</a></sup> The quality spread in both panels and inverters is wide enough to matter over a twenty-year life: one percent of a three-kilowatt system is thirty watts being turned into heat continuously, every hour the sun is up.<sup><a href="#ref-205" title="Ep 205: Solar Factories and HVDC Lines - Pollent Power Pushing (June 30, 2014)">[205]</a></sup> In one documented case, a 12-kilowatt array of twenty to twenty-eight panels plus battery storage and installation came to about thirty-two thousand dollars before incentives, against which a thirty percent federal credit and a utility programme payment covered roughly nineteen thousand.<sup><a href="#ref-692" title="Ep 692: Like a steam engine in your house (April 15, 2025)">[692]</a></sup>

At grid scale, managing demand in a constrained neighbourhood has proved cheaper than building a substation to serve it, and the comparison is worse than it first looks for the substation: it produces nothing once built, where distributed generation keeps producing energy for its whole life.<sup><a href="#ref-630" title="Ep 630: Renewable Energy Policy with Ari Gerstman (May 2, 2023)">[630]</a></sup> A modest battery is enough to bridge the evening demand peak: with generation stopping around midday for a west-facing array, storage can carry a house through to eight or nine at night before any grid import resumes, covering the whole dip in the daily demand curve.<sup><a href="#ref-702" title="Ep 702: Test Point Accupuncture (September 14, 2025)">[702]</a></sup>

In low-income markets, solar projects fail on financing rather than on hardware: microfinance interest rates are high because borrowers commit and then default, and recovering the equipment is expensive enough that the cost feeds back into the rates; retaining ownership and profit-sharing with a local operator sidesteps the credit problem entirely.<sup><a href="#ref-309" title="Ep 309: An Interview with Stefan Dzisiewski-Smith (July 27, 2016)">[309]</a></sup>

## Alternative and emerging applications

### Solar thermal comparison

Given finite roof area, photovoltaic panels beat solar hot water because electricity is fungible: it can heat water through a heat pump, charge a vehicle, run the house or go into storage, where a solar thermal collector only ever produces hot water.<sup><a href="#ref-674" title="Ep 674: Turtles as a Service (July 25, 2024)">[674]</a></sup>

### Water-sited arrays

Covering irrigation canals with panels pays twice: the structure anchors to solid ground either side of a channel only a few metres wide, the shade suppresses evaporation, and unlike open water there is no bird fouling. Floating arrays on lakes get the cooling benefit but bring the problem that floating structures move and collide.<sup><a href="#ref-580" title="Ep 580: Electrical Archeology (March 6, 2022)">[580]</a></sup>

### Road-integrated panels

Road-surface photovoltaics are far more expensive than rooftop for a fraction of the energy once the concrete, glass and structure are counted; mounting angled panels above a path instead, as a canopy, keeps the conventional geometry and gains weather protection underneath.<sup><a href="#ref-249" title="Ep 249: Wearables Might Have Limited Fashion Options - Lachrymogenic Lane Language (May 12, 2015)">[249]</a></sup> Putting an existing panel under glass in a novel location is packaging rather than innovation in solar technology, a distinction that matters when judging what a project actually claims to have advanced.<sup><a href="#ref-225" title="Ep 225: Worktrips and Workspaces - Junket Jactation Jiltedness (November 25, 2014)">[225]</a></sup>

### Vehicles

Eight hundred watts of vehicle-mounted panel puts very little energy into a car battery over a day — enough that continuous driving is impossible even under full sun in the Australian outback, and recharging means stopping for hours and clearing the panels.<sup><a href="#ref-627" title="Ep 627: Works on my machine (April 9, 2023)">[627]</a></sup> An integrated solar roof on a production vehicle realistically adds around five miles of range a day, meaningful for some owners and negligible for others, and a power pole or tree shading the parking spot removes even that.<sup><a href="#ref-662" title="Ep 662: The non-Stinky Car (March 20, 2024)">[662]</a></sup> The stronger argument for a solar roof on an electric vehicle is not daily range but never being completely stranded: a slow trickle turns being stuck permanently into being stuck for a day.<sup><a href="#ref-662" title="Ep 662: The non-Stinky Car (March 20, 2024)">[662]</a></sup>

### Indoor and low-power devices

Cells optimised for indoor light are a distinct product from rooftop panels, and they are what makes a genuinely battery-free indoor sensor possible.<sup><a href="#ref-376" title="Ep 376: An Interview with Richard Ginus (January 21, 2018)">[376]</a></sup> Low-power system design around small panels rewards scrutiny of actual requirements: in one case, hundreds of hours went into optimising a half-watt panel and battery chemistry to sustain readings every minute, when users turned out to be satisfied with four readings a day — a thousandth of the power budget — so that asking users first would have removed the entire engineering problem.<sup><a href="#ref-268" title="Ep 268: An Interview with Luke Iseman of yCombinator (September 22, 2015)">[268]</a></sup>

### Spacecraft

Spacecraft solar panels are built as cells assembled onto a printed circuit board backing, with deployment done by a spring that pushes the panel or antenna element out once released.<sup><a href="#ref-679" title="Ep 679: Satellite Design Engineering with Dan Esparon (October 11, 2024)">[679]</a></sup> Externally mounted spacecraft panels swing between about minus 100 and plus 100 degrees Celsius, but the cold extreme is benign because no current is being drawn from them there; the thermal cycling matters more than either absolute limit.<sup><a href="#ref-220" title="Ep 220: An Interview with Shaun Meehan - Doctiloquent Dove Deployer (October 13, 2014)">[220]</a></sup>

Deployment geometry is a reliability concern. A lander found intact on Mars years later had soft-landed successfully and deployed four of its five panels; the one that stayed shut was covering the antenna that would have phoned home, illustrating that stacking a communications dependency underneath a deployable is a single point of failure that no amount of redundancy elsewhere covers.<sup><a href="#ref-233" title="Ep 233: Glass and Gongkai GSM - Unzymotic Ursidae Upbuilding (January 20, 2015)">[233]</a></sup> The longevity of photovoltaics is equally well documented in orbit: a satellite lost in 1967 resumed transmitting 46 years later, when its tumble happened to bring the panels into enough light and the antenna into the right orientation.<sup><a href="#ref-322" title="Ep 322: World Trade Futurity (WTF) (November 9, 2016)">[322]</a></sup>

## Limits and misconceptions

The claim that a country's entire demand fits on a small area of panels is arithmetically defensible and practically empty, because it sets aside both storage and the transmission needed to move that power from where it is generated to where it is used.<sup><a href="#ref-580" title="Ep 580: Electrical Archeology (March 6, 2022)">[580]</a></sup> The recurring claim that the eighty percent a panel does not convert can be mirrored into a second panel, and again into a third, to exceed a hundred percent efficiency is a perpetual-motion argument in new packaging: the unconverted energy is not sitting there as reflected light waiting to be collected.<sup><a href="#ref-652" title="Ep 652: For a couple weeks there... (November 28, 2023)">[652]</a></sup>

An order-of-magnitude feel for energy per unit panel area and per unit battery mass lets an engineer reject an implausible product claim on sight, without doing the calculation — and the calculation is then worth doing to confirm the instinct rather than to form it.<sup><a href="#ref-233" title="Ep 233: Glass and Gongkai GSM - Unzymotic Ursidae Upbuilding (January 20, 2015)">[233]</a></sup>
